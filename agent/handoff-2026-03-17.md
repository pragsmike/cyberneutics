### Independent evaluation (04-evaluation-1.md)

Scored the deliberation at 10/15 (all five rubrics at 2/3). Five specific gaps identified:
1. Vic's effect size claim ("0.6-point shift detectable if variance is low") was unsupported math

5. "Near zero cost" was repeated without substantiation

### Remediation round (Round 4)

The committee addressed all five gaps. Most significant outcomes:
- Vic withdrew the effect size claim and replaced it with an honest admission: viability cannot be determined in advance
- The C2/C3 token confound was surfaced — the comparison that matters is C2 vs. B1-ext, not C2 vs. B1. This changed the difficulty criterion from B1 ≤ 1 to B1-ext ≤ 1
- Joe challenged the portfolio framing with a sharp counterfactual; Tammy conceded it's valid for Deliberation-Neutral only
- Frankie provided concrete cost estimates: 4-7 sessions / 15-25% overhead
- Maya hardened her conditions with a reassessment trigger

### Re-evaluation (06-evaluation-2.md)

Verdict: High trustworthiness as decision input. At bar, no further remediation required.

## Mistakes and lessons

1. **The review skill was invoked directly by the system rather than by me.** The `/review --situation ../situations/black-swan-phase-a` was triggered as a continuation of work from a prior conversation turn. This is fine mechanically but successors should know: the review skill reads from the deliberation directory and writes the evaluation file — it's a fully self-contained operation.

2. **The evaluation feedback loop takes a full conversation cycle.** Deliberation → evaluation → remediation → re-evaluation consumed a large context window. For future deliberations on complex topics, be aware that the remediation cycle roughly doubles the transcript length. The 02-deliberation.md file grew to ~53KB after the remediation round was appended.

3. **The most important insight came from the evaluation, not the deliberation.** The C2/C3 token confound — the single most consequential finding — was prompted by the evaluation's recommendation #4 ("critical unexamined assumption"). The committee didn't discover it on its own. This validates the evaluation feedback loop as more than quality assurance: it's a substantive contribution mechanism.

## Theoretical/conceptual notes

The most significant conceptual outcome of this session: **the evaluation feedback loop is a substantive contribution mechanism, not just quality assurance.** The C2/C3 token confound — which reshapes the entire study design — was prompted by the evaluation, not discovered by the committee. The evaluator asked "what if committee architectures just produce more text?" and the remediation round traced the implications (changes the primary comparison, changes the difficulty criterion, adds a reassessment trigger). This is the cybernetic feedback loop working as theorized: generator + evaluator in tension produces insights neither would reach alone.

The deliberation also produced a clean decomposition of the portfolio framing into genuine vs. ad hoc components. Joe's counterfactual test ("would you have proposed this if the data had been different?") is a general-purpose tool for detecting post-hoc rationalization. Worth noting for future deliberations.
