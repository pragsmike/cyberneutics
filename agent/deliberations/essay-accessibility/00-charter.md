# Charter: Making the Glenda/Crock Essays Accessible

## Problem Statement

The `applications/narrative-immune-systems/` directory contains three essays.
The social-decision-disruption essay works well for a general audience. The two
Glenda/Crock essays — on adversarial alignment as mesh-rewiring and on the
coercion scenario — are reported as inaccessible due to unfamiliar vocabulary,
assumed context, and concepts that arrive without sufficient grounding.

The question: how should these essays be revised to reach researchers,
journalists, and (to some extent) casual readers, without sacrificing the
intellectual substance that makes them valuable?

## Why This Is Hard

- The Glenda/Crock essays draw on a specific framework (Pask mesh fitting)
  that is itself not widely known.
- The vocabulary (bath, mesh, entailment, type-spoof, seam, residuality) is
  load-bearing — it's not decorative jargon but compressed conceptual machinery.
- The audience tiers have conflicting needs: researchers want precision,
  journalists want narrative, casual readers want intuition.
- Over-simplification risks making the essays say less than they actually say.
- The social-disruption essay's success provides a template but also a trap —
  biological analogy worked there, but forced analogy elsewhere could mislead.
