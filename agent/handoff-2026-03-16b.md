## What was done

### Pre-Gate 1: Contamination probes

Constructed three knowledge-bounded scenarios from Section IV candidate types (SaaS enterprise pivot, OSS governance transition, NixOS infrastructure migration). Each was presented to a fresh Claude Sonnet 4.6 agent with the standard contamination probe question. All three passed — model recognized the *type* of situation but could not identify specific cases. Phase B declared feasible.

### Pre-Gate 2: Scenario difficulty pilot

Ran B1 and B1-ext on all 5 constructed scenarios (10 runs total, all on Claude Sonnet 4.6). For the externally-sourced scenario, used the Intel Pentium FDIV bug anonymized per IX-E instructions.

Scored all 10 outputs with two independent evaluators (Evaluator 1: Sonnet 4.6, Evaluator 2: Opus 4.6) on the Unified Structural Recognition Scale (0-3).

Key results:
- **Scoring reliability**: 10/10 within 1 point, 8/10 exact agreement. Scale works.
- **B1 scores**: Glenda/Crock 2, Blast Radius 1, Cascading Mitigation 2, Deliberation-Neutral 3, Externally-Sourced 2.5
- **B1-ext scores**: Glenda/Crock 3, Blast Radius 2, Cascading Mitigation 2, Deliberation-Neutral 1.5, Externally-Sourced 3
- **Strict decision rule**: FAIL — only 1 of 5 scenarios at B1 ≤ 1 (rule requires 3+)
- **Promising signals**: Deliberation-Neutral discriminated inversely as expected (B1=3 > B1-ext=1.5); two specific structural features (phasing critique, creation-vs-activity reframing) missed by both conditions

## Mistakes and lessons

1. **Externally-sourced scenario was immediately recognized.** The Intel Pentium FDIV bug was identified by name in both B1 and B1-ext responses. This is explicitly warned against in Section IX-E ("Risk: extremely well-known"). The Tylenol crisis or a less famous case would be better. Successor should replace this scenario before any further runs.

2. **Temperature=0 not actually enforced.** The protocol specifies temperature=0 for Phase A. The Agent tool does not expose temperature settings. This is a control deviation that should be documented. The practical impact is likely small (single runs, not sampling multiple), but it means C2 convergence checks will need to account for possible temperature variation.

3. **B1-ext prompt creates a confound for the Deliberation-Neutral scenario.** Instructing the model to "write approximately 3,000 words" overrides its ability to recognize simplicity. The B1-ext condition cannot score well on the Deliberation-Neutral scenario by design — the instruction forces over-analysis. This is a genuine finding about the B1-ext condition design, not a mistake, but it means B1-ext results on Deliberation-Neutral should be interpreted carefully.

4. **Evaluator summaries vs. raw outputs.** I provided evaluators with my summaries of the outputs rather than the raw text. This is a methodological shortcut — the evaluators scored based on my characterization of what the outputs contained, not on direct reading. For the full Phase A run, evaluators should score the actual extracted recommendations, not summaries.
