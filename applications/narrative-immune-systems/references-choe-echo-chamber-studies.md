## 1. Simchon et al. (2025/2026) — Inoculation Meta-Analysis (SDT)

**Full citation:** Simchon, A., Zipori, T., Teitelbaum, L., Lewandowsky, S., & Van der Linden, S. (2026). A signal detection theory meta-analysis of psychological inoculation against misinformation. *Current Opinion in Psychology*, 67, 102194.

**DOI:** (Published October 2025, journal volume dated 2026)

**URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352250X25002076

**Method:** Meta-analysis of 33 inoculation experiments (N = 37,075) using a hierarchical Bayesian framework and signal detection theory.

**Key findings:**
- Inoculation (prebunking) improves discernment between reliable and unreliable content.
- Critically, inoculation does *not* induce response bias — it does not make people more cynical or distrustful of legitimate information.
- The SDT framing disentangles discrimination ability (d') from response bias (criterion shift), which previous meta-analyses had not done.

**Cyberneutics relevance:** Direct SDT framing makes this immediately connectable to the metacognition research program (meta-d'/d' as noise figure). The finding that inoculation improves discrimination without inducing bias is the thymus property — the immune system learns to distinguish self from non-self without becoming autoimmune. This is the empirical evidence that prebunking-as-negative-selection works at the individual cognitive level. The open question is whether it scales to network-level behavior in echo chamber architectures.

## 2. Van der Linden et al. (2026) — Instagram Prebunking Field Study

**Full citation:** Noble, N. S., et al. (2026). Prebunking misinformation techniques in social media feeds: Results from an Instagram field study. *Harvard Kennedy School Misinformation Review*.

**URL:** https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/prebunking-misinformation-techniques-in-social-media-feeds-results-from-an-instagram-field-study/

**Method:** Field experiment. A 19-second prebunking video about emotionally manipulative content was shown as a Story Feed ad to N = 375,597 Instagram users in the UK. Discernment measured via Instagram's quiz functionality (N = 806 respondents).

**Key findings:**
- Treatment group users were 21 percentage points better at identifying manipulation in a news headline.
- Effects persisted for five months.
- Treated users were more likely to click on a link to learn more (engagement effect).

**Cyberneutics relevance:** The 5-month durability is significant — it suggests inoculation is not a transient priming effect but something closer to genuine pattern-recognition training. The "emotionally manipulative content" focus connects directly to the emotion-first vs. inference-first discrimination we developed in conversation: this study trained people to detect emotion-first construction, and the training stuck. The field-experiment methodology (real Instagram feeds, not lab conditions) strengthens ecological validity. Scale (375K users) demonstrates feasibility of prebunking at the organ-to-bloodstream transition Choe describes.

## 3. Kim et al. (2026) — Digital Voter Suppression on Facebook (PNAS)

**Full citation:** Kim, Y. M., Dahlke, R., et al. (2026). Targeted digital voter suppression efforts likely decrease voter turnout. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 123(5), e2519944123.

**DOI:** 10.1073/pnas.2519944123

**URL:** https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2519944123

**Data deposited:** ICPSR, https://doi.org/10.3886/E242583V1

**Method:** Recruited >10,000 US participants (representative sample) who installed an ad-tracking app (EScope) capturing all ads viewed for six weeks before the 2016 election. Matched individual-level ad exposure to verified state voting records. First study to link individual Facebook ad exposure to validated turnout data.

**Key findings:**
- Individuals exposed to vote-suppressing Facebook ads were 1.9% less likely to vote.
- Extrapolated nationally: approximately 4.7 million suppressed votes in 2016 (election decided by ~77,000 votes across three states in the Electoral College).
- Nonwhite users in minority-majority counties of battleground states received voter suppression ads at up to 10x the rate of white users in non-battleground states.
- 17% of identified suppression campaigns were connected to Russian IRA operations.
- IRA used Facebook's own ad targeting presets (interest categories including "Martin Luther King, Jr.," "African American Civil Rights Movement," "African American history") to target Black voters with content urging election boycott.
- Compared to white voters in white-majority non-battleground counties, nonwhites in nonwhite-majority battleground counties were 14.2% less likely to vote.

**Cyberneutics relevance:** This is the empirical smoking gun for social decision disruption as signal hijack. The targeting mechanism is precisely the type-spoofing attack described in the narrative immune systems framework: content that mimics legitimate political speech (civic concern, community empowerment) but carries a demobilization payload. The use of culturally resonant targeting categories (civil rights history) to deliver suppression messages is the adversarial emotion-first construction at industrial scale — the emotional payload (distrust, futility) is delivered through identity-affirming framing, and the "reasoning" (don't vote) is the pre-installed conclusion. The racial targeting data quantifies the precision weapon aspect of the Glenda/Crock threat model applied to democratic infrastructure. Directly relevant to computational social science's DARPA SocialSim work on information cascades.

## 4. Bond et al. (2012) — 61-Million-Person Facebook Mobilization Experiment

**Full citation:** Bond, R. M., Fariss, C. J., Jones, J. J., Kramer, A. D. I., Marlow, C., Settle, J. E., & Fowler, J. H. (2012). A 61-million-person experiment in social influence and political mobilization. *Nature*, 489(7415), 295–298.

**DOI:** 10.1038/nature11421

**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11421

**Method:** Randomized controlled trial during the 2010 US congressional elections. 61 million Facebook users were randomly assigned to one of three conditions: (a) a "social message" showing friends' faces who had voted, (b) an "informational message" without friends' faces, or (c) no message. Validated against actual voting records for ~6.3 million matched users.

**Key findings:**
- The social message increased turnout by approximately 340,000 votes (60,000 direct + 280,000 via social contagion).
- The informational message alone (without social proof from friends) had *zero* measurable effect on turnout — statistically indistinguishable from the control group.
- Close friends exerted approximately 4x more influence on mobilization than the message itself.
- Effects propagated to friends-of-friends (two degrees of separation).
- The 340,000 additional votes represent ~0.14% of the 236 million voting-age population, but potentially account for a substantial portion of the 0.60% turnout growth between 2006 and 2010.

**Cyberneutics relevance:** This is the foundational empirical result for Choe's dark-share strategy and for the organ/bloodstream trust boundary formalization. The finding that *informational content alone has zero effect* while *social-proof-carrying content from close ties has measurable effect* is a precise empirical demonstration of the trust credential hypothesis: content crossing from organ to bloodstream survives or dies based on the relationship metadata it carries, not the informational payload. The close-friend effect (4x) quantifies the trust gradient at the boundary. The social contagion finding (280K indirect vs. 60K direct) demonstrates cascade dynamics in dense tie clusters — directly relevant to computational social science's cascade modeling work. The echo chamber structure that pundits dismiss as epistemically unhealthy is empirically the structure that maximizes the mobilization mechanism.

## 5. CIRCLE (2025) — Youth Political Information Sources

**Full citation:** CIRCLE (Center for Information & Research on Civic Learning and Engagement). (2025). Youth Rely on Digital Platforms, Need Media Literacy to Access Political Information. Tufts University.

**URL:** https://circle.tufts.edu/latest-research/youth-rely-digital-platforms-need-media-literacy-access-political-information

**Method:** Survey of 18–34-year-olds analyzing political information sources, broken down by voter/nonvoter status, vote choice, race/ethnicity, gender, and socioeconomic status.

**Key findings:**
- Young nonvoters rely on Facebook for political information at significantly higher rates than voters: 33% vs. 21%.
- Young voters are more likely to get information from news websites (38% vs. 21%), X/Twitter (18% vs. 11%), podcasts (13% vs. 6%), and Reddit (13% vs. 8%).
- News websites/apps remain the largest single source across all youth (35%), followed by YouTube (29%), TikTok (25%), Instagram (24%), Facebook (23%).
- Young Trump voters relied more on YouTube, X/Twitter, and Facebook; young Harris voters relied more on Instagram, Reddit, and news websites.
- Video content dominates youth information diet across platforms.

**Cyberneutics relevance:** The nonvoter-Facebook concentration finding is the strategic basis for Choe's platform choice. The demobilized population that Kim et al. showed is most vulnerable to voter suppression is disproportionately *on Facebook*. This makes Facebook simultaneously the attack surface (where suppression content is delivered) and the defense surface (where inoculation and mobilization content can reach the target population). The platform-specific information diet differences by voter status suggest that different trust boundaries operate on different platforms — a bloodstream ecology question relevant to the organ/bloodstream formalization.

## 6. Civic Power Report (2025) — Independent Political Organizations

**Full citation:** Pro-Democracy Campaign, Power Lab, & Democracy and Power in Action Fund (DPI). (2025). Civic Power: The Role and Impact of Independent Political Organizations in Expanding the Electorate and Building Governing Influence. Movement Voter Project.

**URL:** https://movement.vote/blog/2025-12-15-civic-power-report-organizing-is-how-we-win-elections-and-pass-bold-policies/

**Method:** Analysis of voter contact data from Independent Political Organizations (IPOs) funded by MVP during the 2024 election cycle, compared to party/candidate contact programs. Voter contact matched to turnout records.

**Key findings:**
- IPOs reached 1.3 million unique voters who were not contacted by any other program — not the presidential campaigns, not the parties.
- These voters were younger, more racially diverse, and less frequent voters than those contacted by mainstream party operations.
- IPOs prioritized person-to-person contact (phone, doors) over cold text outreach.
- IPOs achieved significantly higher contact rates: 9.3% vs. 5.4% for non-IPO groups.
- The 1.3 million uniquely-contacted voters had a 64.6% turnout rate, yielding an estimated 820,000 voters who turned out having been contacted only by IPOs.
- 61% of IPOs' contacts were to voters with vote propensity scores below 80 (less frequent voters), compared to non-IPO groups which focused on higher-propensity voters.

**Cyberneutics relevance:** Empirical evidence for the relational organizing model that Choe's dark-share strategy operationalizes at the individual level. The finding that person-to-person contact reaches populations invisible to traditional campaign infrastructure connects to Bond et al.'s close-friend effect: the trust credential is the personal relationship, not the informational content. The "year-round organizing" emphasis (vs. election-cycle-only mobilization) is the calibration register argument applied to political organizing: sustained relationship-building creates the trust infrastructure that enables mobilization when needed, rather than trying to manufacture trust in the mobilization window. The 820K turnout figure from uniquely-contacted voters demonstrates the scale of the "echo chamber as base of operations" strategy.
