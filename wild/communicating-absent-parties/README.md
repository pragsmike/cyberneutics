# Communication with Absent Parties

## Situation

Standard communication theory — Shannon, Wiener, Pask — presupposes co-present, mutually responsive interlocutors. The sender transmits, the receiver responds, errors get corrected, and shared understanding emerges through feedback. But a large and undertheorized class of real communication lacks this structure entirely. Nuclear waste warnings must persist for 10,000+ years across civilizational discontinuities. Dead scripts (Linear B, Voynich, Indus Valley) present texts whose senders cannot be interrogated. SETI confronts signals from entities that may share no cognitive architecture with the receiver. Information cascades strip the originator's identity and intent as content propagates through networks. Biological heredity transmits genetic and epigenetic messages from ancestral organisms that are structurally absent at the moment of reception. In every case, the feedback channel is severed: the sender cannot correct misinterpretation, and the receiver cannot request clarification.

The Perplexity deep research report (`absent_party_report.pdf`) surveys eight domains where this problem surfaces and finds a unifying thread: absent-party communication is not a marginal edge case but a central, recurring structural challenge. The theoretical frameworks differ across domains — Shannon in nuclear semiotics, Pask in conversation theory, Peirce in biosemiotics, Gadamer in hermeneutics — but the underlying information-theoretic challenge is isomorphic. The report identifies five major theoretical gaps where these domains fail to connect and proposes Brier's cybersemiotics as a candidate integrative framework.

The report's key finding: heredity itself — the transmission of biological information from parent to offspring, where the sender is structurally absent at the moment of gene expression — is the ur-case of absent-sender communication. Every other domain addresses a specialized variant of this same fundamental problem.

## Connection to Cyberneutics

The absent-party problem maps onto multiple active threads in this repository. The connections are not incidental — absent-party communication is where the cyberneutics architecture hits its hardest test cases.

**CT's productive boundary.** Pask's Conversation Theory defines absent-party communication as its hard boundary: no conversation, no mutual teachback, no CT observables. But the report (Section 3.6) argues this boundary is productive, not merely limiting. It defines the design space for feedforward-only communication: the sender must embed enough structure in the artifact to let a future interpreter reconstruct a viable entailment mesh without ever achieving mutual agreement. The artifact becomes a "frozen conversation" — incomplete, but sufficient to seed a conversational process in the interpreter.

**Organ vs. bloodstream.** The organ/bloodstream distinction — a controlled channel with inspectable transformations versus an ambient medium of unprovenanced material — maps directly onto absent-party communication. The organ assumes a controlled channel with feedback — the sender knows the receiver's codebook, can correct errors, can verify reception. The bloodstream is unprovenanced text requiring external judgment about consistency with a trusted corpus. Absent-party communication is the extreme bloodstream case: no provenance, no feedback, no sender to interrogate. Every nuclear warning, every undeciphered script, every genetic message is bloodstream-mode communication. The bloodstream model's machinery — rubric-scored consistency checking against a trusted mesh — is exactly the operation decipherers and interpreters perform.

**Calibration register at the limit.** The metacognition thread's calibration register (meta-d' tracking) requires ground-truth feedback to measure how well the system distinguishes signal from noise. But absent-party cases have no ground truth: there is no sender to confirm whether the interpretation is correct. The register must shift from tracking correspondence (predicted vs. actual) to tracking internal consistency — whether the interpretation is self-coherent and coherent with the broader evidential mesh. This is arguably what all hermeneutics does. The Voynich manuscript is the limit case: the evidence may be consistent with multiple incompatible decipherments (or with no decipherment at all, if it is a hoax). The calibration register needs a zero-feedback mode.

**Fan/funnel and reconstructed perspective.** The committee architecture can accommodate an absent party by constructing a "reconstructed perspective" character — a committee member representing the absent sender's likely worldview, equipped with high uncertainty priors across all its assessments. This is what good historians and decipherers do implicitly: they construct a model of the absent author's intentions, knowledge, and constraints, then test whether the text is consistent with that model. The fan operation (divergent scenario generation) applied to an absent sender's possible frameworks is the formal analog of Gadamer's "fusion of horizons" with a frozen horizon.

**Furry logic and measurement-based type assignment.** The furry logic thread (`wild/diary/2026-03-13-furry-logic.md`) argues that type membership is measurement, not declaration — a distribution over type-space produced by a specific rubric applied to a specific text. This is exactly what decipherers do: "this glyph probably maps to this phoneme" is a rubric-scored type assignment with calibrated uncertainty. Kober's triplets, Ventris's grid, and Rao's conditional entropy calculations are all measurement-based type assignments applied to artifacts from absent senders. The furry logic framework provides the formal vocabulary for what decipherment has always been doing.

**Promises as everyday absent-party communication.** A promise encodes intent into a structure (contract, commitment, social norm) in the hope that the future interpretive community will decode it faithfully. The promisor attempts to freeze one side of a conversation — to make organ what is actually bloodstream. The promise is the everyday, mundane case of absent-party communication: the sender may be present but has no control over how the commitment is interpreted downstream.

## What the Report Covers

**Section 1: The Problem Space — A Taxonomy of Absence.** Five modalities of absence: temporal (deep time), spatial (interstellar distance), epistemic (non-overlapping frameworks), structural (cascade/propagation), and biological (cross-generational signaling). Real-world cases typically combine multiple modalities. A mapping table connects modalities to research domains and paradigm cases.

**Section 2: Nuclear Semiotics and Long-Term Warning Design.** The WIPP Human Interference Task Force (1980–81), Sebeok's "atomic priesthood" relay system, the Sandia four-level message hierarchy, hostile architecture proposals (Landscape of Thorns), Flusser's pre-literate vs. post-literate distinction, Finland's Onkalo repository (no surface marking), Voigt's concentric linguistic rings, the "ray cat" biological channel proposal, and the OECD RK&M Initiative's nine-approach toolbox.

**Section 3: Pask's Conversation Theory — Boundary Conditions.** CT fundamentals (entailment meshes, agreement over understanding), Pask's explicit statement that absent-party communication produces no CT observables, Scott's "frozen entailment structures," Pangaro's four-level communication taxonomy, Laurillard's absent-teacher problem in pedagogy, Green's axiomatics (amity, faith, resonance), and Glanville's second-order reframing that turns the absent party's "presence" into a construction by the interpreter.

**Section 4: Decipherment as Inverse Hermeneutics.** Linear B as the paradigmatic success (Kober's distributional analysis as conditional entropy computation), the Voynich manuscript as the paradigmatic underdetermined case, Rao's computational approaches to the Indus Valley script, MIT neural decipherment, von Foerster's eigenforms applied to decipherment convergence, and the key gap: no published work applies Pask's entailment mesh formalism to decipherment despite it being the natural framework.

**Section 5: Anamaria Berea — A Unified Research Program.** Berea's academic profile (GMU, SETI Institute, NASA Frontier Development Lab), the Folger/Literary Genome NLP project, ASPIRE ONE lunar archive (multi-modal encoding for receiver-agnostic deep-time communication), technosignature research, DARPA SocialSim (agent-based modeling of information cascades), the "Message in a Bottle" paper, and the 2025 Space Policy paper on generalized planetary communication frameworks.

**Section 6: SETI as Communication Theory.** Vakoch's exosemiotics, Munévar's evolutionary epistemology critique (mathematics may not be universal), Freudenthal's Lincos constructed language, Dubinina and Tsybulco's hermeneutic SETI (Gadamer applied to alien signals), Doyle's information-theoretic analysis of animal communication as a terrestrial analog, Herzing's Wild Dolphin Project, and the Zoo Hypothesis/Dark Forest theory as theories of strategic communicative absence.

**Section 7: Information Cascades Without Source Feedback.** DARPA SocialSim (Berea as scientific lead), the BHW 1992 foundational cascade model, Daley-Kendall rumor dynamics, Vosoughi/Roy/Aral's finding that false news spreads faster than truth, Wardle and Derakhshan's information disorder taxonomy, Luhmann's autopoietic communication (the sender is always structurally external to the communication system), and LLM-generated misinformation as a new category of absent-sender communication where there is no intentional sender at all.

**Section 8: Adjacent Hermeneutic Traditions.** Gadamer's frozen horizon, Derrida's iterability as constitutive absence (a sign is defined by its capacity to function without its sender), Ricoeur's distanciation (the text becomes autonomous from authorial intent), Eco's open work and Model Reader, Hayles's bridging of cybernetics and literary theory (meaning is always embodied), Luhmann's three-selection model, Kittler's post-hermeneutic media theory (the medium determines what can be communicated), and Stiegler's tertiary retention (artifacts as cross-generational memory).

**Section 9: Biosemiotics Boundary Cases.** Barbieri's code biology and organic codes, Hoffmeyer's semiosphere and semiotic freedom, Pattee's epistemic cut (DNA as simultaneously physical molecule and symbolic structure), Jablonka and Lamb's four-dimensional inheritance, niche construction as feedforward communication in the physical world, Deacon's absential features (properties defined by what is absent), and the Tartu school's Umwelt theory. Key insight: the biological world has been solving absent-sender communication for 3.8 billion years, and its solutions (redundant coding, modular architecture, error correction, layered channels) converge with engineering proposals for nuclear warnings and SETI messaging.

**Section 10: Brier's Cybersemiotics — The Integrative Framework.** Brier's five-level model integrating Shannon, autopoiesis, Peircean firstness, Luhmann's social systems, and Paskian conversation theory. The framework maps how absence operates at each level but does not explicitly address the absent-party problem — applying cybersemiotics to this problem remains an open research program. Brier's critique: if Shannon-Wiener were sufficient, the nuclear warning problem would have been solved decades ago. The absent interpretant in Peirce's triadic semiotics explains both why absent-party communication is hard (the sender's intended interpretant chain is truncated) and why it is not impossible (semiosis is open-ended).

**Section 11: Cross-Cutting Synthesis.** A comparative table mapping all eight domains against modality of absence, feedback channel status, framework, and key unsolved problem. Five theoretical gaps (detailed below). Four convergent design principles across all domains: redundancy, multi-modal encoding (iconic + indexical + symbolic), institutional relay, and designed interpretive latitude. An information-theoretic isomorphism: all absent-party systems must encode survivably, provide redundancy, include metacommunicative signals, and accommodate unknown receiver codebooks. A formal characterization of absent-party communication as a tuple (S, R, M, C, T, E) and the Gödelian question of whether pure self-referential encoding has a fundamental limit.

**Section 12: Annotated Bibliography.** Comprehensive bibliography organized by section, covering nuclear semiotics, Pask/CT, decipherment, Berea's work, SETI, information cascades, hermeneutics, biosemiotics, cybersemiotics, and cross-cutting references.

### Five Theoretical Gaps (Section 11.2)

These are the report's identified contribution points — places where connections between domains have not been made despite being structurally available:

1. **No Paskian treatment of absent-party decipherment.** Entailment meshes are the natural formalism for what decipherers do (reconstruct the derivational structure of an absent sender), but no published work makes this connection explicit.
2. **No systematic cybernetic framing of nuclear semiotics.** The HITF literature draws on Shannon and Peirce but never employs the conceptual vocabulary of second-order cybernetics (observing systems, structural coupling, conversation theory).
3. **Incomplete bridge between biosemiotics and information cascade theory.** Both deal with structurally absent sources; the theoretical vocabularies (Peircean sign theory vs. game theory/epidemiology) are disjoint. Luhmann's autopoiesis could bridge them but the connection is undeveloped.
4. **Berea's work needs explicit grounding in second-order cybernetics.** Her research program is implicitly cybernetic (complex adaptive systems, agent-based models, information theory) but does not engage explicitly with Wiener, Ashby, von Foerster, Pask, or Maturana.
5. **The Derrida-cybernetics connection remains undertheorized.** Derrida's iterability thesis and cybernetic feedforward-only communication address the same structural problem from radically different intellectual traditions. No published work maps the isomorphism systematically.

## What the Report Misses or Undertheorizes

**Deliberately engineered absence as adversarial strategy.** The report treats structural absence as something that *happens* through propagation dynamics (Section 7) — the sender's identity is stripped away as information cascades through networks. It does not treat absence as something that is *weaponized*. Disinformation campaigns where the originator deliberately absents themselves — so the target community must process the message without a sender to interrogate — are the adversarial case. This is the Glenda/Crock problem from the narrative immune systems work (`applications/narrative-immune-systems/`): the attacker's absence is strategic, not incidental. The attacker engineers the bloodstream condition — unprovenanced text that must be evaluated purely on internal consistency — while knowing that the consistency-checking infrastructure may itself be compromised. The report's Section 7.7 notes LLM-generated misinformation as an absent-sender form but does not develop the adversarial framing. This connects directly to the narrative immune systems research program: if absence can be engineered, then detecting engineered absence (vs. incidental absence) becomes a core challenge.

**The promise as absent-party communication.** Not mentioned in the report. A promise attempts to freeze one side of a conversation — to encode the promisor's intent into a durable structure (contract, commitment, norm, institution) that will persist beyond the moment of utterance. The promisor is present at encoding but structurally absent at the moment of interpretation: they cannot control how the promise is parsed, invoked, or enforced downstream. Every legal contract, constitutional provision, and software license is an absent-party communication designed to function as organ in what is actually bloodstream. The failure modes of promises — creative reinterpretation, bad-faith parsing, changed circumstances that render the original intent moot — are isomorphic to the failure modes of nuclear warnings and undeciphered scripts.

**Machine-machine conversation.** Pask's experiments involved machines mediating human learning (SAKI, CASTE, THOUGHTSTICKER, the Colloquy of Mobiles), but whether he ever set up pure machine-machine conversation with no human in the loop is an open question. Pangaro has discussed Pask's chains of human-machine interaction, and the Colloquy of Mobiles involved machine-machine signaling, but the pure M-individual to M-individual case — where the entailment meshes on both sides are machine-generated and the "agreement over understanding" criterion is evaluated without human judgment — may be unexplored territory. See `wild/potential-to-sense/` for context. This matters because LLM-to-LLM pipelines (the cyberneutics committee running on separate instances) are exactly this case. When two LLMs converse without a human gate, is what emerges "conversation" in Pask's sense, or statistical attractor convergence without genuine agreement? The `wild/potential-to-sense/` essay argues the latter — LLM-only discourse explores a statistical landscape but lacks the pragmatic grounding that human participation provides.

**Categorical treatment of frozen entailment meshes.** Section 11.6 gestures at functors between sender and receiver categories (C_S -> C_R) but does not develop the formalism. The cyberneutics palgebra work on decorated cospans is directly relevant: a frozen entailment mesh is a decorated cospan with one interface unconnected — the sender's side of the boundary is populated with structure, but the receiver's side is an open port waiting for connection. Decipherment is the construction of a functor from the graph structure of C_S to C_R when C_R is unknown. The categorical framing would formalize what the report leaves as a suggestion.

The absent-party problem is a strong framing for outreach: it is the unifying thread of her career, stated in terms that connect her empirical domains to each other rather than treating them as separate projects. Cyberneutics offers formal architecture for the problem she has been working on from five directions:

## Open Questions

- Is there a Godelian limit on self-referential encoding? Can any message fully specify its own interpretation, or does every absent-party communication system necessarily leave an interpretive remainder? (Section 11.5)
- Can the calibration register be adapted for zero-feedback environments? What does meta-d' mean when there is no ground truth — only internal consistency?
- What is the categorical treatment of a frozen entailment mesh? Is a decorated cospan with one unconnected interface the right formalization? What does the pushout along the empty interface produce?
- Did Pask ever run pure machine-machine conversations? The Colloquy of Mobiles involved machine-machine signaling, but did any system achieve the "agreement over understanding" criterion without human participants?
- How does deliberately engineered absence differ formally from incidental absence? Is there a detectable structural signature of adversarial bloodstream — text that was designed to lack provenance vs. text that lost provenance through propagation?
- What would a "calibrated promise" institution look like? If promises are absent-party communications, what institutional design embeds enough metacommunicative structure to make the promise robust to reinterpretation?
- What is the relationship between Hoffmeyer's semiotic freedom and the receiver's capacity to interpret absent-party messages? Does higher semiotic freedom (greater interpretive latitude) help or hurt when the sender's codebook is unknown?
- Can the convergent design principles (redundancy, multi-modal encoding, institutional relay, interpretive latitude) be derived from a single information-theoretic framework, or are they genuinely independent strategies?
