## Executive Summary

What does it mean to communicate when one party is absent, unreachable, or non-responsive? This question — seemingly peripheral to mainstream communication theory, which presupposes co-present, mutually responsive interlocutors — turns out to be a central, cross-cutting problem addressed (often implicitly) across at least eight distinct scholarly traditions. This report synthesizes deep research findings across those eight domains and identifies the **absent-party problem** as a unifying theoretical thread connecting nuclear semiotics, cybernetic conversation theory, script decipherment, SETI, information cascade dynamics, continental hermeneutics, biosemiotics, and the emerging integrative work of researchers such as Anamaria Berea and Søren Brier.

The central problematic can be stated precisely: in any communicative act, the standard cybernetic model ([Wiener, 1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybernetics:_Or_Control_and_Communication_in_the_Animal_and_the_Machine)) presupposes a feedback loop — the receiver's response modulates the sender's subsequent output, enabling error correction, mutual calibration, and convergence on shared meaning. When the feedback channel is severed — by deep time (nuclear waste warnings designed to persist 10,000 years), by interstellar distance (SETI), by death (hermeneutic interpretation of historical texts), by radical epistemic incommensurability (undeciphered scripts), by structural decoupling (information cascades), or by biological discontinuity (cross-generational genetic signaling) — the communicative act does not simply degrade. It *changes in kind*. The sender must anticipate the receiver's entire cognitive and semiotic repertoire without confirmation. The receiver must reconstruct the sender's intentional structure from material residue alone. The message becomes an artifact whose meaning is constituted by the interpretive act, not merely transmitted through it.

[Gordon Pask's Conversation Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversation_theory) (CT) is the cybernetic framework that most explicitly theorizes this boundary: if there is no conversation — no mutual teachback, no agreement over an understanding — then there are, strictly speaking, no CT observables. Yet this boundary is productive, not merely limiting. It defines the space in which **feedforward-only** communication must operate, and it is precisely the space occupied by nuclear warning design, interstellar messaging, decipherment, and biological heredity. Across all eight domains surveyed here, we find a recurring structural pattern: the absence of the interlocutor forces the communication system to rely on redundancy ([Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Mathematical_Theory_of_Communication)), multi-modal encoding (Peirce/Jakobson), institutional relay mechanisms ([Sebeok](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/6705990)), iterability ([Derrida](https://doi.org/10.2307/1343073)), or code-based organic conventions ([Barbieri](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12304-014-9236-4)). The theoretical frameworks differ, but the underlying information-theoretic challenge is isomorphic.

This report maps these isomorphisms, identifies five major theoretical gaps where the connections remain undertheorized, and proposes [Brier's cybersemiotics](https://utorontopress.com/9780802092519/cybersemiotics/) as a candidate integrative framework. A key finding cutting across all domains: heredity itself — the transmission of biological information from parent to offspring, where the "sender" (the parental generation) is structurally absent at the moment of "reception" (gene expression in the offspring's developmental context) — is the **ur-case** of absent-sender communication. Every other domain in this report addresses a specialized variant of the same fundamental problem.

## 1. The Problem Space — A Taxonomy of Absence

The absent-party communication problem manifests in at least five distinguishable modalities of absence. Each modality imposes different constraints on the communication system and demands different compensatory strategies. The taxonomy below is analytic rather than empirical: real-world cases often combine multiple modalities simultaneously.

### 1.1 Temporal Absence (Deep Time)

The sender and receiver are separated by spans of time that exceed the lifespan of any known semiotic convention. Languages transform beyond recognition in 1,000–2,000 years; political institutions rarely survive 500 years; material substrates (stone, metal, ceramic) degrade on timescales of 1,000–100,000 years. The paradigm case is nuclear waste repository warning design, where the regulatory period is [10,000 years (WIPP) to 100,000 years (Onkalo)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onkalo_spent_nuclear_fuel_repository). Biological heredity — where genetic information encoded by ancestral organisms is "read" by descendant organisms millions of years later — represents the extreme case of temporal absence: **3.8 billion years** of messages from absent senders, all still being read.

### 1.2 Spatial Absence (Interstellar Distance)

The sender and receiver are separated by distances imposing lightspeed-limited delays measured in years, decades, or centuries. The paradigm case is SETI: a signal from a star system 100 light-years away means a 200-year minimum round-trip for any exchange. At interstellar scales, the feedback loop is not merely slow — it is effectively severed, because neither party can assume the other still exists when a response would arrive. [Douglas Vakoch's work on active METI](https://meti.org/) (Messaging Extraterrestrial Intelligence) and [Freudenthal's Lincos language project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincos_(artificial_language)) both confront this constraint directly. The lightspeed limit thus transforms a quantitative delay into a qualitative change in the nature of the communicative act.

### 1.3 Epistemic Absence (Non-Overlapping Frameworks)

The sender and receiver occupy cognitive or semiotic frameworks with no guaranteed overlap. This is the decipherment problem: the receiver possesses a text produced by a sender whose language, conceptual categories, and referential world may share nothing with the receiver's own. [Linear B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_B), the [Voynich manuscript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript), and the [Indus Valley script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_script) each exemplify different degrees of epistemic absence. [Gonzalo Munévar's evolutionary epistemology critique](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-27437-4_8) of SETI argues that even mathematics — often assumed to be a universal language — may not be shared across independently evolved intelligences. Epistemic absence can obtain even between contemporaneous parties: the dolphin-human communication barrier studied by [Denise Herzing](https://www.wilddolphinproject.org/) is a spatial-present, temporal-present, but epistemically-distant case.

### 1.4 Structural Absence (Cascade / Propagation)

The sender's identity, intentions, and context are progressively stripped away as information propagates through a network. In **information cascades** ([Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer, and Welch, 1992](https://doi.org/10.1086/261849)), each agent observes the behavior of predecessors and rationally ignores private signals, producing herding effects decoupled from the original information source. In [Niklas Luhmann's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Systems_(book)) autopoietic social systems theory, the sender is "structurally in the environment" of the communication system — communication selects information, utterance, and understanding independently of any individual sender's intention. In LLM-generated misinformation, the "sender" may be an algorithmic process with no intentional state at all — absence rendered not by distance or death but by the statistical architecture of the system itself.

### 1.5 Biological Absence (Cross-Generational Signaling)

The sender (parental organism or ancestral lineage) is absent at the moment of reception (gene expression, epigenetic activation, niche-constructed environmental modification). Heredity, in the biosemiotic framing of [Jesper Hoffmeyer](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-007-4917-0_11) and [Marcello Barbieri](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12304-014-9236-4), is a communication system in which the "message" (the genome, the epigenome, the constructed niche) was composed by an entity that no longer exists and cannot be consulted. [Eva Jablonka and Marion Lamb's](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262525848/evolution-in-four-dimensions/) four-dimensional model of inheritance (genetic, epigenetic, behavioral, symbolic) shows that biological absence is not a single phenomenon but a family of related transmission mechanisms, each with different fidelity, bandwidth, and error-correction properties.

### 1.6 The Taxonomy Table

| Modality of Absence | Defining Constraint | Research Domains | Paradigm Case |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Temporal (Deep Time)** | Sender/receiver separated by 10³–10⁵ years | Nuclear Semiotics, Biosemiotics | WIPP 10,000-year marker system |
| **Spatial (Interstellar)** | Lightspeed delay severs feedback loop | SETI, Berea's planetary communication | SETI signal from 100+ light-years |
| **Epistemic (Framework Gap)** | No shared code, language, or conceptual schema | Decipherment, SETI, Hermeneutics | Undeciphered scripts; alien signal interpretation |
| **Structural (Cascade)** | Source identity stripped by propagation dynamics | Information Cascades, Luhmann's systems theory | Viral misinformation; LLM-generated content |
| **Biological (Cross-Generational)** | Sender absent at moment of gene expression | Biosemiotics, Code Biology | Genetic code; epigenetic inheritance |

## 2. Nuclear Semiotics and Long-Term Warning Design

### 2.1 The WIPP Human Interference Task Force

The field now called **nuclear semiotics** was formally inaugurated in 1980–1981 when the U.S. Department of Energy and the Bechtel Group commissioned a **Human Interference Task Force** (HITF) to investigate problems connected with the post-closure, final marking of a filled nuclear waste repository. The HITF's mandate was specific: devise a method of warning future generations not to mine or drill at the **Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP)** near Carlsbad, New Mexico — and to ensure this warning would remain effective for at least **10,000 years**, the arbitrary regulatory period established by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency under 40 CFR Part 191.

The key problem articulated by the HITF was not engineering or political but fundamentally communicative: the sender and receiver would be separated not merely by space but by the entirety of possible civilizational change. No shared code could be assumed. No feedback channel would exist. The message had to function regardless of whether human society continued, regressed, advanced, or transformed beyond recognition. The definitive treatment of the resulting marker system proposals appeared in [Trauth, Hora, and Guzowski's Sandia report SAND92-1382](https://gwern.net/doc/technology/1993-trauth.pdf) (1993), which established the canonical four-level hierarchy of messages, graduated from immediate visceral impact to detailed technical documentation.

The **four message levels** are:
- **Level 1 (Rudimentary)** — "Something man-made is here," communicated through architecture, earthworks, and sound.
- **Level 2 (Cautionary)** — "Something man-made is here and it is dangerous," through physical markers and pictographs.
- **Level 3 (Basic)** — What, why, when, where, who, and how, through pictographic and multilingual text.
- **Level 4 (Complex)** — Detailed technical records, maps, diagrams, and star charts.

The paradigmatic Level 1–3 message includes the now-iconic text, reproduced from the Sandia report:

> "This place is a message... and part of a system of messages... pay attention to it! This place is not a place of honor... no highly esteemed deed is commemorated here... nothing valued is here. What is here was dangerous and repulsive to us. This message is a warning about danger."

The HITF four-level system is, in Shannon-theoretic terms, an error-correcting code operating across a non-stationary channel: the redundant, multi-modal structure ensures that if any individual layer degrades, the others can bootstrap a receiver into understanding the remaining layers.

### 2.2 Sebeok's Atomic Priesthood and the Semiotic Framework

Thomas A. Sebeok (1920–2001), one of the most distinguished American semioticians, was engaged by Bechtel/ONWI in 1981 as a specialist in signs and communication. His resulting report, [*Communication Measures to Bridge Ten Millennia*](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/6705990) (BMI/ONWI-532, 1984), is explicitly structured around the Shannon-Weaver communication model as filtered through Roman Jakobson's semiotic extension. Sebeok draws directly on Norbert Wiener's distinction between **feedback** and **feedforward**: the entire nuclear warning design problem is framed as a pure feedforward problem — the sender must anticipate, without confirmation, the cognitive and semiotic capacities of an unknown receiver.

Sebeok argued that effective long-term messages must mix all three Peircean sign types:
- **Iconic** (images of hurt bodies, threat postures)
- **Indexical** (the site itself as an index of buried danger)
- **Symbolic** (text and numerical data)

No single sign type is sufficient, because each type is differentially vulnerable to cultural change, physical degradation, and context collapse. The redundant use of all three modes is the semiotic analog of Shannon's redundancy theorem.

His most provocative proposal was the **"atomic priesthood"** — a self-perpetuating commission of experts maintaining the truth about repository sites through artificially created ritual and legend, modeled on the Catholic Church's institutional longevity. The relay system divided the 10,000-year period into segments of approximately 250 years, with living custodians at each relay point translating and re-encoding the warning. This proposal was ultimately not adopted, but it was the first serious application of institutional cybernetics to the deep-time communication problem. [Sebastian Musch's analysis in *Zygon: Journal of Religion and Science* (2016)](https://www.zygonjournal.org/article/14327/galley/29031/download/) and [Marcel Danesi's *Warning Signs: The Semiotics of Danger* (Bloomsbury, 2022)](https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/warning-signs-9781350178304/) credit Sebeok as inaugurating the field and extend his framework to danger semiotics broadly.

### 2.3 Hostile Architecture: Landscape of Thorns and Kindred Designs

The physical marker proposals generated by the WIPP expert panels represent the most visually striking dimension of nuclear semiotics. Designed primarily by **Michael Brill (BOSTI)** with drawings by **Safdar Abidi**, the hostile architecture concepts operated on a semiotic premise: certain forms, shapes, and configurations of landscape communicate danger **without depending on learned codes**. The major proposals include:

- **Landscape of Thorns** — a dense, random forest of irregularly sized concrete thorns up to 50 feet high
- **Spike Field** — a regular grid of basalt spikes emerging from the earth
- **Menacing Earthworks** — lightning-bolt-shaped mounds designed to appear violated and dangerous
- **The Black Hole** — an enormous basalt slab rendering land uninhabitable through heat absorption
- **Forbidding Blocks** — hundreds of house-sized black concrete blocks in an irregular grid with narrow, hot, ominous "streets"

The [DOE's current WIPP marker plan](https://wipp.energy.gov/pdfs/site_markers.pdf) excludes the hostile architecture proposals in favor of berms, granite monuments, buried radar-reflective objects, and multilingual archives. The hostile architecture proposals were rejected partly on grounds of the **"treasure-hunter" paradox**: anything sufficiently monumental might attract rather than repel curious future investigators. This paradox illustrates a fundamental tension in feedforward communication design — the message must simultaneously compel attention and discourage approach.

### 2.4 Jakobson's Communication Model and Its Application

Roman Jakobson's "Linguistics and Poetics" (1960) — which appeared in Sebeok's edited volume *Style in Language* (MIT Press, 1960) — extended Shannon's model by identifying the functions served by each component of the communication act: **referential** (context), **poetic** (message), **emotive** (addresser), **conative** (addressee), **phatic** (contact), and **metalingual** (code). Sebeok directly invokes Jakobson's taxonomy in framing the nuclear warning problem.

The key insight: nuclear warning design must achieve the **conative function** (directing future behavior: "do not dig here") in the absence of the **phatic** and **metalingual** functions. The phatic function ("is the channel working?") and the metalingual function ("are we using the same code?") both require bidirectional communication. Remove these, and the integrity of the entire communicative act becomes uncertain. This is why pictographic and architectural messages — which operate at the pre-linguistic level — are more promising than text: they bypass the need for shared code, operating at the level of the icon and index rather than the symbol.

### 2.5 Flusser, Post-Literacy, and Technical Images

**Vilém Flusser's** media theory — particularly his five-stage model of human abstraction (concrete experience → traditional images → linear text → technical images → computation), developed in [*Into the Universe of Technical Images*](https://doi.org/10.5749/minnesota/9780816670208.001.0001) (1985/2011) — addresses precisely the conditions that nuclear warning design must confront. Flusser's key insight for this problem: the HITF literature generally assumes that future receivers will be **pre-literate** (unable to read modern languages because civilization has collapsed), when the actual danger may be that they will be **post-literate** — able to compute and process images but not trained to decode linear alphabetic text. In either case, the traditional text-based warning fails.

Flusser's distinction between **dialogical** and **discursive** communication maps directly onto the feedforward/feedback distinction: nuclear warning is the extreme case of discursive communication, where the sender cannot observe whether the message is received, and the message must itself constitute the dialogue it cannot have. [Daniel Irrgang's 2023 analysis in *Theory, Culture and Society*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/02632764231168576) provides a systematic account of Flusser's concept of the technical image and its relevance to durable communication.

### 2.6 Galison, Onkalo, and Deep-Time Institutions

**Peter Galison's** contribution to nuclear semiotics is primarily one of **political epistemology**. His essay ["Removing Knowledge" (2004)](https://galison.scholars.harvard.edu/resource/pdf-14) demonstrates that the classified universe of U.S. government documents may be larger than the publicly accessible universe of knowledge. The paradox Galison illuminates: the same institution (the DOE) that spent decades suppressing public knowledge of nuclear activities is now tasked with ensuring that knowledge survives 10,000 years. This institutional self-contradiction complicates every design decision. His 2015 documentary [*Containment*](http://www.containmentmovie.com) (with Robb Moss) is the most artistically ambitious direct engagement with the WIPP marker problem.

**Finland's Onkalo repository** represents a radical departure: [Posiva Oy has proposed to leave no surface markers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onkalo_spent_nuclear_fuel_repository) when the facility is sealed — a wager that engineered barriers are sufficient and that surface marking, by attracting archaeological attention, might do more harm than good. **Michael Madsen's** documentary [*Into Eternity* (2010)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1194612/) addresses this directly, speaking in second person to an unspecified future viewer and thereby *enacting* the communication problem it documents. The film's formal strategy is itself a response to the absent-party problem: by addressing a hypothetical "you," Madsen constitutes the receiver performatively.

**Vincent Ialenti's** [*Deep Time Reckoning* (MIT Press, 2020)](https://newbooksnetwork.com/vincent-ialenti-deep-time-reckoning-how-future-thinking-can-help-earth-now-mit-press-2020) provides crucial ethnographic grounding, showing how Finnish nuclear waste experts actually model futures 100,000 years out. Their "safety analysis" is a form of feedforward communication design: constructing a model of an unknown future receiver and reasoning about what messages will survive and be understood.

### 2.7 The Ray Cat Solution and Biological Channels

**Françoise Bastide and Paolo Fabbri**, responding to a *Zeitschrift für Semiotik* poll (1982–1984), proposed the **"ray cat" solution**: genetically engineer cats to change color in the presence of ionizing radiation, coupled with deliberately cultivated folklore explaining that "if your cat changes color, flee." This proposal uses a **biological channel** rather than a material or institutional one — cats reproduce themselves across generations without human maintenance, and the information is embedded in the cultural substrate (folklore) rather than in objects that can be destroyed.

**Stanisław Lem**, responding to the same poll, proposed analogous biological channels: "Atomic Flowers" — genetically engineered plants carrying encoded information about waste sites in their DNA. These proposals anticipate the biosemiotic insight (developed in Section 9) that biological systems have evolved the most robust solutions to cross-generational communication, precisely because the constraints they face are structurally identical to the nuclear warning problem.

### 2.8 Voigt's Concentric Linguistic Rings and Temporal Redundancy

**Vilmos Voigt** (Eötvös-Loránd University) proposed a graduated translation system: install warning signs in all major global languages in concentric rings around the storage site. As languages change and become unreadable, new signs with translations of the older ones would be added in the next ring out — while the older signs remain. The outer rings would thus provide the key to reading the inner ones, replicating the function of the **Rosetta Stone** in a planned, deliberate architecture of progressive translation.

This is an elegant **temporal redundancy solution**: rather than designing a single sign system that will survive 10,000 years, design a system that generates its own interpretive key through layered obsolescence. The concept has deep affinities with Shannon's error-correcting codes and with the biological strategy of redundant gene copies (paralogs) that preserve function even as individual copies accumulate mutations. The [OECD Nuclear Energy Agency's RK&M Initiative (2011–2018)](https://www.oecd-nea.org/upload/docs/application/pdf/2019-12/7421-rkm-final.pdf) developed this principle systematically, compiling a toolbox of 35 mechanisms for record, knowledge, and memory preservation grouped into nine approaches, with the core principle that no single mechanism is sufficient.

### 2.9 Recent Developments (2020–2026)

Several significant developments have extended the nuclear semiotics field since 2020. [Keating and Storm's 2023 paper in *Progress in Environmental Geography*](https://doi.org/10.1177/27539687231174242) examines nuclear memory through archival, aesthetic, and speculative lenses, connecting the WIPP problem to broader questions of environmental long-termism. [Isabel Scott's analysis in *The Macksey Journal* (2023)](https://mackseyjournal.scholasticahq.com/article/115433-pandora-s-atomic-box-religious-myth-as-a-guide-for-semiotic-communication/attachment/220800.pdf) applies religious myth theory to the atomic priesthood proposal, arguing that myth is a more robust carrier of behavioral injunctions than technical documentation. A 2025 [*Baltic Worlds* roundtable](https://balticworlds.com/the-time-temporalities-of-nuclear-waste/) on "The Time and Temporalities of Nuclear Waste" synthesizes recent interdisciplinary work across Scandinavia, where the question is most acute due to Finland's Onkalo and Sweden's planned deep geological repository at Forsmark.

## 3. Pask's Conversation Theory — Boundary Conditions

### 3.1 CT Fundamentals and Architecture

Gordon Pask (1928–1996) developed **Conversation Theory** (CT) over roughly two decades, crystallizing it in three major works: [*The Cybernetics of Human Learning and Performance*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversation_theory) (Hutchinson, 1975), *Conversation, Cognition and Learning: A Cybernetic Theory and Methodology* (Elsevier, 1975), and *Conversation Theory: Applications in Education and Epistemology* (Elsevier, 1976). Pask's research firm System Research Ltd. served as his "epistemological laboratory," and his principal collaborators included Bernard Scott, Dionysius Kallikourdis, and Robin McKinnon-Wood.

CT's foundational claim is that **knowledge is constituted through conversation** — specifically, through a process Pask calls "agreement over an understanding." Two participants (which may be individuals, cognitive sub-systems, or formal agents) engage in **mutual teachback**: A explains topic T to B, then B demonstrates understanding by teaching T back to A (or by deriving consequences that A recognizes as valid). This mutual demonstration of understanding — not mere signal exchange — is what constitutes a conversation in Pask's sense. The distinction is critical: Shannon's model counts any faithful signal transmission as communication, while Pask's model requires that the receiver demonstrate understanding to the sender's satisfaction.

The formal backbone of CT is the **entailment mesh**: a directed graph of topic nodes connected by entailment relations, where each node represents a procedure for producing or recognizing a concept, and each edge represents a derivation or dependency. Two conversants are said to "agree over an understanding" of topic T when their respective entailment structures for T are **isomorphic** — not identical, but functionally equivalent in the sense that each can predict the other's derivations.

### 3.2 Pask on the Limits: No Conversation, No Observables

Pask himself articulated the boundary condition most directly in a passage widely cited by his interpreters:

> "If there were no conversation, there would be no CT observables."

This is not merely a methodological limitation — it is an **ontological claim**. For Pask, knowledge, concepts, and understanding are not pre-existing entities that conversation reveals; they are *constituted* by conversation. Without the mutual process of teachback and agreement, there is nothing for CT to describe.

**Bernard Scott**, Pask's closest collaborator and the principal continuator of CT, has extended this boundary analysis in multiple works available through [The Pask Archive at pangaro.com](https://www.pangaro.com/Pask-Archive/scott.html). Scott distinguishes between:
- **Proto-conversation**: partially formed, asymmetric exchanges that approach but do not fully achieve mutual teachback
- **Null conversation**: the complete absence of feedback

The absent-party case falls into the latter category — but Scott recognizes that artifacts produced by a conversant can function as "frozen" entailment structures that a future interpreter can attempt to reconstruct. The book, the recording, the nuclear warning marker — all are frozen conversations, incomplete but potentially seed-bearing.

### 3.3 Pangaro's Limit Taxonomy

**Paul Pangaro**, one of Pask's most influential interpreters and the principal architect of CT's application to interaction design, has developed a taxonomy of communication that explicitly positions Pask's conversational model against Shannon's transmission model. Available at [pangaro.com/published/cybercon-pangaro.html](https://www.pangaro.com/published/cybercon-pangaro.html), Pangaro's taxonomy distinguishes four levels:

1. **Data transfer** — Shannon's model: the receiver merely captures bits
2. **Information exchange** — the receiver acquires new states
3. **Conversation** — mutual construction of shared understanding through teachback
4. **Collaboration** — joint action emerging from conversational agreement

The absent-party case, in Pangaro's framing, is a case where only levels (1) or (2) are available — the message can be transmitted and received, but the conversational closure (level 3) that would constitute genuine understanding cannot occur in real time. This is not a failure of communication; it is a structural condition that demands a different theory. Pangaro has also noted the asymmetric information structure of absent-party communication: the sender must encode more than the receiver needs, precisely because the sender cannot know what the receiver needs.

### 3.4 Laurillard's Absent-Teacher Problem

**Diana Laurillard's Conversational Framework** — the most widely adopted pedagogical application of Pask's ideas, developed in [*Teaching as a Design Science* (Routledge, 2012)](https://www.routledge.com/Teaching-as-a-Design-Science-Building-Pedagogical-Patterns-for-Learning-and-Technology/Laurillard/p/book/9780415803878) — faces the absent-party problem directly in the context of mediated learning. When a learner interacts with a textbook, a recorded lecture, or a software tutorial, the "teacher" is absent. Laurillard's framework requires adaptation and feedback cycles between teacher and student — cycles that recorded media cannot provide.

Laurillard's solution is to design media that **simulate conversational closure**: practice environments with automated feedback, peer discussion prompts, and structured reflection tasks. The design challenge is isomorphic to nuclear semiotics: how do you build a pedagogical artifact that functions in the teacher's absence? The answer in both domains is the same: embed the missing feedback in the artifact's structure, anticipating the range of possible receiver states and encoding responses to each. This is what Laurillard calls "adaptive teaching" and what the HITF calls "multi-level encoding" — the sender's cognition is transferred into the artifact itself.

### 3.5 Green's Axiomatics: Amity, Faith, and Resonance

**Nick Green**, building on Pask's later **Interaction of Actors Theory** (IA), has proposed a set of axioms for interaction that explicitly include conditions for non-conversational communication. Green's three foundational axioms for successful actor interaction are:
- **Amity**: willingness to engage with the other party
- **Faith**: trust that the other is acting in good faith
- **Resonance**: structural compatibility that allows mutual calibration

In the absent-party case, amity and faith must be **designed into the artifact itself** — the nuclear warning must embody a stance of good-faith communication toward an unknown future receiver. This is not merely a sentimental observation: it is a design constraint. An artifact that does not project amity (openness to the receiver's framework) and faith (trust in the receiver's interpretive capacity) cannot function as a communication. The hostile architecture proposals, paradoxically, violate the amity condition: they are designed to repel rather than engage, which may undermine their communicative function.

### 3.6 Glanville's Second-Order Perspective: Communication Without Interlocutor

**Ranulph Glanville**, Pask's most philosophically oriented student, extended CT into second-order cybernetics by arguing that the observer is always part of the system observed. Glanville's work on "communication without interlocutor" — developed in papers available through [CEPA Archive (cepa.info/2467)](https://cepa.info/2467) — addresses the case where one party in a conversation is absent or replaced by an artifact.

Glanville's key insight: the absent party's "presence" is constituted by the **interpreter's act of interpretation**. The conversation does not halt when one party disappears — it becomes a *second-order conversation* between the interpreter and their own model of the absent other. When a scholar reads Wittgenstein, a nuclear engineer reads the WIPP marker system, or a SETI researcher analyzes a candidate signal, they are not passively receiving a message but actively constituting a model of an absent interlocutor. This reframing turns the absent-party problem from a deficit (missing feedback) into a **constitutive condition** (the interpreter's creativity is required to complete the circuit).

From Glanville's perspective, the absent-party problem is not a pathological limit case of communication — it is the paradigmatic case that reveals what communication always is: the construction of an other with whom one converses, whether or not that other is physically present.

### 3.7 Entailment Meshes as Artifacts: Reconstruction from One-Way Communication

Pask recognized that entailment meshes — the formal structures representing a conversant's conceptual organization — can exist as *artifacts* independent of the person who constructed them. A book, a diagram, a software system, a genetic code: all are entailment mesh artifacts. The question, in the absent-party case, is whether a receiver can reconstruct a viable entailment mesh from the artifact alone, without mutual teachback.

This question is the CT version of the decipherment problem. The decipherer of Linear B (Section 4) is attempting to reconstruct Alice Kober's and Michael Ventris's entailment meshes — the conceptual structures they used to organize sign relations — from the pattern of their published notations and correspondence. The SETI researcher is attempting to reconstruct an alien civilization's entailment mesh from a radio signal. The biosemiotician studying epigenetic inheritance is attempting to reconstruct the "logic" embedded in a methylation pattern by an ancestral organism.

**Key theoretical gap**: No published work has explicitly applied Pask's entailment mesh formalism to decipherment, SETI, or biosemiotics. This is a significant theoretical lacuna that represents one of the five major gaps identified in the cross-cutting synthesis (Section 11).

## 4. Decipherment as Inverse Hermeneutics

### 4.1 The Decipherment Problem as Communication Theory

Decipherment of an unknown writing system is, at its core, a problem of **reconstructing the intentional structure of an absent sender from material residue alone**. This makes it simultaneously:
- A communication-theoretic problem: a channel with an unknown encoder
- An information-theoretic problem: what statistical properties of a signal indicate linguistic versus non-linguistic structure?
- A hermeneutic problem: how does the interpreter's own conceptual structure interact with an alien semiotic system?

The cybernetic tradition — especially second-order cybernetics (von Foerster), conversation theory (Pask), and the ecosystemic epistemology of Bateson — offers conceptual resources that have been only partially applied to decipherment. The connection that has never been made explicit, and which this synthesis draws: successful decipherment is the construction of a functor from the material residue of the absent sender's encoding system to the receiver's own conceptual framework (the category-theoretic framing is developed further in Section 11).

### 4.2 Linear B: Kober's Distributional Analysis as Conditional Entropy

The decipherment of **Linear B** — achieved by Michael Ventris in 1952, with crucial contributions from **Alice Kober** and **Emmett Bennett** — represents the paradigmatic success case. [Linear B was discovered by Arthur Evans at Knossos beginning in 1878](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_B), excavated systematically from 1900 onward; Evans withheld most tablets from publication until after his death (1941), delaying rigorous statistical analysis for decades.

**Kober's distributional analysis** (performed without computers, using hand-sorted index cards from 1943 to her death in 1950) identified systematic patterns of sign variation that indicated inflectional morphology. In information-theoretic terms, Kober was computing **conditional entropy**: given a particular sign sequence, what are the probabilities of the signs that follow? Her "triplets" — groups of three related sign sequences sharing a common root but with different endings — revealed the grammatical structure of the language without knowing what any sign meant.

The key cybernetic observation: Kober was **reconstructing the absent sender's entailment mesh from statistical regularities alone**. She did not need to know the meaning of any individual sign; she needed only to detect the structural relationships between signs. This is precisely the procedure that Gregory Bateson characterized as detecting a "difference that makes a difference" — the fundamental unit of information in his *Steps to an Ecology of Mind* ([Bateson, 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steps_to_an_Ecology_of_Mind)).

**Ventris's grid** (the two-dimensional array of sign values organized by syllabic structure) is an entailment mesh artifact: a formal structure that represents the combinatorial logic of the absent sender's encoding system. Once the grid was correct, the meaning of individual signs could be inferred by structural position — the grid *is* the absent sender's entailment mesh, reconstructed without conversation.

### 4.3 The Voynich Manuscript and Statistical Anomaly

The **Voynich manuscript** ([Beinecke MS 408](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript)) represents the paradigmatic failure case — or, more precisely, the paradigmatic case of **underdetermined decipherment**. [Montemurro and Zanette's 2013 analysis in *PLoS ONE*](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0066344) applied information-theoretic methods to demonstrate that the Voynich text exhibits long-range word-frequency correlations consistent with natural language. The text follows Zipf's law, has non-trivial entropy profiles, and shows semantic-level clustering that rules out random generation. Yet no proposed decipherment has achieved scholarly consensus.

The Voynich case raises the possibility of **irreducible underdetermination**: the evidence may be consistent with multiple incompatible decipherments, or with no decipherment at all if the text is a hoax. This is the worst-case scenario for absent-party communication theory: the receiver cannot determine whether the artifact encodes a genuine message or is noise that mimics structure. Von Foerster's constructivist epistemology reframes this: the receiver *always* constructs a model of the sender, but in the Voynich case, the evidence fails to converge on a unique model. The absent sender's entailment mesh cannot be reconstructed from the available evidence — the channel capacity is simply too low relative to the complexity of the encoding.

### 4.4 Computational Approaches: Rao and MIT Neural Decipherment

**Rajesh Rao et al.'s** work on the [Indus Valley script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_script) applies information-theoretic analysis to determine whether sign sequences exhibit linguistic structure. [Rao et al. (Science, 2009)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1170391) computed conditional entropy for Indus sign sequences and found values between those of linguistic and non-linguistic systems, suggesting the Indus signs encode language but with constraints imposed by the medium (most inscriptions are very short, averaging around 5 signs). This work provoked a significant methodological debate when Farmer, Sproat, and Witzel argued (2004) that the entropy profiles are consistent with non-linguistic symbol systems — a dispute that remains unresolved.

**MIT's computational decipherment group** (Barzilay, Luo, Knight) has developed neural approaches that treat decipherment as **machine translation with a lost source language**. [Luo et al. (TACL, 2019)](https://doi.org/10.1162/tacl_a_00169) successfully applied neural decipherment to Ugaritic (a known test case) and Linear B before attempting transfer to undeciphered scripts. The neural approach instantiates a key insight: if a partially deciphered or related language is available (Ugaritic → Hebrew; Linear B → Greek), the absent sender's encoding system can be reconstructed by finding the transformation that maps the related language into the target script. This is a formal implementation of the hermeneutic circle: the receiver uses what they know to bootstrap into what they do not know.

### 4.5 The Cybernetic Frame: Eigenforms and Entailment Meshes

The connection to second-order cybernetics is deep but largely implicit in the decipherment literature. **Heinz von Foerster's** concept of the **eigenform** — a stable self-referential structure that emerges from recursive operations — maps directly onto the decipherer's process: by iteratively applying hypotheses about sign values and checking for internal consistency, the decipherer converges on a stable interpretation that is both self-consistent and consistent with the evidence. The deciphered text is an eigenform of the interpretive process.

The parallel to Pask is explicit: Pask's "agreement over an understanding" is the social analog of von Foerster's eigenform. In both cases, stability emerges from recursive mutual confirmation. In the absent-party case, the confirmation must come from internal consistency rather than from the other party — the decoder asks not "do you agree?" but "does this interpretation predict the next element correctly?"

### 4.6 Undeciphered Scripts as Limit Cases

Several historical scripts remain undeciphered, each representing a different configuration of the absent-sender problem:

- **Linear A** (Minoan, ~1800–1450 BCE): the writing system is partially understood (it shares some signs with the deciphered Linear B) but the underlying language is unknown — a case of known medium, unknown message
- **Indus Valley script** (~2600–1900 BCE): extremely short average inscription length (~5 signs) makes statistical analysis difficult; whether the signs encode language at all remains contested
- **Proto-Elamite** (~3100–2900 BCE): approximately 1,600 known tablets with around 1,000 distinct signs — too many for a simple alphabet, suggesting a logographic or logo-syllabic system
- **Phaistos Disk** (~1700 BCE): a single artifact with 242 tokens of 45 distinct sign types, printed with stamps — suggesting standardized production, but providing too little text for statistical decipherment
- **Rongorongo** (Easter Island, ~18th century): reverse boustrophedon writing on wooden tablets, most of which were destroyed in the 19th century

Each undeciphered script instantiates a different degree of **epistemic absence**. The degrees can be measured along multiple dimensions — temporal, linguistic, cultural, and inferential — illustrating that the absent-sender problem is not binary but parametric.

### 4.7 Bateson, Von Foerster, and the Cybernetics of Decipherment

**Gregory Bateson's** definition of information as "a difference that makes a difference" (*Steps to an Ecology of Mind*, 1972) provides the minimal cybernetic criterion for detecting communication in an unknown system: if a variation in the signal correlates with a variation in context or behavior, information is present. This is precisely what Kober's distributional analysis detects — systematic differences between sign sequences that correlate with grammatical function.

**Heinz von Foerster's** constructivist epistemology extends this insight: the decipherer does not "discover" the message hidden in the text; rather, the decipherer *constructs a model of the absent sender* that is consistent with the evidence. The deciphered text is an eigenform of the decipherer's recursive modeling process — a stable self-referential structure that emerges from iterative hypothesis-testing. This constructivist reframing has important implications: decipherment is not a passive "reading" of a message but an active construction that depends on the decipherer's own cognitive and cultural resources. Different decipherers, with different pre-understandings (in Gadamer's sense), may construct different models of the absent sender.

### 5.2 The Folger / Literary Genome Project

During her tenure at ATA, LLC (2016–2018), Berea led a **"literary genome"** NLP project in collaboration with the **Folger Shakespeare Library** — the world's largest collection dedicated to Shakespeare. The project applied natural language processing, text mining, and semantic analysis to Renaissance and Early Modern texts. As stated in her [GMU CV PDF](https://science.gmu.edu/media/anamaria-bereacvlongpdf):

> *"Project leader on data science for the humanities, the **literary genome**, in collaboration with **Folger Shakespeare Library**."*

The literary genome concept — computing a kind of genomic fingerprint of literary texts through statistical and NLP analysis — parallels established digital humanities methods such as those described in [the 2012 DH paper "Computing and Visualizing the 19th-Century Literary Genome"](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/dh2012/conference/programme/abstracts/computing-and-visualizing-the-19th-century-literary-genome.1.html) (stylometry + topic modeling + network analysis to measure influence across corpora). Berea's application at Folger extended this methodology to Renaissance/Early Modern texts.

In the absent-party framing, this work treats the text as a signal whose latent structure (vocabulary distributions, semantic networks, stylometric patterns) can be **reverse-engineered** to reconstruct something about authorial intent — the same epistemological challenge as decoding an undeciphered manuscript or interpreting an extraterrestrial transmission. The author of a Shakespearean text is dead; the text is a record left by a sender who cannot be questioned or corrected. This is, structurally, the decipherment problem applied to known languages.

### 5.3 The ASPIRE ONE Lunar Archive

The **ASPIRE ONE Lunar Record** is a deep-time cultural and scientific archive placed on the Moon, designed to preserve Earth's heritage for future intelligent entities. It [launched on January 15, 2025 as part of the Ghost Riders in the Sky Lunar Mission](https://www.gmu.edu/news/2025-01/george-mason-scientist-anamaria-berea-leads-team-design-lunar-cultural-archive) via Firefly Aerospace's Blue Ghost Lander from Kennedy Space Center, as part of NASA's Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) initiative within the Artemis program. It [successfully landed on Mare Crisium on March 2, 2025](https://www.gmu.edu/news/2025-03/mason-scientist-orchestrates-moon-based-message-bottle-future-generations).

The payload is housed in a **LifeShip Pyramid** (anodized aluminum, gold hue, palm-sized). Storage systems include:
- A **1GB microchip** (digital content — videos, images, texts)
- A **1MB NanoFiche analog capsule** (selected content engraved for millennial-scale survival)
- **Synthetic DNA** encoding (for scenarios where digital technology is obsolete)

Protective engineering against temperature extremes, radiation, and micrometeorite impacts gives an estimated survivability of up to billions of years (no weathering on Moon). Content spans a 24-minute "Daily Life" video, constitutions of 192 countries, Apollo 11 launch codes, and works by Newton, Curie, and Asimov.

The theoretical framework is explicitly **receiver-agnostic**: the archive is designed to be meaningful to "archaeologists or future pioneers" — human, posthuman, or otherwise. As noted in [GMU Science News coverage](https://science.gmu.edu/news/mason-scientist-anamaria-berea-orchestrates-moon-based-message-bottle-future-generations), the team grappled explicitly with the identity of the future receiver. Key design principles:

1. **Receiver-agnostic design**: "Who speaks for Earth?" — format choice (video blending visuals, subtitles, and code) is itself a semiotic strategy
2. **Redundancy across media types**: digital + analog (NanoFiche) + synthetic DNA provides degradation tolerance across radically different technological futures
3. **Representational breadth over iconic depth**: a broad cross-section of global human life, not a curated "greatest hits"
4. **Dry run for interstellar messaging**: explicitly framed as a rehearsal for future more extensive messages

The [Interstellar Foundation project page](https://www.interstellarfoundation.com/records/aspire-one) provides full documentation of the project's scope and institutional network.

## 6. SETI as Communication Theory

### 6.1 Vakoch's Exosemiotics

**Douglas Vakoch**, president of [METI International](https://meti.org/), is the scholar who has most explicitly positioned SETI as a problem in communication theory rather than merely signal detection. His edited volumes — [*Archaeology, Anthropology, and Interstellar Communication* (NASA SP-2013-4413, 2014)](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/archaeology_anthropology_and_interstellar_communication_tagged.pdf), *Communication with Extraterrestrial Intelligence* (SUNY Press, 2011), and *The Drake Equation: Estimating the Prevalence of Extraterrestrial Life through the Ages* (Cambridge, 2015) — systematically bridge SETI with semiotics, hermeneutics, and communication theory.

Vakoch's concept of **exosemiotics** — the study of sign systems that might be used in interstellar communication — confronts the absent-party problem in its most radical form: the sender and receiver may share no evolutionary history, no perceptual apparatus, no cognitive architecture, and no semiotic conventions. The challenge is not to find the right words but to find the right *kind* of sign — a sign that can bootstrap meaning from shared physical laws alone, because shared physical laws may be the only commonality.

Vakoch's approach involves constructing messages that encode information about physics, mathematics, and biology at multiple levels of abstraction, so that a receiver with sufficient intelligence can identify the encoding scheme from the message itself. This is the interstellar equivalent of the WIPP Level 4 technical documentation: a message that contains its own decoder.

### 6.2 Munévar's Evolutionary Epistemology Critique

**Gonzalo Munévar's** philosophical critique of SETI, developed in his contribution to [Vakoch (ed.), *Communication with Extraterrestrial Intelligence* (Springer)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-27437-4_8), argues that the assumption of universal mathematical communication is unfounded. Drawing on **evolutionary epistemology**, Munévar contends that mathematical structures may be contingent on the specific evolutionary and developmental history of the species that produces them. An independently evolved intelligence might have cognitive structures for which our mathematics is simply not a natural description.

This is a radical deepening of the epistemic absence modality: if even mathematics is not universal, the feedback-free communication problem becomes genuinely intractable. The SETI researcher cannot assume any shared semiotic ground — not physical constants, not mathematical structures, not logical connectives. Every proposed anchor for interstellar communication rests on an assumption about what is "universal" that cannot be verified without a confirmed contact. Munévar's challenge has not been adequately answered in the SETI literature; it remains the deepest theoretical objection to the project.

### 6.3 Freudenthal's Lincos and Constructed Languages

**Hans Freudenthal's** [*Lincos: Design of a Language for Cosmic Intercourse*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincos_(artificial_language)) (North-Holland, 1960) was the first systematic attempt to construct a language for interstellar communication from mathematical primitives. Lincos begins with natural numbers and arithmetic, builds up to logical connectives, variables, quantifiers, time, behavior, space, and eventually social concepts. The fundamental assumption is that mathematics provides a shared semiotic ground — precisely the assumption Munévar challenges.

Later extensions include:
- **Carl DeVito and Richard Oehrle's** revised Lincos (1990s), which adds biochemistry and physical constants
- **Yvan Dutil and Stéphane Dumas's** "Cosmic Call" messages (1999, 2003), transmitted from the Evpatoria Planetary Radar to several nearby stars
- **Alexander Ollongren's** continuation of Freudenthal's program in *Astrolinguistics: Design of a Linguistic System for Interstellar Communication Based on Logic* (Springer, 2012)

The Lincos program is the most sustained technical engagement with the feedforward communication problem in the interstellar context. Its limitations mirror those of the WIPP marker system: the encoder must decide, without feedback, what the receiver can bootstrap from — and there is no way to verify the decision.

### 6.4 Hermeneutic SETI: Dubinina and Tsybulco

**Dubinina and Tsybulco's** 2019 work, ["Hermeneutics of SETI"](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2019.01.002) in *Acta Astronautica*, applies Gadamerian hermeneutics to SETI, arguing that the "fusion of horizons" between human and extraterrestrial sign systems is the central challenge. Their **"hermeneutic SETI"** approach treats an intercepted alien signal not as data to be decoded but as a *text to be interpreted* — with all the hermeneutic circularity that implies.

The interpreter must bring a pre-understanding to the signal, which is then modified by the signal's resistance to that pre-understanding. This is Gadamer's hermeneutic circle applied to the most extreme case of absent-sender communication. Dubinina and Tsybulco's contribution is to recognize that the alien signal is not a puzzle with a unique solution but an invitation to a hermeneutic process that begins in the receiver's horizon and can never fully escape it. SETI, on this view, would tell us as much about ourselves as about the sender.

### 6.5 Doyle's Information Theory and the Animal Bridge

**Laurance Doyle's** information-theoretic analysis of animal communication systems provides the empirical bridge between SETI and biosemiotics. Doyle, working at the SETI Institute with collaborators Brenda McCowan and Sean Hanser, applied Zipf's law and Shannon entropy to humpback whale songs and bottlenose dolphin vocalizations in [*Acta Astronautica* (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2011.05.014), demonstrating that these systems exhibit statistical structure consistent with rule-governed communication.

The methodological insight is profound: the same information-theoretic tools used to assess whether a SETI signal is linguistic can be applied to non-human communication on Earth. Animal communication is the **intermediate case** between human and extraterrestrial — the sender is present but does not share our semiotic framework. Success in developing interpretive methods for animal communication would provide a validated toolkit for the more extreme case of interstellar signals. Doyle's program is, in effect, a test environment for absent-party communication theory.

### 6.6 Denise Herzing and the Wild Dolphin Project

**Denise Herzing's** three-decade [Wild Dolphin Project](https://www.wilddolphinproject.org/) and the **CHAT** (Cetacean Hearing and Telemetry) system represent the most sustained empirical effort to establish two-way communication with a non-human species. CHAT uses a wearable underwater computer that listens for dolphin mimicry of human-created whistles and plays the corresponding word back when detected.

Herzing's work is significant for the absent-party problem because it tests, *in vivo*, the conditions under which communication can be established between cognitive systems with non-overlapping semiotic frameworks. Crucially, CHAT has achieved **partial conversational closure**: dolphins have been observed spontaneously producing the CHAT whistles in contexts consistent with their semantic associations, suggesting that the bootstrapping process can work — at least in the favorable case where the receiver (dolphin) is present, intelligent, and motivated to communicate. This is the terrestrial analog of SETI's interstellar challenge, conducted under the best possible experimental conditions.

### 6.7 Zoo Hypothesis and Dark Forest as Communication Theories

The **Zoo Hypothesis** (Ball, 1973) and **Liu Cixin's** Dark Forest theory (from [*The Three-Body Problem*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three-Body_Problem_(novel)), 2008/English 2014) are not merely speculative solutions to the Fermi Paradox — they are theories about the **communication dynamics of absent parties**:

- **Zoo Hypothesis**: extraterrestrial civilizations are present but deliberately non-communicative — structurally absent by *choice*, not by physical constraint. This is the most disturbing variant of the absent-party problem: the sender is there, capable of communication, and choosing not to engage.
- **Dark Forest theory**: civilizations are absent from the communication channel because visibility is lethal — any civilization that reveals its position is destroyed by others. This frames absence as a strategic equilibrium in a game-theoretic communication environment.

Both theories frame absence as a **strategic communication decision** rather than a physical constraint, adding a game-theoretic dimension to the otherwise information-theoretic framing of SETI.

### 6.8 The SETI Post-Detection Hub

The [SETI Post-Detection Hub](https://seti-post-detection.org/), established in 2023 at the University of St Andrews under **John Elliott**, coordinates international preparation for how to interpret and respond to a confirmed extraterrestrial signal. This is the institutional instantiation of the absent-party problem: a dedicated infrastructure for handling the most extreme case of feedforward communication — receiving a message from a sender who may be dead, millions of light-years away, and whose encoding system is entirely unknown.

SETI's central contribution to the broader synthesis is the recognition that **communication without feedback is not an edge case** — it may be the normal condition of communication across cosmic scales. The 13.8 billion years of the universe's history means that most potential senders have been "absent" for timescales that dwarf the nuclear waste warning period.

## 7. Information Cascades Without Source Feedback

### 7.1 DARPA SocialSim Program

The **Computational Simulation of Online Social Behavior (SocialSim)** program was [launched by DARPA](https://www.darpa.mil/program/computational-simulation-of-online-social-behavior) to develop "innovative technologies for high-fidelity computational simulation of online social behavior" focused on information spread and evolution. The program funded multiple performers; UCF's Complex Adaptive Systems Laboratory's **Deep Agent** system (with Anamaria Berea as scientific lead, total funding approximately $6.2 million) was a major performer. Deep Agent used agent-based models and deep reinforcement learning to simulate cross-platform information cascades across Reddit, Twitter, and GitHub.

SocialSim's design goal was to simulate how information — including deliberately false information — propagates through a population of autonomous agents, each making rational decisions based on locally available information. The program produced the most sophisticated computational models yet constructed of the absent-sender communication problem in the social domain.

### 7.2 Foundational Cascade Theory: BHW 1992

**Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer, and Welch's** 1992 paper ["A Theory of Fads, Fashion, Custom, and Cultural Change as Informational Cascades"](https://doi.org/10.1086/261849) in the *Journal of Political Economy* established the foundational model. The BHW model describes a sequential decision problem in which each agent observes the actions (but not private signals) of predecessors and rationally updates beliefs. Beyond a critical mass, agents **rationally ignore their private information and follow the herd** — producing a cascade.

The connection to absent-party communication is structural: as the cascade propagates, the identity, intentions, and evidential basis of the original information source are progressively stripped away. The "sender" becomes absent not through distance or death but through the **dynamics of propagation itself**. A cascade is a communication event in which the sender's contribution to meaning is progressively attenuated by each retransmission, until the message is fully decoupled from any sender.

Key formal result from BHW: cascades are **fragile** — they can be disrupted by the arrival of a sufficiently strong contrary signal, but once established, even very weak private signals cannot dislodge them. This fragility has implications for absent-party communication theory: a message that enters a cascade acquires a kind of momentum that is independent of its truth value.

### 7.3 Daley-Kendall Rumor Dynamics

The **Daley-Kendall model** (1965) formalizes rumor propagation as an epidemiological process with three population classes: ignorants (susceptibles), spreaders (infectives), and stiflers (removed). The model captures a key feature of absent-sender communication: the rumor's content and framing evolve as it passes through the population, with **no mechanism for the original sender to correct drift or distortion**. Each retelling is a lossy re-encoding with no error-correction channel back to the source.

The Daley-Kendall model differs from the BHW model in a critical respect: it models a stochastic *process* rather than a rational *equilibrium*. Rumors spread not because agents are rationally herding but because the social interaction dynamics (encounter rates, recovery rates) determine propagation. This suggests that the absent-sender problem in social dynamics may be better modeled as a physical diffusion process than as a game-theoretic equilibrium — with the sender's original message playing the role of an initial condition that is progressively lost to thermal noise.

### 7.4 Vosoughi, Roy, and Aral (2018)

[Vosoughi, Roy, and Aral's 2018 *Science* study](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aap9559) of 126,000 rumor cascades on Twitter found that **false news spreads "farther, faster, deeper, and more broadly than the truth in all categories of information."** The effect was driven primarily by human behavior (novelty, emotional valence) rather than bots. The study analyzed cascades from 2006 to 2017, covering all major rumor categories.

This finding has direct implications for absent-sender communication: the original sender's intended meaning is not merely diluted by propagation — it is **systematically distorted** by the receiver population's cognitive biases. False information has higher novelty value (by definition — it violates expectations) and thus triggers stronger emotional responses that drive retransmission. The absent sender's message is thus not just degraded but actively deformed by the cascade dynamics, in a direction determined by the receivers' psychology rather than the sender's intent.

### 7.5 Wardle and Derakhshan (2017): Information Disorder Framework

**Claire Wardle and Hossein Derakhshan's** 2017 Council of Europe report ["Information Disorder: Toward an Interdisciplinary Framework for Research and Policy Making"](https://rm.coe.int/information-disorder-toward-an-interdisciplinary-framework-for-researc/168076277c) introduced a tripartite taxonomy of disinformation:
- **Mis-information**: false content, shared without intent to harm
- **Dis-information**: false content, shared with intent to harm
- **Mal-information**: true content, shared with intent to harm

The absent-sender framing unifies all three: once information enters the cascade, the sender's intent (or lack thereof) is **irrelevant to propagation dynamics**. The information behaves as an autonomous agent in the network. This point connects to Luhmann's systems theory (Section 8): in a Luhmannian frame, communication is always already independent of the sender's intention — the cascade is just the most visible case of this universal condition.

### 7.6 Luhmann's Autopoietic Communication and the Absent Sender

**Niklas Luhmann's** three-selection model of communication — information, utterance, understanding — provides the deepest theoretical reframing of the absent-sender problem in the social domain. In Luhmann's [*Social Systems* (Stanford, 1995)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Systems_(book)), the sender is structurally "in the environment" of the communication system. Communication is an *operation of social systems*, not of individuals:

> Communication is the synthesis of three selections: information (a difference selected from a range of possible differences), utterance (the way the information is put into the world), and understanding (the understanding of the difference between information and utterance).

In this framework, the absent sender is not an anomaly but the **normal condition**: all communication is, in a precise sense, communication without a sender. The individual who "sends" a message is an attribution made by the communication system after the fact — the actual operation is performed by the system, not the individual. Information cascades make this visible by stripping away the attribution.

Luhmann's framework also explains why cascades are so difficult to correct: once a communication event has been processed by the social system (information + utterance + understanding have all been selected), the sender's subsequent correction is a *new* communication event, not a modification of the original. The absent sender cannot reach back into the cascade to correct it.

### 7.7 LLM-Generated Misinformation as the New Absent-Sender Form

The emergence of large language models (2022–2026) has created an entirely new category of absent-sender communication: **algorithmically generated text** with no intentional agent behind it. LLM-generated misinformation is "absent-sender" in the strongest possible sense — there is no sender, only a stochastic process that produces plausible-looking text. This collapses the distinction between mis-information and dis-information: the "intent" dimension of Wardle and Derakhshan's framework becomes inapplicable when the source is a language model.

The cascade dynamics of LLM-generated content extend the BHW framework: the "original information source" is replaced by a process that has no private signal at all. A language model generating text about a historical event has no access to the event — it has access only to the distribution of tokens that appeared in its training corpus, which is itself a cascade of human communications about the event. LLM-generated misinformation is, in a precise sense, a simulation of the cascade dynamics it instantiates.

## 8. Adjacent Hermeneutic Traditions

### 8.1 Gadamer: The Frozen Horizon

**Hans-Georg Gadamer's** philosophical hermeneutics, developed primarily in [*Truth and Method*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_and_Method) (1960; German: *Wahrheit und Methode*), centers on the concept of **Horizontverschmelzung** — the "fusion of horizons." A "horizon" is the range of vision from a particular vantage point: the interpreter approaches a text with their own horizon of expectations, prejudices, and questions, while the text embodies the horizon of its absent author.

For the absent-party problem, the critical concept is the **frozen horizon**: when the author is dead or otherwise unreachable, their horizon is fixed — it cannot expand, adapt, or respond to the interpreter's questions. All the work of bridging must be done by the interpreter. The fusion is *asymmetric*: the interpreter fuses with a static structure, like a dancer partnering a statue. This asymmetry is precisely analogous to the feedforward condition in nuclear semiotics: the sender cannot adjust.

Gadamer's hermeneutics implicitly theorizes the absent-party problem through his account of **tradition**: the horizon of a text or artwork is not merely the individual author's but the accumulated horizon of all previous interpreters. Communication with an absent author is always, simultaneously, communication with a tradition — a chain of relay interpreters who have collectively elaborated the text's meaning. This is Sebeok's relay system translated from institutional design into philosophical anthropology.

### 8.2 Derrida: Iterability as Constitutive Absence

**Jacques Derrida's** ["Signature Event Context" (1972)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1343073) provides the most radical philosophical treatment of the absent-party problem. Derrida argues that **iterability** — the capacity of a sign to be repeated in the absence of its original sender, receiver, and context — is not an accident that befalls communication but its **constitutive condition**. A sign that could only function in the presence of its author would not be a sign at all. Writing is defined precisely by its capacity to function when the author is absent, dead, or unknown.

> "For a writing to be a writing it must continue to 'act' and to be readable even when what is called the author of the writing no longer answers for what he has written."

The "absence of the sender" is therefore not a special case — it is the **general condition of all communication**, merely foregrounded in certain extreme cases (nuclear warnings, interstellar messages, ancient texts). This is a radical inversion of the standard cybernetic model: rather than treating feedback as the norm and its absence as the exception, Derrida treats *absence* as the norm and feedback as the contingent, temporary special case.

For the category-theorist reader: Derrida's iterability is the property that makes the representamen a representamen — the capacity to function as a sign across multiple contexts, including contexts where the original object and interpretant are unavailable. It is what makes the sign a morphism in a category that persists across the removal of particular objects.

### 8.3 Ricoeur: Distanciation and the Autonomous Text

**Paul Ricoeur's** hermeneutic theory centers on **distanciation** — the process by which a text becomes autonomous from its author's intention and original context. Once inscribed, the text "says" something that the author may not have intended, and it addresses readers the author never imagined. Ricoeur distinguishes the text's "sense" (what it says, its internal semantic structure) from its "reference" (what it is about, its relation to the world). Distanciation liberates the text from the author's psychology and makes it available for new interpretive acts.

For the absent-party problem, distanciation is not a loss but a **precondition for cross-temporal communication**. The fact that the text escapes its author's control is precisely what allows it to survive the author's death and to mean something to radically different readers. Ricoeur's framework supports the ASPIRE ONE design principle: a message that is too tightly bound to the sender's intentions cannot survive the sender's absence. The message must be designed to "breathe" — to accommodate multiple readings without collapsing into arbitrary meaning.

### 8.4 Eco: Open Work and Model Reader

**Umberto Eco's** theory of the "open work" (*Opera Aperta*, 1962) and his concept of the "Model Reader" provide a semiotic framework for understanding how texts designed for absent receivers function. The **open work** is structured to invite multiple interpretations without collapsing into arbitrary meaning — it provides enough constraint to guide reading while leaving enough openness to accommodate readers with different horizons.

Eco's **"Model Reader"** is the hypothetical ideal interpreter inscribed in the text's structure — the reader the text "wants." This concept is operationally important for nuclear warning design, SETI messaging, and archival communication: in each case, the designer must construct a Model Reader for an entity that does not yet exist, and embed that construction in the message's structure. The WIPP Level 4 documentation assumes a Model Reader who can follow technical diagrams and understands nuclear physics; the ASPIRE ONE video assumes a Model Reader who can recognize biological motion. Each design decision encodes a theory of the receiver — a theory that may or may not prove correct.

### 8.5 Hayles: Bridging Cybernetics and Literary Theory

**N. Katherine Hayles's** [*How We Became Posthuman* (University of Chicago Press, 1999)](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/H/bo3769963.html) is the most sustained attempt to bridge cybernetics, information theory, and literary/cultural theory. Hayles traces how the cybernetic concept of information was progressively **disembodied** — separated from the material substrate that carries it — and argues that this disembodiment has profound consequences for how we understand communication with absent parties.

If information is substrate-independent (as Shannon's theory implies), then it can survive the destruction of its original medium — but Hayles argues that **meaning is always embodied**, so the substrate matters. The nuclear warning problem illustrates Hayles's point with unusual clarity: the same information encoded in stone, in language, in architecture, and in DNA is not the "same" message, because each medium shapes the conditions of reception. A warning inscribed in stone reaches the body differently than one embedded in folklore. Hayles's framework suggests that the choice of medium is not merely a technical decision but a semiotic one: each medium constitutes a different relationship between sender and receiver.

### 8.6 Luhmann's Three-Selection Model

As discussed in Section 7, **Luhmann's** communication theory treats the sender as structurally external to the communication system. The three-selection model — information, utterance, understanding — makes the sender's absence not an anomaly but the ordinary condition. This framework provides the most rigorous sociological account of why communication continues to function when the sender is gone.

Luhmann's systems theory connects to Pask's conversation theory through the concept of **structural coupling**: two autopoietic systems that interact regularly develop structural correspondences that enable anticipatory responses. In the absent-party case, this structural coupling is one-directional — the sender's structure is inscribed in the artifact, and the receiver's autopoietic processes respond to it. What looks like communication is, from Luhmann's perspective, a coincidence of two autonomous structural processes — a coincidence that may or may not produce "understanding" in any psychologically meaningful sense.

### 8.7 Kittler: Post-Hermeneutic Media Theory

**Friedrich Kittler's** media theory proposes that **media technologies — not human intentions — determine what can be communicated**. In [*Discourse Networks 1800/1900*](https://monoskop.org/Friedrich_Kittler) (1985) and *Gramophone, Film, Typewriter* (1986), Kittler argues that each media epoch creates its own conditions of possibility for communication, and that meaning is an effect of the medium rather than an intention of the author.

For the absent-party problem, Kittler's post-hermeneutic stance suggests that the question is not "what did the sender mean?" but **"what does the medium permit?"** — a radical reframing that shifts attention from the absent sender to the material infrastructure of transmission. The nuclear warning designer is not asking what message to send but what medium will survive 10,000 years; the SETI researcher is not asking what message to interpret but what signal structures can be produced at interstellar distances. In each case, the medium constrains the possible space of messages before any individual sender makes a choice.

Kittler's framework also illuminates the LLM case: the large language model is a medium — a Kittlerian "discourse network" — that determines what can be "said" before any user prompt specifies a message. The absent sender of LLM-generated text is not any individual user but the discourse network itself, which selects from the distribution of human language its training corpus encoded.

### 8.8 Stiegler: Tertiary Retention

**Bernard Stiegler's** concept of **tertiary retention** — drawing on Husserl's phenomenology of time-consciousness — provides the most direct philosophical account of how artifacts carry meaning across generations. In [*Technics and Time* (3 vols., Stanford, 1994–2001)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Stiegler):

- **Primary retention**: the just-past moment still held in consciousness
- **Secondary retention**: memory
- **Tertiary retention**: externalized memory — tools, texts, recordings, institutions

Stiegler argues that tertiary retention is **constitutive of human temporality**: we are the beings who inherit and transmit through artifacts. Nuclear warning markers, lunar archives, genetic codes, and LLM-generated texts are all forms of tertiary retention — messages from absent senders preserved in material substrates that outlast their originators. The absent-party problem is, in Stiegler's terms, the fundamental problem of human technicity: we are always already in communication with absent senders, because our world is constituted by artifacts that encode the intentions of those who made them.

## 9. Biosemiotics Boundary Cases

### 9.1 Barbieri's Code Biology and Organic Codes

**Marcello Barbieri** (b. 1943, Ferrara) is the central figure in **code biology**, a program he distinguishes sharply from mainstream biosemiotics. His career-defining insight, developed from the 1980s onward and formalized in [*Biosemiotics* (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12304-014-9236-4), is that biological life is constituted not by copying alone but by **coding** — and that codes are irreducibly different from physical laws or chemical reactions.

An organic code requires three conditions:
1. Two independent molecular "worlds" (e.g., nucleotides and amino acids)
2. A set of adaptor molecules that create a mapping between them (e.g., tRNAs)
3. Demonstration that the mapping is **arbitrary** — that it could in principle have been otherwise

The genetic code satisfies all three criteria: tRNAs map codons to amino acids, and the mapping is not chemically determined (there is no direct stereochemical affinity forcing a given codon to bind a specific amino acid). Barbieri has identified at least **280 organic codes** in biology, from the histone code to the signal transduction code, each connecting distinct molecular domains through arbitrary but stable mappings.

For the absent-party problem, Barbieri's contribution is foundational: **the genetic code is a message from absent ancestors**. The "sender" (the ancestral organism that established the code) is not present when the code is "read" (during translation in the ribosome). The code's meaning is constituted by the molecular machinery that interprets it, not by the sender's intention. This is the biological analog of Derrida's iterability: the genetic code functions precisely because it can operate in the sender's absence.

### 9.2 Hoffmeyer's Semiosphere and Semiotic Freedom

**Jesper Hoffmeyer** (1942–2019), the leading figure of the Copenhagen school of biosemiotics, introduced the concept of the **semiosphere** — the sphere of sign processes that pervades and constitutes the living world, analogous to Vernadsky's biosphere. His [work on semiotic freedom](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-007-4917-0_11) — the capacity of organisms to interpret signs in multiple ways, increasing with evolutionary complexity — provides a biosemiotic measure of how much "interpretive latitude" a receiver has.

Higher semiotic freedom means a greater capacity to extract meaning from ambiguous or degraded signals — precisely the capacity needed to interpret messages from absent senders. In Hoffmeyer's framework, evolutionary complexity is partly a story of increasing capacity to reconstruct absent senders' "intentions" from fragmentary signals. The mammalian immune system, which infers the presence of past pathogens from antibody patterns in the blood, is an extreme case of this capacity: it is communicating with an absent enemy.

**Sign action without a sender**: Hoffmeyer emphasizes that in most biological communication, there is no "sender" in the intentional sense. A flower's color "signals" to bees not because the flower intends to signal but because the color has been selected as a reliable correlate of nectar. The bee interprets a sign that was "sent" by natural selection, not by any individual flower. This is the biological analog of information cascade dynamics: the "sender" is an evolutionary process, not an agent.

### 9.3 Pattee's Epistemic Cut

**Howard Pattee's** concept of the **epistemic cut** — the irreducible distinction between the physical dynamics of a system and its symbolic description — is fundamental to understanding how biological communication works across generations. Developed in ["The Physics of Symbols: Bridging the Epistemic Cut" (*BioSystems*, 2001)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0303-2647(00)00110-1), the epistemic cut identifies the distinction between:
- The physical molecule (DNA as a chemical entity subject to thermodynamic laws)
- The symbolic structure (DNA as a message subject to semiotic interpretation)

The genetic code operates at the epistemic cut: DNA is simultaneously a physical molecule subject to chemical laws and a symbolic structure subject to semiotic interpretation. Pattee argues that this duality is **irreducible** — neither the physical nor the symbolic description can be derived from the other. This is why biological information is robust to physical damage: the symbolic structure can be reconstructed from a degraded physical substrate, as long as enough of the redundant encoding survives. It is also why biological information can be "misread" — the physical molecule is unchanged, but the interpreting machinery reads a different symbol.

For the absent-party problem, the epistemic cut explains why biological messages survive the sender's death: the symbolic dimension is substrate-independent enough to be read by a new interpreter, while the physical dimension ensures material continuity. The WIPP designers, in aspiring to messages that survive civilizational change, are attempting to engineer an artificial epistemic cut — messages that maintain their symbolic dimension across physical and cultural degradation.

### 9.4 Epigenetic Inheritance as Cross-Temporal Messaging

**Eva Jablonka and Marion Lamb's** [*Evolution in Four Dimensions* (MIT Press, 2014, rev. ed.)](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262525848/evolution-in-four-dimensions/) demonstrates that biological communication across generations operates through multiple channels with different fidelity, bandwidth, and error-correction properties. Their four-dimensional model includes:
- **Genetic inheritance**: high fidelity, low bandwidth, very slow change
- **Epigenetic inheritance**: medium fidelity, medium bandwidth, faster change
- **Behavioral inheritance**: lower fidelity, higher bandwidth, fastest change
- **Symbolic inheritance**: lowest fidelity, highest bandwidth, cultural change

Epigenetic inheritance — the transmission of gene-expression patterns through DNA methylation, histone modification, and small RNA pathways — is particularly significant for the absent-party framing: the "message" (the epigenetic mark) was set by environmental conditions experienced by the parent, but it is "read" by the offspring in a potentially different environment. The sender (the parental generation's cellular machinery) is absent, and the receiver (the offspring's developmental process) must interpret the mark without access to the sender's context.

Recent research has dramatically extended the temporal range of epigenetic inheritance: some methylation patterns have been shown to persist for at least four generations in mammals, suggesting that the "messages" encoded by great-great-grandparents' environmental experiences are still being "read" by descendants. This is a form of deep-time communication within the biological domain — structurally parallel to the nuclear waste warning problem.

### 9.5 Niche Construction and Mycorrhizal Networks

**Odling-Smee, Laland, and Feldman's** [*Niche Construction: The Neglected Process in Evolution* (Princeton, 2003)](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691044378/niche-construction) extends the absent-sender framework to **ecological inheritance**: organisms modify their environments in ways that affect the selection pressures experienced by future generations. The beaver dam, the earthworm's soil modification, and the human-built environment are all **messages to future organisms** — feedforward communications embedded in the physical world rather than in a symbolic medium.

**Mycorrhizal networks** — the underground fungal networks connecting trees in forests — represent a particularly striking case of absent-sender communication in the biological domain. They mediate chemical signaling between plants that may be separated by meters or decades, creating a communication system where the "sender" may no longer exist when the signal is received. A tree destroyed by storm or disease may have already sent chemical signals through the mycorrhizal network that will influence the growth of neighboring trees for years afterward — a biological analog of the nuclear warning marker that continues to communicate after the warning-system designers have died.

### 9.6 Deacon's Absential Features

**Terrence Deacon's** [*Incomplete Nature: How Mind Emerged from Matter* (W.W. Norton, 2012)](https://wwnorton.com/books/Incomplete-Nature/) introduces the concept of **absential features** — properties defined by what is absent rather than what is present. Information, function, purpose, and meaning are all absential: they are defined by the constraints they impose, the possibilities they exclude, the states they prevent. A genetic sequence "means" something precisely because its structure constrains which amino acids can follow — its meaning is in the absences it creates.

Deacon's framework provides a deep theoretical justification for treating absence as **constitutive rather than privative**. The absent sender is not a deficiency in the communication system — the sender's absence is what makes the message a message rather than a direct interaction. If the sender were present, there would be no need for a message; the interaction would be direct, pre-linguistic, immediate. The absent sender is thus the condition of possibility for the message as such. This inverts the standard cybernetic model and brings it into alignment with Derrida's thesis about iterability.

### 9.7 Kull and the Tartu School

**Kalevi Kull**, the leading figure of the Tartu school of biosemiotics (following **Jakob von Uexküll's** tradition of *Umwelt* theory), emphasizes the **Umwelt** — the organism's subjective, species-specific perceptual world — as the fundamental unit of biosemiotic analysis. Kull's work on ["The Logic of Animal Umwelten" (*Chinese Semiotic Studies*, 2018)](https://doi.org/10.1515/css-2018-0003) develops the implications for cross-species communication.

Communication between organisms with different Umwelten is **communication across an epistemic gap** — a biological analog of the SETI problem. The Tartu school's contribution is to show that this gap is not the exception but the rule: every act of inter-species communication (and, by extension, every act of cross-generational communication) involves interpretation across incommensurable experiential worlds. The honeybee's waggle dance encodes spatial information in a format that is inaccessible to non-bees — the dance is a message designed (by evolution) for receivers with a specific Umwelt, and it is opaque to beings with different sensory architectures.

This implies a deep limit on the generalizability of any communication system: every message is designed for a specific Umwelt, and the farther the receiver's Umwelt is from the sender's, the more the message must bootstrap its own interpretation.

### 9.8 Key Insight: Heredity as the Ur-Case

The central insight from the biosemiotics literature is that **heredity itself is the ur-case of absent-sender communication**. The genetic code, epigenetic marks, niche modifications, and behavioral traditions are all messages from ancestors who are no longer present. The biological world has been solving the absent-sender problem for **3.8 billion years** — and the solutions it has evolved (redundant coding, modular architecture, error-correction mechanisms, layered transmission channels) are strikingly convergent with the engineering solutions proposed for nuclear warning design and interstellar messaging.

This convergence is not coincidental: it reflects the fact that the underlying information-theoretic constraints are the same. Any communication system that must encode information for unknown receivers, transmit it through degrading channels, and enable reconstruction without feedback will converge on the same design principles — because those principles are dictated by the mathematics of information, not by the specific domain.

## 10. Brier's Cybersemiotics — The Integrative Framework

### 10.1 The Problem of Integration

The eight domains surveyed in this report draw on distinct theoretical traditions — Shannon's information theory, Pask's conversation theory, Peircean semiotics, Gadamerian hermeneutics, Luhmann's autopoiesis, Barbieri's code biology — that have developed largely in isolation. Each tradition provides genuine insight into the absent-party problem but remains incomplete. **Søren Brier's cybersemiotics** represents the most systematic attempt to bridge these traditions into a single coherent framework.

### 10.2 Brier's Framework: The Five-Level Model

Brier's foundational work, [*Cybersemiotics: Why Information Is Not Enough*](https://utorontopress.com/9780802092519/cybersemiotics/) (University of Toronto Press, 2008), argues that neither information theory nor semiotics alone can provide a complete account of communication and cognition:

- **Information theory** (Shannon, Wiener) captures the syntactic dimension — the structure and transmission properties of signals — but cannot account for meaning, interpretation, or lived experience
- **Semiotics** (Peirce, Sebeok) captures the semantic and pragmatic dimensions — how signs mean and how they are used — but lacks the mathematical rigor and the account of physical substrate that information theory provides

Brier proposes a **five-level model** integrating these traditions:

**Level 1**: *Physical-chemical information processing.* The domain of Shannon's mathematical theory and thermodynamic entropy. Signs at this level are physical configurations subject to natural laws. Absent-party communication at this level: the physical artifact (the nuclear marker, the DNA molecule) persists as a material trace.

**Level 2**: *Biological autopoiesis and code biology.* The domain of Maturana and Varela's autopoiesis and Barbieri's organic codes. Living systems process information through self-referential, organizationally closed processes. Absent-party communication at this level: the biological interpreter's autopoietic processes determine how the material trace is "read."

**Level 3**: *Psychological-phenomenological experience.* The domain of Peirce's firstness — qualia, feelings, the "inner" dimension of sign processes. This is the level that pure information theory cannot reach. Absent-party communication at this level: the phenomenological experience of encountering the absent sender's message shapes interpretation.

**Level 4**: *Social communication systems.* The domain of Luhmann's autopoietic social systems and Habermas's communicative action. Communication is an operation of social systems that cannot be reduced to individual psychology. Absent-party communication at this level: social systems process the message according to their own operational logic.

**Level 5**: *Linguistic-conceptual frameworks.* The domain of Pask's conversation theory and Gadamer's hermeneutics. Understanding is constituted through mutual interpretation and the fusion of horizons. Absent-party communication at this level: the interpreter's entailment mesh engages with the frozen entailment mesh of the absent sender.

### 10.3 Cybersemiotics and the Absent-Party Problem

Brier's framework could potentially unify the eight domains surveyed here by providing a consistent account of how absence operates at each level:

- At Level 1, the absent sender's signal persists as a physical trace (the nuclear marker, the DNA sequence)
- At Level 2, the biological interpreter's autopoietic processes determine how the trace is "read"
- At Level 3, the phenomenological experience of encountering the absent sender's message shapes interpretation
- At Level 4, social systems process the message according to their own operational logic
- At Level 5, the interpreter's entailment mesh engages with the frozen entailment mesh of the absent sender

However, Brier's work does not explicitly address the absent-party problem in these terms. His focus is on the integration of existing theoretical traditions rather than on the specific case of communication without feedback. The application of cybersemiotics to the absent-party problem remains an **open research program**. Recent work by Brier in the *Journal of Biosemiotics* and through the International Association for Cybersemiotics and [*Cybernetics & Human Knowing*](https://chfrp.wordpress.com/cybernetics-human-knowing/) continues to develop the framework, but the explicit application to deep-time warning design, SETI, decipherment, and biological heredity has not yet been carried out.

### 10.4 Brier's Critique of Computational Information Theory

A central argument in Brier's work is that the Shannon-Wiener information concept is **necessary but not sufficient** for understanding communication. Shannon's information is purely syntactic — it measures the reduction of uncertainty in a signal but says nothing about what the signal means or how it is experienced. Wiener's cybernetic feedback model adds the dimension of control and self-regulation, but remains agnostic about subjective experience.

Brier argues that **Peirce's triadic semiotics** (sign, object, interpretant) provides the missing dimension: the interpretant — the effect the sign produces in the interpreter — is irreducibly experiential and cannot be captured by a purely formal or computational description. For the absent-party problem, this critique is consequential. If the Shannon-Wiener framework were sufficient, absent-party communication would be a purely technical problem of signal preservation and noise reduction — and it would have been solved by the nuclear engineers and information theorists long ago. The fact that it remains unsolved after four decades of effort (the HITF was commissioned in 1981) suggests that something beyond syntactic information preservation is required: what Brier calls the "semantic and pragmatic closure" of the communication act.

### 10.5 Peirce's Semiosis and the Absent Interpretant

Brier's reliance on Peircean semiotics highlights a feature of the absent-party problem that other frameworks obscure: in Peirce's triadic model, every sign relation involves a **representamen** (sign vehicle), an **object** (what the sign refers to), and an **interpretant** (the effect produced in the interpreter). The interpretant is itself a sign, producing further interpretants in an infinite chain of semiosis.

When the sender is absent, the **interpretant chain that the sender would have produced is truncated** — the sender's intended interpretant is unavailable. The receiver must generate their own interpretant chain, which may diverge from the sender's intended chain. This is not a failure of communication but the normal condition of semiosis: Peirce himself recognized that sign interpretation is always fallible and revisable.

The cybersemiotic integration thus provides a principled account of why absent-party communication is genuinely difficult (it requires the receiver to generate an interpretant chain without access to the sender's intended interpretants) while also explaining why it is not impossible (semiosis is inherently open-ended and does not require the sender's presence to proceed). The absent sender's message provides the representamen; the world provides the object; the receiver's semiotic competence generates the interpretant. **The circuit is incomplete but functional.**

### 11.2 Five Major Theoretical Gaps

**Gap 1: No Paskian Treatment of Absent-Party Decipherment.** Pask's entailment mesh provides a natural formal framework for what decipherers actually do — reconstruct the derivational structure of an absent sender — yet no published work applies this formalism to script decipherment. Kober's triplets, Ventris's grid, and computational decipherment approaches are all implicitly entailment-mesh reconstructions, but the connection has never been made explicit. The formal statement would be: decipherment is the construction of an entailment mesh isomorphism between the observer's conceptual framework and the absent sender's encoding system, using the statistical properties of the text as constraints.

**Gap 2: No Systematic Second-Order Cybernetic Framing of Nuclear Semiotics.** Although Sebeok (1984) draws on Shannon and Wiener, and Flusser's media theory is cybernetics-adjacent, no published work provides a systematic second-order cybernetic analysis of the nuclear warning problem. The HITF literature treats the problem as engineering and semiotics; it does not employ the conceptual vocabulary of von Foerster's observing systems, Maturana's structural coupling, or Pask's conversation theory. A second-order cybernetic framing would reframe the problem: not "how do we send a message to a future receiver?" but "how do we design a system that will construct a model of the future receiver, and embed that model in the message's structure?"

**Gap 3: Incomplete Bridge Between Biosemiotics and Information Cascade Theory.** Both domains deal with communication where the original source is structurally absent, but the theoretical vocabularies are disjoint. Biosemiotics (Barbieri, Hoffmeyer) uses Peircean sign theory; cascade theory (BHW, network science) uses game theory and epidemiology. Luhmann's autopoiesis could bridge the two — both biological heredity and social information cascades involve autopoietic systems processing signs according to their own operational logic — but the connection has not been systematically developed.

**Gap 4: Berea's Work Needs Explicit Grounding in Second-Order Cybernetics.** Berea's research program is implicitly cybernetic — she studies communication as a complex adaptive system using agent-based models, network analysis, and information theory. But her published work does not explicitly engage with the cybernetic tradition (Wiener, Ashby, von Foerster, Pask, Maturana). Grounding her framework in second-order cybernetics would strengthen its theoretical foundations and connect it to the broader absent-party synthesis. The natural bridge is through Maturana's concept of structural coupling: the question in every Berea sub-domain is how two structurally coupled systems (or their successors) maintain semiotic coherence across temporal, spatial, or epistemic gaps.

**Gap 5: The Derrida–Cybernetics Connection Remains Undertheorized Despite Stiegler.** Derrida's iterability thesis and cybernetic feedback theory address the same structural problem — how communication functions in the absence of presence — but from radically different intellectual traditions. Stiegler's concept of tertiary retention provides a partial bridge, and Hayles's work on cybernetics and literary theory gestures toward the connection, but no published work has systematically mapped the isomorphism between Derrida's constitutive absence and the cybernetic concept of feedforward-only communication. This gap is the deepest: closing it would require showing that iterability is not merely a philosophical observation about signs but a formal property of any communication system that operates without feedback.

### 11.3 Convergent Solutions Across Domains

Despite the diversity of theoretical frameworks, the eight domains converge on a remarkably consistent set of **design principles for absent-party communication**:

**Redundancy.** Shannon's fundamental insight — that redundancy counteracts noise — appears in every domain: the WIPP four-level message system, the multi-modal encoding of ASPIRE ONE (digital + analog + DNA), the genetic code's degeneracy (multiple codons mapping to the same amino acid), the hermeneutic tradition's emphasis on multiple readings, and Eco's open work. The principle is universal because it follows from the mathematics of information, not from domain-specific considerations.

**Multi-modal encoding.** Peirce's triadic sign theory — iconic, indexical, symbolic — provides the framework: effective absent-party communication uses all three modes simultaneously, so that degradation of any one does not destroy the message. WIPP uses all three: the Landscape of Thorns (iconic: spikes look dangerous), the site itself (indexical: it marks what lies beneath), and the multilingual text (symbolic). ASPIRE ONE uses all three: video (iconic), the payload's lunar location (indexical), and the constitutions and codes (symbolic).

**Institutional relay.** Sebeok's relay system, Stiegler's tertiary retention, and the biosemiotic concept of cultural niche construction all involve **embedding the message in self-perpetuating social or biological structures** that actively maintain and re-encode it across generations. The genetic code is maintained by the ribosomal machinery that reads it; the nuclear warning is maintained (ideally) by the custodial institutions that oversee the site; the ASPIRE ONE archive is maintained by the lunar environment itself (no weathering).

**Designed interpretive latitude.** Eco's Model Reader, Hoffmeyer's semiotic freedom, and Laurillard's pedagogical design all address the same challenge: creating messages that are **structured enough to guide interpretation but open enough to accommodate receivers with different frameworks**. The optimal message is not maximally constrained (which would make it decipherable only by receivers identical to the sender) nor maximally open (which would make it arbitrary and meaningless to any receiver).

**Eigenform convergence.** Von Foerster's eigenforms, Pask's agreement over an understanding, and the decipherer's iterative hypothesis-testing all describe the same process: a **self-stabilizing interpretive cycle** that converges on a stable meaning without requiring the sender's confirmation. The receiver tests a hypothesis, the message either confirms or resists it, and the receiver updates — eventually converging on a stable interpretation that is the eigenform of the iterative process.

### 11.4 The Information-Theoretic Isomorphism

At the deepest level, all eight domains share a common information-theoretic structure. In each case, the communication system must:

1. **Encode information** in a form that survives degradation of the channel (noise)
2. **Provide sufficient redundancy** that the message can be reconstructed from partial reception
3. **Include metacommunicative signals** that allow the receiver to calibrate their decoding process
4. **Accommodate the possibility** that the receiver's decoding apparatus is unknown or incommensurable with the sender's encoding apparatus

[Shannon's channel coding theorem (1948)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Mathematical_Theory_of_Communication) guarantees that reliable communication is possible over a noisy channel, provided the transmission rate is below the channel capacity. But Shannon's theorem assumes a **fixed, known channel** and a **receiver that shares the sender's codebook**. The absent-party problem violates both assumptions: the channel is not merely noisy but potentially reconfigured (languages change, materials decay, media become obsolete), and the receiver's codebook is unknown.

This means that the standard Shannon framework must be extended in two directions:
- Toward a theory of communication over **non-stationary channels** (channels whose properties change over time)
- Toward a theory of **codebook-free communication** (communication where the encoding scheme must be inferred from the message itself)

**Kolmogorov complexity theory** offers a partial solution: a message that contains its own decompression algorithm (a self-extracting archive, in computational terms) can in principle be decoded by any sufficiently powerful computational system, regardless of prior agreement on a codebook. The WIPP four-level message system approximates this ideal: Level 4 (technical records) provides the "codebook" for interpreting Levels 1–3. Freudenthal's Lincos attempts the same strategy for interstellar communication, building the language from mathematical primitives that are (hypothetically) universally computable. The genetic code achieves it through the ribosome — a molecular machine that is both the decoder and part of the message.

### 11.5 Toward a Formal Theory of Absent-Party Communication

The synthesis presented here suggests the outlines of a formal theory. An absent-party communication system can be characterized by a tuple:

```
(S, R, M, C, T, E)
```

where:
- **S** = the sender (absent at time of reception)
- **R** = the receiver (unknown to sender at time of encoding)
- **M** = the message (the artifact)
- **C** = the channel (potentially degrading and non-stationary)
- **T** = the temporal separation (sender encoding → receiver decoding)
- **E** = the epistemic distance (degree of framework non-overlap between S and R)

The system's **reliability** depends on the interaction of these parameters: as T increases, C degrades; as E increases, the effective codebook shrinks; as both increase together (the nuclear warning case, the SETI case), the system approaches a fundamental limit.

The key **design variable** is **self-reference**: the degree to which M contains information about how to interpret itself. Pure self-reference (a message that is its own decoder) is the theoretical ideal; in practice, every absent-party communication system attempts to approximate it through layered encoding (WIPP), mathematical bootstrapping (Lincos), multimodal redundancy (ASPIRE ONE), or structural regularity that invites pattern-matching (the genetic code).

The central open question is whether there exists a **fundamental limit on self-referential encoding** — a Gödelian incompleteness for communication that proves no message can fully specify its own interpretation. The analogy with Gödel is more than metaphorical: Gödel's incompleteness theorems show that any sufficiently powerful formal system contains truths that cannot be proven within the system. By analogy, any sufficiently complex absent-party message may contain meanings that cannot be fully specified within the message itself — meanings that require the receiver's interpretive creativity to complete. This would be a formal vindication of Glanville's insight that the interpreter's creativity is not a workaround for the absent party's absence but the constitutive condition of the communication.

### 11.6 Implications for Audio Production

For a reader versed in audio production, several connections warrant special mention. The mastering process addresses a version of the absent-party problem: the mix engineer creates a sonic artifact for an unknown listener on unknown playback equipment in an unknown acoustic environment. The standard solution — monitoring on multiple systems (full-range monitors, near-fields, consumer earbuds, car stereos), applying frequency-domain corrections that anticipate a range of playback conditions, and using reference tracks to calibrate subjective judgment — is structurally isomorphic to the WIPP strategy of multi-level, multi-modal encoding.

The mix is a feedforward communication designed to survive translation across an unknown channel. A "master" that sounds excellent on one system but fails on another has failed the multi-modal redundancy principle — it has encoded the message in a single sign mode (the full-range monitoring environment) rather than distributing it across the full range of possible reception conditions. The mastering engineer who checks their work on earbuds is applying Jakobson's insight about multiple sign modes: if the message fails at any single mode of reception, the communication has failed.

More broadly, the concept of "translating" a mix across different playback environments is formally parallel to Voigt's concentric linguistic ring system: both are strategies for ensuring that the message survives multiple layers of mediation, with each layer providing enough redundancy to reconstruct the essential content even if adjacent layers are degraded.

### 11.7 Implications for Category Theory: The Functor Framing

**Category theory** provides a potentially powerful formal language for the absent-party problem. The key concept is the **functor**: a structure-preserving mapping between categories.

If we model:
- The sender's conceptual framework as a category **C_S** (with objects as concepts and morphisms as inferential relations)
- The receiver's framework as a category **C_R**

Then successful communication requires a functor:

```
F: C_S → C_R
```

that preserves the relevant structural relations. The **absent-party problem** is the problem of constructing this functor when C_R is unknown.

Pask's entailment mesh is, in categorical terms, a directed graph that approximates the morphism structure of C_S; **decipherment** is the construction of a functor from that graph to C_R. The decipherer's iterative hypothesis-testing is, in categorical terms, searching for a functor F such that F applied to the sender's sign structures maps to recognizable structures in the receiver's conceptual category.

**Natural transformations** between functors provide the formal language for what Gadamer calls the "fusion of horizons": a natural transformation between F_1: C_S → C_R and F_2: C_S → C_R is a systematic family of morphisms in C_R that maps between two different interpretations of the sender's framework. The hermeneutic circle, in these terms, is the process of constructing a sequence of natural transformations that converge on a stable functor — a stable interpretation of the absent sender's conceptual structure.

**Biological heredity** in this framing: the genetic code is a functor from the category of codon sequences to the category of amino acid sequences, mediated by the tRNA molecular machinery. The "absent-party problem" of heredity is that the ancestral organism that established the code (selected the functor) is absent when the code is applied — but the molecular machinery (the functor itself) has been preserved, and it faithfully maps new codon sequences to amino acid sequences without needing to consult the ancestor.

**The SETI challenge** in this framing: constructing a functor F: C_S → C_R when C_S (the alien sender's conceptual framework) is entirely unknown except through its outputs (the signal). This is a problem of **functor reconstruction from morphism data** — a problem that has not, to our knowledge, been formally addressed in the category theory literature, and that represents a significant opportunity for formal theory-building at the intersection of category theory, cybernetics, and communication theory.

### Information Cascades (Section 7)

### Hermeneutic Traditions (Section 8)

- **Gadamer, Hans-Georg** (1960/2004). *Truth and Method* (2nd rev. ed.). Continuum. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_and_Method) — The central text of philosophical hermeneutics; "fusion of horizons" concept; the frozen horizon analysis is derivable from Gadamer's account of tradition.

- **Derrida, Jacques** (1972). Signature Event Context. *Glyph* 1: 172–197. [JSTOR DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1343073) — The most radical philosophical treatment of the absent-party problem; iterability as the constitutive condition of all communication, not its exception.

- **Eco, Umberto** (1962/1989). *The Open Work*. Harvard University Press. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Open_Work) — Open work theory and Model Reader concept; the semiotic framework for designing texts for absent receivers; balance between constraint and openness.

- **Hayles, N. Katherine** (1999). *How We Became Posthuman: Virtual Bodies in Cybernetics, Literature, and Informatics*. University of Chicago Press. [UCP](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/H/bo3769963.html) — The key bridge between cybernetics and literary/cultural theory; disembodiment of information and its implications for meaning; embodiment argument for why substrate matters.

- **Kittler, Friedrich** (1985/1986). *Discourse Networks 1800/1900* and *Gramophone, Film, Typewriter*. Stanford University Press. [Monoskop](https://monoskop.org/Friedrich_Kittler) — Post-hermeneutic media theory; media technologies determine what can be communicated; shifts attention from absent sender to material infrastructure of transmission.

- **Stiegler, Bernard** (1994–2001). *Technics and Time* (3 vols.). Stanford University Press. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Stiegler) — Tertiary retention theory; technics as constitutive of human temporality; artifacts as messages from absent senders that constitute our inherited world.

- **Bateson, Gregory** (1972). *Steps to an Ecology of Mind*. University of Chicago Press. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steps_to_an_Ecology_of_Mind) — "A difference that makes a difference"; ecosystemic epistemology; the minimal cybernetic criterion for information detection; the bridge between cybernetics and hermeneutics.

- **Maturana, Humberto and Varela, Francisco** (1980). *Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living*. D. Reidel. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis_and_Cognition) — Structural coupling and autopoiesis; the theoretical basis for understanding how two closed systems can achieve communication without full information transfer.

- **Luhmann, Niklas** (1984/1995). *Social Systems*. Stanford University Press. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Systems_(book)) — Three-selection model of communication; the sender is structurally external to the communication system; all communication is, in a precise sense, communication without a sender.

### Biosemiotics (Section 9)

- **Barbieri, Marcello** (2015). Code Biology — A New Science of Life. *Biosemiotics* 8: 239–258. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12304-014-9236-4) — The theoretical foundation of code biology; the organic code concept; the genetic code as a message from absent ancestors; at least 280 organic codes identified in biology.

[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-007-4917-0_11) — Semiosphere and semiotic freedom concepts; biosemiotic measure of a receiver's interpretive capacity; evolution as increasing capacity to interpret absent-sender signals.

- **Pattee, Howard** (2001). The Physics of Symbols: Bridging the Epistemic Cut. *BioSystems* 60(1–3): 5–21. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0303-2647(00)00110-1) — The epistemic cut between physical dynamics and symbolic description; explains why biological messages survive the sender's death through substrate independence of the symbolic dimension.

- **Jablonka, Eva and Lamb, Marion** (2014). *Evolution in Four Dimensions: Genetic, Epigenetic, Behavioral, and Symbolic Variation in the History of Life* (rev. ed.). MIT Press. [Norton](https://wwnorton.com/books/Incomplete-Nature/) — Absential features theory; information, function, purpose, and meaning are defined by what is absent; the absent sender is constitutive of the message as such.

[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/css-2018-0003) — Tartu school biosemiotics; Umwelt theory and its implications for cross-species communication; every act of cross-species communication involves interpretation across incommensurable experiential worlds.

### Cross-Cutting Foundations

- **Shannon, Claude E.** (1948). A Mathematical Theory of Communication. *Bell System Technical Journal* 27: 379–423. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Mathematical_Theory_of_Communication) — The foundational paper; establishes channel capacity, redundancy, and the channel coding theorem; the standard that all absent-party communication theories must extend.

- **Wiener, Norbert** (1948). *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine*. MIT Press. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybernetics:_Or_Control_and_Communication_in_the_Animal_and_the_Machine) — The foundational cybernetics text; feedback, feedforward, and the distinction between them; the implicit framework for all absent-party communication theory.

- **von Foerster, Heinz** (1981). *Observing Systems*. Intersystems Publications. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_von_Foerster) — Second-order cybernetics; eigenforms; the observer as part of the observed system; the constructivist epistemology that reframes decipherment as eigenform construction.

- **Liu Cixin** (2008/2014). *The Three-Body Problem* (English trans. Ken Liu). Tor Books. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three-Body_Problem_(novel)) — Dark Forest theory as a communication theory; strategic absence as a game-theoretic equilibrium in a universe of hidden civilizations.

- **Sebeok, Thomas A. and Danesi, Marcel** (2000). *The Forms of Meaning: Modeling Systems Theory and Semiotic Analysis*. Mouton de Gruyter. — Modeling systems theory applied to semiotic analysis; integrates Sebeok's semiotic approach with systems-theoretic modeling; a key bridge text.

- **Brier, Søren and Joslyn, Cliff** (eds.) (2012). Special issue: Cybersemiotics and information ethics. *Cybernetics & Human Knowing* 19(3). — Applies cybersemiotic framework to information ethics; relevant to questions of responsibility in absent-party communication design.

- **McCowan, B., Hanser, S., and Doyle, L.** (1999). Quantitative tools for comparing animal communication systems. *Animal Behaviour* 57(2): 409–419. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S000334729986997X) — The foundational information-theoretic animal communication paper by Doyle's group; establishes the methodology that connects SETI signal analysis to biosemiotics.
