## Central Thesis

Standard communication theory (Shannon, Wiener, Pask) presupposes co-present, mutually responsive interlocutors. When the feedback channel is severed — by deep time, interstellar distance, death, radical epistemic incommensurability, structural propagation, or biological discontinuity — communication does not merely degrade. It changes in kind. Across all eight domains surveyed, a structural pattern recurs: the absence of the interlocutor forces reliance on redundancy, multi-modal encoding, institutional relay, iterability, or organic codes.

Key finding: **heredity is the ur-case of absent-sender communication.** Every other domain is a specialized variant.

## Five Modalities of Absence (Section 1)

1. **Temporal** — deep time separating sender and receiver (WIPP, Onkalo, heredity)
2. **Spatial** — interstellar distance severing the feedback loop (SETI)
3. **Epistemic** — non-overlapping cognitive/semiotic frameworks (undeciphered scripts)
4. **Structural** — sender's identity stripped through cascade propagation (information cascades, Luhmann)
5. **Biological** — cross-generational genetic/epigenetic signaling (heredity, epigenetics, niche construction)

## Convergent Design Principles (Section 11)

All absent-party communication systems converge on four strategies:
1. **Redundancy** — encode the same message multiple ways; assume some channels will fail
2. **Multi-modal encoding** — iconic + indexical + symbolic (Peirce's trichotomy applied practically)
3. **Institutional relay** — embed the message in a social/institutional structure that outlasts the artifact
4. **Designed interpretive latitude** — leave room for the receiver to reconstruct meaning rather than demanding precise decoding
