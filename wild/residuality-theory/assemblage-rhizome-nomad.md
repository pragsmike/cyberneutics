# Assemblage, Rhizome, Nomad: Deleuze-Guattari as Residuality's Operative Grammar

## What this note argues

Three concepts from Deleuze-Guattari — *assemblage*, *rhizome*, and *nomadic distribution* — are operative in residuality theory even though O'Reilly never uses the vocabulary. The structural correspondences are not loose analogies. Each of the three concepts maps onto a load-bearing residuality construct closely enough that a Deleuze-Guattari reader recognizes the residuality construction immediately, and a residuality practitioner reading *A Thousand Plateaus* recognizes their own discipline.

This is more than the diffuse "Deleuzian flavor" the 2021 *Philosophy of Residuality Theory* paper acknowledges and the 2023 *Residuality and Representation* paper develops. It is a specific claim: residuality theory's *conceptual grammar* — its ontology of what residues are, how they connect, and how the architect moves among them — is Deleuze-Guattarian even in passages that use neither name.

The claim matters for two reasons. First, it tightens the philosophical reading of residuality available to cyberneutics: where the 2021 paper anchors on Serres and Latour and the 2023 paper anchors on Kant, Cilliers, and Deleuze, the *Mille Plateaux* vocabulary unifies the picture in a way O'Reilly's piecewise references do not. Second, it sharpens what cyberneutics inherits: cyberneutics' own architectural commitments are also Deleuze-Guattarian, and the convergence between residuality and cyberneutics under this grammar is stronger than the convergence under the 2021 paper's Serres/Latour reading.

A previous diary entry ([2026-04-20](../diary/2026-04-20-residuality-philosophy-paper-reading.md)) characterized Deleuze in the 2021 paper as "name-check only." That characterization was correct *for that paper alone* but mis-extrapolated to the corpus. The 2023 paper has substantive Deleuze engagement, and the corpus as a whole operates on a Deleuze-Guattarian conceptual grammar that the 2021 paper's single-line citation does not exhaust.

## 1. Residue as assemblage

In Deleuze and Guattari's vocabulary an *assemblage* (agencement) is a contingent, heterogeneous arrangement of elements — human, technological, conceptual, semiotic — held together by forces of *territorialization* and capable of *deterritorialization* into other arrangements. An assemblage is defined not by what its parts are essentially but by what holds among them, contingently, in a specific situation. Dictionary definitions miss the point: an assemblage is constituted by the relations that obtain *now*, not by the categories of its members.

O'Reilly's residue, as defined in the 2020 *Introduction to Residuality Theory*: "a collection or set of components, infrastructures, people, and information flows — a flow being the transfer of data from one actor in a system to another." (See [2020 §2](../../references/papers/Residuality-Oreilly-2020.md).) The residue is *deliberately* heterogeneous (components, infrastructures, people, flows), *deliberately* under-defined (O'Reilly resists tightening the definition because tightening would reinstate the structuralism he is escaping), and constituted by what holds together under a specific stressor — what *survives* a particular deterritorialization, in D-G's terms.

The mapping is not analogical. It is one-to-one:

| Assemblage (Deleuze-Guattari) | Residue (O'Reilly) |
|---|---|
| Heterogeneous: human + technical + semiotic + material | Heterogeneous: components + infrastructures + people + flows |
| Constituted by what holds *now*, not by essence | Constituted by what survives *this* stressor, not by category |
| Held together by forces of territorialization | Held together by the configuration that survives stress |
| Deterritorializable into other arrangements | Different stressors produce different residues from the same components |
| Resists tight definition; defined extensively, not intensively | "Deliberately open concept... contains anything relevant to the environment and the particular stressor" (2020) |

Two clarifying observations. First, an architecture-as-stack-of-residues — the ontology O'Reilly's 2020 and 2022 papers operate on — is exactly an *assemblage of assemblages*, with the higher-order structure (the stack) itself an assemblage held together by the architect's stressor analysis. This is *Mille Plateaux* assemblage theory at two levels. Second, the residuality theorist's deliberate refusal to taxonomize residues — treating each one as a contingent grouping rather than as an instance of a category — is the methodological discipline that an assemblage-aware reader expects: assemblages defy classification by principle, not by oversight.

The 2023 paper makes this implicit when it says residues "consist only of that which is different between each residue" ([2023 §5](../../references/papers/Residuality-Oreilly-2023.md)). This is the assemblage's *constitutive difference*: each residue is what differs between attractor states, and those differences themselves are what hold the residue together as a unit. The Deleuzian commitment to difference-as-constitutive-of-identity is operative in the residue's own definition, even when Deleuze is not cited.

## 2. The hypernetwork of residues as rhizome

Deleuze and Guattari's rhizome is the alternative to arborescent (tree-like) thinking. Where a tree has a root, hierarchy, and a privileged direction of growth, a rhizome has no root, no hierarchy, no privileged level, and no centerable structure. Multiple entry points, multiple exits, multiplicity rather than dichotomy, decenterability — these are the rhizome's structural commitments. *Mille Plateaux* presents the rhizome as the form that complex, dynamic, non-totalizable structures take.

O'Reilly's 2020 paper, citing Johnson 2014 on hypernetworks: "These residues are sets and collectively can be described as a hypernetwork with the multidimensional links between them restricting and constraining the actions and the form of other residues. It is also possible to model the market, customers, etc. through their stressor relationships and build an overall picture of the system and the broader ecosystem." (See [2020 §2](../../references/papers/Residuality-Oreilly-2020.md).) A hypernetwork has no privileged level (residues themselves are sets that can contain other residues), no root (any residue can be entered from any stressor), and no fixed structure (the hypernetwork changes as stressors are added). It is a rhizome by structural definition.

Three specific commitments make the mapping load-bearing rather than decorative:

**No privileged level.** The 2020 paper says architecture is "multidimensional, each dimension being a residue." Each residue is its own dimension; no residue dominates the others. This is rhizomatic ontology applied to architecture: any residue can be the entry point, any can be the exit, and the architecture is the multiplicity of their interconnections rather than a hierarchy among them. The 2022 paper formalizes this with NKP analysis, where N and K are properties of the network as a whole, not properties of any privileged node.

**Multiple entry points.** O'Reilly's "stack of shadows we cannot see without turning various lights on and off" (2020 §2) is exactly the rhizomatic insight that *no single view of the system suffices*; the architecture is the union of views, none of which is canonical. Different stressors enter the rhizome at different points and reveal different residues. The architect's job is not to find the correct entry point but to walk many entries.

**Decenterability.** The 2020 paper insists that the residual architecture "can also receive new residues at any point in the life cycle and still hold true." A residue can be added without restructuring the existing architecture, because the architecture has no center to disrupt. This is decenterability in D-G's specific sense: the rhizome accepts new lines without reorganizing.

The contrast O'Reilly is drawing in 2021 between the *component metaphor* and *residuality theory* is, structurally, the contrast between arborescent and rhizomatic thinking applied to software architecture. Component-metaphor architecture is arborescent: it has a privileged decomposition (the component tree), a hierarchy (architectural layers), and a center (the system's primary function). Residual architecture is rhizomatic: no privileged decomposition, no fixed hierarchy, no single center. O'Reilly does not name the contrast that way, but the contrast is the same one *Mille Plateaux* makes.

## 3. The architectural walk as nomadic distribution

The third concept is the most subtle and the most directly operationalized in O'Reilly's papers. Deleuze's distinction in *Difference and Repetition* and *A Thousand Plateaus* between *smooth space* and *striated space* — between the desert/sea/steppe traversed by intensities and the city/grid/cadastre traversed by coordinates — gives rise to *nomadic distribution*: motion that follows what surfaces in the terrain rather than what a plan dictates.

The nomad does not occupy fixed coordinates. The nomad moves with the herd, the season, the watering hole — following intensities that the smooth space presents. The sedentary equivalent is the surveyor who imposes a grid first and then moves within it. Deleuze's claim is that complex, dynamic spaces *cannot* be navigated by sedentary methods; they require nomadic distribution.

O'Reilly's *architectural walk*, named explicitly as "Deleuzian" in the 2023 paper and operationally introduced at 31:16 of the NDC Oslo 2024 talk, is nomadic distribution applied to architecture. The 2023 paper:

> "The Deleuzian idea of difference and repetition is best explained through the metaphor of a walk. The first time we take a walk, we experience just one view of the walk. As we take the same walk over time, we notice differences. The walk for us is defined not by that first walk, but the sum of the differences between all the subsequent walks across seasons, years, and changes in paths, buildings, and the surrounding environment."

The walk is intensive, not extensive: each repetition follows what *surfaces* in the terrain — seasonal change, altered paths, new buildings — rather than what a plan dictates. The architecture is constituted by the union of differences across walks, not by a planned traversal. This is nomadic distribution exactly: the architect moves where the differences lead, and the architecture *is* the path the walks have left.

The contrast O'Reilly draws between residuality and conventional architecture is, in *Mille Plateaux* vocabulary, the contrast between nomadic distribution (residuality) and striated occupation (the component metaphor). Conventional architecture imposes a grid first — components, layers, interfaces — and then moves within it. Residual architecture refuses the grid. The architect walks, notices what holds and what fails, and lets the architecture emerge from the residue of those walks.

Three specific consequences for residuality practice that the *Mille Plateaux* vocabulary makes vivid:

**Process-philosophy commitment is nomadic, not just metaphysical.** O'Reilly's 2023 paper insists residuality is process philosophy. *Mille Plateaux* shows what process philosophy *does* in practice: it produces nomadic methods, not just nomadic ontologies. The architectural walk is the practical form of the process commitment. An architect who accepts process philosophy in principle but plans the residuality engagement on a sedentary schedule has not absorbed the lesson.

**Criticality is the test for nomadic survival.** The criticality goal — the system's ability to reorganize across attractor transitions — is exactly the nomad's survival condition: can the assemblage move with the terrain when the terrain shifts? Criticality is not robustness in the engineer's sense (resistance to perturbation); it is *nomadic robustness* (capacity to reterritorialize when the smooth space's intensities reorganize).

**Random stressor analysis is nomadic stressor-finding.** The 2022 paper's deliberate inclusion of impossible and irrelevant stressors is the nomadic move: do not impose a probability grid first; let what surfaces in the terrain inform the walk. The bagging-and-boosting move at the partition level is itself nomadic — it refuses a fixed partition of the stressor space and walks the partitions instead.

## 4. What the convergence shows

These mappings show three things at once.

**Residuality theory is more thoroughly Deleuze-Guattarian than O'Reilly explicitly acknowledges.** The 2023 paper's substantive Deleuze engagement is the surface of a deeper structural commitment. The corpus operates on assemblage-rhizome-nomad even where the vocabulary is absent. This is not a criticism of O'Reilly — the diary 2026-04-20 entry quoted the 2021 paper's note that "the techniques present in residuality theory were discovered through the kind of tinkering espoused by Taleb, and the similarities between residual analysis and Serres' thinking are uncanny, as these methods evolved not from an intellectual framework or knowledge of Serres' work, but from the need to survive and deliver structure in conditions of uncertainty." Independent convergence with Deleuze-Guattari is the epistemic-strengthening pattern, not the weakening one.

**Cyberneutics inherits the same grammar.** The committee deliberation pipeline is itself an assemblage (heterogeneous characters held together by adversarial structure, deterritorializable into different deliberations on different topics); the calibration register is a rhizome (multiple entry points across deliberations, no privileged record, decenterable); the Probe operation is nomadic distribution applied to decision spaces (each run a walk, the decision landscape map the union of differences across walks). The convergence between residuality and cyberneutics under this grammar is not coincidence — both traditions are working in the same conceptual space, recognized through different surface vocabularies.

**Essay-13's spine is now visible.** The graduating essay should not be "cyberneutics and residuality compared" — that frames them as two systems being benchmarked. The spine is sharper: *both arrived at Deleuze-Guattarian architectural commitments through independent practice, and the convergence between them is the operative form of narrative proof at the methodology level*. The two programs cross-validate each other not because they share citations but because they are nomadic about the same terrain.

## 5. What this changes about the existing material

The diary entry [2026-04-20](../diary/2026-04-20-residuality-philosophy-paper-reading.md) characterized the Deleuze engagement in the 2021 *Philosophy* paper as "name-check only." That characterization was accurate for that single paper but did not survey the corpus. The 2023 paper develops Deleuze substantively, and the corpus as a whole operates on Deleuze-Guattarian grammar. The recommendation in that diary — "Essay-13's Deleuze framing has to be revised... O'Reilly's actual post-structural anchors are Serres and Latour" — was 2021-paper-bounded and is corrected here. Deleuze is a primary anchor in the 2023 paper; Serres and Latour are primary anchors in the 2021 paper; the corpus has multiple primary anchors, and essay-13 should reflect that rather than picking one.

The bilateral note [`cyberneutics-and-residuality.md`](cyberneutics-and-residuality.md) §3 currently treats Deleuze briefly under "Other connections" without engaging the assemblage-rhizome-nomad grammar. That section will be updated to point at this note.

The survey [`state-of-residuality-2026.md`](state-of-residuality-2026.md) §9 currently presents processuality/criticality/difference as the 2023 triad and treats the Deleuzian walk in one paragraph. The survey is a third-party presentation of O'Reilly's view and should not import the present note's analytical claim wholesale. But §9 will be updated to give the 2023 paper's Deleuze engagement proper weight — the substantive engagement is in the source text, regardless of which vocabulary frames it.

## 6. Open work

Two threads worth pursuing:

**Stacey under the same grammar.** Stacey's *Complexity and Organizational Reality* — load-bearing for the 2021 *Philosophy* paper, four citations there — has its own complexity-science vocabulary that may or may not map onto the assemblage-rhizome-nomad grammar. **Stacey has been ordered (late April 2026); delivery expected late April / early May 2026.** When the book arrives, a follow-up note will check whether Stacey's three causalities and dominant-discourse critique fit cleanly into the same conceptual frame, or whether they preserve a distinct philosophical line that the present note has flattened. The diary entry [2026-04-20](../diary/2026-04-20-residuality-philosophy-paper-reading.md) flagged the Stacey gap; this note's analysis cannot stand without that confirmation.

**The deterritorialization side of the assemblage.** The present note focuses on residue-as-assemblage and the territorialization that holds it together. *Mille Plateaux* is at least equally interested in *deterritorialization* — the breaking-apart that lets new assemblages form. Residuality theory has an answer here too (the stressor that breaks the residue is the deterritorializing force; the new residue is the reterritorialization), but the 2020–2023 papers do not develop this side as explicitly. Essay-13 might be where deterritorialization gets its proper treatment, and where the framing would matter is in talking about *legacy systems* — the residue whose original territorialization no longer holds and which must be deterritorialized before new architecture can form. This connects directly to the Naur theory-building bridge developed in the 2026-04-25 diary.
