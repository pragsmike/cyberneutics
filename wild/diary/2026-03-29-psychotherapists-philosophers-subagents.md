# Diary: Psychotherapists, Philosophers, and the Recurrence of Sub-Agents

**Date:** 2026-03-29

**Context:** A batch of book acquisitions prompted by the thread from Bradley's distributional compositionality into philosophy — wanting to understand knowledge theories because of what LLMs reveal about the logical structure of language, even at the syntactic level. The acquisitions: Deleuze/Guattari (*Difference and Repetition*, *Anti-Oedipus*, *A Thousand Plateaus*), Segal (*The Dream of Reality*, on Von Foerster), Mill (*Utilitarianism*), Doctorow (on enshittification), and Coles (*The Call of Stories*). Background listening: lectures on Quine's "Two Dogmas of Empiricism."

## The path into philosophy

The motivation is a delightful recursion. Bradley's work on distributional compositionality shows that language has inherent logical structure — literal logical primitives compose at the syntactic level, and the mappings into semantic space reveal this composition. That's mathematics, thinking with a particular flavor. But the questions it raises — what it means for language to have logical structure, what knowledge is, how meaning relates to syntax — are philosophy. The math doesn't answer those questions; it sharpens them. So the inquiry into how language models work leads into epistemology, which leads into reading Deleuze and Mill and Quine, which is new territory.

The math and the philosophy think differently about the same problems. The math builds structures and proves properties. The philosophy questions whether the structures capture what matters. Both are needed. Neither substitutes for the other.

## Coles and the two teachers

Robert Coles's *The Call of Stories* opens with his early psychiatric training, where he encountered two approaches. Most of his teachers rewarded abstraction — taking a patient's thinking and mapping it into theoretical models, clinical jargon, neat diagnostic categories. Coles felt this was losing valuable information: individual nuance, the interplay between a person's specific circumstances and their responses, the texture that doesn't survive categorization.

One teacher — Ludwig — encouraged the opposite: spend time listening to the patient's stories. Let them tell them. Don't rush to classify. Coles also accompanied William Carlos Williams on house calls, where the same principle operated — the physician-poet attending to the particulars of each patient's situation rather than filing them under general headings.

This is Bruner's paradigmatic/narrative distinction in clinical practice, with real consequences. The abstracting teachers were doing paradigmatic cognition — subsumption under categories, generalization, theory-building. Ludwig and Williams were insisting on narrative cognition — attention to the particular, the temporal, the situated. Both are legitimate cognitive modes. Coles's insight was that the institutional training systematically rewarded one and punished the other, losing information in the process.

The fan/funnel architecture is a structural response to exactly this problem. The fan forces multiple perspectives — including narrative, particular, situated ones — to operate on the same material. The funnel forces synthesis. Neither mode colonizes the other.

## The recurrence of psychotherapists

A pattern in recent reading: Coles was a psychotherapist. Guattari was a psychotherapist (La Borde clinic). Von Foerster's later work had close associations with therapeutic practice. Freud, obviously, developed his theories of mind from cases in his own clinical practice.

The recurrence is not accidental. Psychotherapists sit at exactly the intersection of theory and lived experience — they must have models of how minds work, but they must also deal with actual humans who refuse to fit the models. That tension is productive. It forces a kind of thinking that pure theorists and pure empiricists can both avoid.

This also explains why so many of the thinkers relevant to cyberneutics come from or through therapeutic practice. The project is about structured thinking under uncertainty, with explicit attention to how reasoning can go wrong. That's close to what a good therapist does: attend to the process of thinking, not just its content.

## Freud's closed loop and Minsky's society

A question that has lingered: why did practically a whole branch of psychology follow Freud almost cultishly? With mathematics, there's close provable reasoning. With physics, experimental checks on theory. With Freud and Jung, little chance of falsifiability. The theories seemed no better grounded than some of Aristotle's more speculative flights.

The answer is probably that the cultishness was partly *because of* the unfalsifiability, not despite it. Psychoanalysis offered a complete hermeneutic system: any observation could be interpreted within the framework, and resistance to the framework was itself interpretable as evidence for it. A closed epistemic loop. That's enormously attractive if you're a practitioner facing irreducible uncertainty about other minds every day. It gives you something to do.

The competition confirms this. Behaviorism had falsifiability and produced genuine science, but at the cost of refusing to talk about inner experience, meaning, narrative — exactly the things clinicians needed to address. So the field split between rigor-with-impoverishment and richness-with-unfalsifiability. Freud won the clinical world because clinicians needed the richness.

But the structural insight underneath Freud's specific (and mostly embarrassing) mechanisms has held up: the id/ego/superego model — competing drives with different objectives, mediated by something that has to produce coherent output — keeps getting rediscovered. Minsky's society of mind is explicitly a committee of specialists with no single seat of consciousness. The cyberneutics committee pipeline does something similar: interacting sub-agents whose collective output has properties none of them individually possess. The crucial difference is that the committee makes no claims about the agents' sentience or intelligence. They perform ersatz reasoning imposed by probabilistically driven language structure. The intelligent behavior, if it appears, is emergent from the interaction under structural constraints — fan/funnel, forced disagreement, synthesis — not from any individual agent's capacity.

The question of whether the sub-agents "really" reason is dissolved rather than answered: bisimulation over ontology. If the system behaves as if these components interact to produce structured deliberation, that's what matters for the purpose of generating inspectable reasoning records. Freud's error wasn't the architecture — it was reifying the components and building an unfalsifiable interpretive apparatus around them.

## Deleuze/Guattari connection

Only early impressions, but the resonance with existing project themes is already visible. Deleuze's emphasis on difference-in-itself rather than difference-from-a-norm, and the rhizome/arborescent distinction in *A Thousand Plateaus*, map onto the star-shaped vs. chain-shaped entailment mesh topology. Their resistance to overcoding and stratification — to premature theoretical closure — is the same concern that motivates the fan stage: force the exploration before allowing convergence.

Guattari's therapeutic background connects back to Coles. Both resist the impulse to reduce lived experience to theoretical models. Deleuze and Guattari's rejection of representational thinking — don't strip the essence into reduced models, attend to the actual flows and connections — is philosophically grounded where Coles's is clinically grounded, but the instinct is the same.

## Quine's "Two Dogmas" and the analytic/synthetic collapse

Background listening, not yet deeply engaged. But the relevance is clear: Quine's attack on the analytic/synthetic distinction undermines exactly the kind of neat categorical boundaries that Coles's abstracting teachers relied on, and that Deleuze/Guattari reject. If the boundary between truths of meaning and truths of fact is not sharp, then the pigeonholing of patients (or texts, or arguments) into clean theoretical categories is doing more violence to the material than it appears.

This connects to furry logic — texts fully inhabiting multiple types simultaneously rather than being graded members of a single type. If the analytic/synthetic distinction is blurred, then so is the boundary between types, and the interesting question is how a text can be *fully* a member of multiple types at once rather than being partially a member of one.
