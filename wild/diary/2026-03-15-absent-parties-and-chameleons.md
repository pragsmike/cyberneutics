# Diary: Absent Parties and Chameleons on Mirrors

**2026-03-15**

---

## The thread

Started from a simple observation: if communication arises from interaction (Pask), what happens when one party can't talk back? The canonical cases are nuclear waste warnings (WIPP, Onkalo — messages that must survive 10,000 years without feedback), decipherment of dead scripts (Linear B, Voynich manuscript, Indus Valley), SETI, and information cascades where the originator is structurally absent from downstream interpretation. Less obvious but structurally identical: a promise is communication with an absent party — your future self, or the future state of a relationship. You encode intent into a structure and hope the future interpretive community decodes it faithfully.

Ran a deep research prompt through Perplexity and got a 41-page synthesis covering eight domains. The taxonomy of absence it produced is useful: temporal (deep time), spatial (interstellar), epistemic (non-overlapping frameworks), structural (cascade/propagation), biological (cross-generational). These aren't cleanly separable — nuclear waste warning involves temporal, epistemic, and structural absence simultaneously — but the decomposition clarifies which compensatory strategies apply where.

## What Pask would have said about WIPP

The WIPP designers were solving a transmission problem: encode a message clearly enough that any future decoder can recover it. Pask would have said the framing was wrong. Understanding isn't recovered — it's constructed through interaction. A message without a conversational partner isn't a message; it's an artifact. The Sandia team's hostile architecture proposals (Landscape of Thorns, Menacing Earthworks) were attempts to build a conversation partner out of landscape — something that provokes a response in the visitor, which is the closest you can get to conversation when one party is 10,000 years dead.

The "ray cats" proposal (Bastide and Fabbri, 1982 — breed cats that change color near radiation, embed this in folklore) is the most Paskian solution in the HITF literature: it doesn't encode a message directly, it builds a living interpretive community that carries meaning forward through interaction. The message survives as cultural practice, not static encoding — which is how entailment meshes persist, through ongoing teachback between participants.

Pask's predicted failure mode: any static encoding degrades without feedback to correct misinterpretation. The warning becomes an invitation. Egyptian tombs demonstrated this empirically — "death to any who enter" became "treasure inside."

## Conversation theory's productive boundary

CT defines the absent-party case as its hard boundary: "If there were no conversation, there would be no CT observables." But the boundary is productive, not just limiting. It defines the space where feedforward-only communication must operate. The sender must embed enough structure in the artifact to seed a new conversational process in a future interpreter.

Bernard Scott's distinction between "proto-conversation" (partially formed, asymmetric exchanges) and "null conversation" (complete absence of feedback) is useful. Scott recognizes that artifacts produced by a conversant can function as "frozen" entailment structures — incomplete but sufficient to seed reconstruction.

Glanville's second-order move: the absent party's "presence" is constituted by the interpreter's act of interpretation. The conversation doesn't halt when one party disappears — it becomes a second-order conversation between the interpreter and their own model of the absent other. This reframes the problem from deficit (missing feedback) to constitutive condition (the interpreter's creativity is required to complete the circuit).

## The pipeline/bath mapping

This maps cleanly onto the pipeline/bath distinction. Pipeline assumes a controlled channel with feedback — you trust the transformations because you can verify them. Bath is unprovenanced text requiring external judgment. Absent-party communication is the extreme bath case: no provenance, no feedback, no sender to interrogate.

The calibration register hits a fundamental limit here. Meta-d' requires ground truth feedback to measure the gap between sensitivity and metacognitive sensitivity. In the absent-party case, there is no ground truth. The register must track internal consistency rather than correspondence — which, as it turns out, is what all hermeneutics has always done. Gadamer's "fusion of horizons" when one horizon is frozen in time is exactly the calibration register operating without ground truth.

## Deliberately engineered absence — a gap in the literature

The Perplexity report treats structural absence (Section 7) as something that happens through propagation dynamics — the sender's identity gets stripped away as information cascades through a network. But there's a case it doesn't cover: deliberately engineered absence as adversarial strategy.

Disinformation campaigns often involve an originator who absents themselves so the target community must process the message without a sender to interrogate. This is the Glenda/Crock problem — the attacker's absence is strategic, not incidental. The message arrives as bath (unprovenanced, no return address) precisely because the attacker designed it that way. The "seams" in the mesh-rewiring attack are temporal and structural rather than intentional — the attack succeeds precisely to the extent that the absence looks incidental rather than engineered.

Nobody in the nuclear semiotics, SETI, or cascade dynamics literature seems to treat the adversary's self-absenting as a formal category distinct from incidental absence. This is a potential contribution point connecting the narrative immune systems research program to the absent-party synthesis.

Luhmann gets closest: his three-selection model treats the sender as structurally "in the environment" of the communication system — communication selects information, utterance, and understanding independently of any individual sender's intention. In this framework, the absent sender is the normal condition. That's a stronger claim than Derrida's iterability (signs function in the sender's absence) because it's operational rather than deconstructive. For cyberneutics purposes, Luhmann may be more useful than Derrida here — the claim is about system architecture, not about the metaphysics of presence.

## Promises as absent-party communication

A promise attempts to freeze one side of a conversation — "I will do X" — but conversations are what unfreezes things. The promise tries to be pipeline (controlled, typed, trusted) in what is actually a bath (uncontrolled future context). That's why promises feel like lies in retrospect: not because the promiser was dishonest, but because the encoding couldn't survive the interpretive drift.

Is there a calibrated promise? One that carries its own uncertainty estimate: "I intend to do X, and here's my historically observed follow-through rate on commitments of this type." Nobody talks like that, but institutions approximate it: credit scores, track records, reputation systems. These are calibration registers for promises — meta-d' applied to commitment reliability.

The promise is the everyday case of the absent-party problem and might be the most intuitive entry point for explaining why static encodings degrade without feedback.

## The Colloquy of Mobiles — Pask's machine-machine conversation

Went looking for whether Pask ever set up pure machine-machine conversations. Found the Colloquy of Mobiles (1968), shown at Cybernetic Serendipity at the ICA London. ZKM calls it "the first example of machines conversing with machines."

Five computer-controlled mobiles: two "males," three "females," suspended from the ceiling. They communicated via light and sound autonomously, with no human intervention required. Males could project beams of light but couldn't satisfy their own drive to have light play on their periphery. Females could reflect light but couldn't generate it. Complementary incompleteness forced cooperation — neither type could achieve satisfaction alone. The mobiles learned to optimize behavior, reaching satisfaction states with minimum energy. Visitors could use flashlights and mirrors to enter the conversation, but were optional.

This matters for the committee design. Pask solved a fundamental problem: what color is a chameleon on a mirror? Two identical adaptive systems facing each other either converge on the same attractor, oscillate, or deadlock. No ground truth to stabilize on. Pask's solution was asymmetry — complementary rather than identical agents. Males emit, females reflect. Different capability profiles create a productive interaction space.

The cyberneutics committee's propensity system is the same structural move. If you run N identical LLM instances with identical prompts, you get the chameleon-mirror problem. Different worldview lenses per character is Pask's male/female asymmetry: functional incompleteness in different dimensions forces genuine interaction rather than mimetic convergence.

## Bisimulation dissolves the "inner life" question

The question "do the committee characters really have needs/drives/propensities?" turns out to be ill-formed. Two systems are bisimilar if every observable transition one can make, the other can match, recursively. You don't need to open the box. If a committee character produces responses observationally indistinguishable from what an entity with a given propensity would produce — challenges the right claims, asks the right follow-ups, updates in the right direction — then for the purposes of the pipeline's function, it has that propensity.

Pask would have agreed. He designed the Colloquy with a "sexual analogy" (his term) as a design decision about observable complementarity, not a claim about machine desire. The mobiles didn't "want" satisfaction — they were specified such that certain states reduced activity and others increased it. From outside, that looks like wanting. From inside, it's a control loop. The difference is underdetermined by observation — which means, by Bateson's criterion ("a difference that makes a difference"), it carries no information.

This cleans up the committee-as-open-game formulation. Propensity is a constraint on the observable transition function, not a psychological claim. It restricts which moves a character can make in the game. The selection function in the open game formalism doesn't ask why a strategy was chosen, only which strategies are available and how payoffs propagate.

## Bisimulation meets the absent party

In the contemporaneous committee case, you can check bisimulation — you can observe all parties in real time and detect when a character drifts out of its specified propensity. Bisimulation failure is measurable degradation, and the calibration register can track it.

In the absent-party case, you can't observe the other system's transitions at all. You're left with artifacts and must infer a transition system that could have produced them. Decipherment is constructing a bisimulation partner for a system you can never observe directly. Kober building her Linear B triplets was constructing a hypothetical transition system bisimilar to whatever the Minoans were doing when they wrote.

The categorical formulation: bisimulation is a relation on objects in a category of labeled transition systems. The committee characters are objects; propensity defines available morphisms; the calibration register tracks consistency of observed morphisms with specified propensity. When an absent party's transition system is unobservable, you work from trace alone — reconstructing the object up to bisimilarity from its output sequence.

## Connection to Anamaria Berea's work

The Perplexity report's taxonomy of absence maps remarkably well onto a single researcher's empirical program. Berea's work spans: literary genome / Voynich manuscript (epistemic absence), ASPIRE ONE lunar archive (temporal absence), DARPA SocialSim (structural absence), technosignatures / SETI (spatial + epistemic absence), biological communication (biological absence). She may be the only researcher whose empirical work touches all five modalities.

Her Folger Shakespeare Library project — NLP on Renaissance and Early Modern texts, including apparently the Voynich manuscript — is decipherment-as-inverse-hermeneutics applied computationally. The ASPIRE ONE lunar archive (landed on the Moon, March 2025) is the forward-looking version of the WIPP problem: encoding cultural heritage for unknown future interpreters across a vast epistemic gap. The "Message in a Bottle" paper (Jiang, Berea et al., 2023) explicitly states: "Given the absence of mutually understood signs, symbols, and semiotic conventions, MIAB seeks to use scientific methods to design an innovative means of communication."

Her 2025 Space Policy paper ("Towards a Generalized Framework for Planetary Communication") is the most explicit articulation of a unified treatment of the absent-party problem. She's been working on this from five directions without necessarily framing it as a single problem. The absent-party problem as unifying thread is a framing that cyberneutics can offer.

Key bridge points: the committee as a reconstructed-perspective engine (constructing an adversarial voice for the absent party), the calibration register as an internal-consistency tracker when ground truth is unavailable, and furry logic as measurement-based type assignment (exactly what decipherers do — "this glyph probably maps to this phoneme" is a rubric-scored type assignment with calibrated uncertainty).

## Brier's cybersemiotics — candidate integrative framework

The report surfaces Søren Brier's cybersemiotics as an attempt to integrate Shannon's information theory, Peircean semiotics, Pask's conversation theory, Luhmann's autopoiesis, and Barbieri's code biology into a five-level model. Haven't read Brier. The claim that neither information theory nor semiotics alone can account for communication echoes cyberneutics' position that neither Shannon-style channel models nor single-LLM generation produces trustworthy reasoning. Worth investigating whether Brier's framework provides formal resources the palgebra doesn't already cover.

## Five theoretical gaps (from the report)

1. No Paskian treatment of absent-party decipherment — entailment mesh reconstruction is what decipherers do, but nobody's made the connection explicit
2. No systematic cybernetic framing of nuclear semiotics — Sebeok drew on Shannon/Wiener but not von Foerster/Pask/Maturana
3. Incomplete bridge between biosemiotics and information cascade theory — both deal with absent sources but use disjoint vocabularies
4. Berea's work needs explicit grounding in second-order cybernetics — she arrived at Paskian conclusions from economics/complexity without calling out the tradition
5. The Derrida-cybernetics connection remains undertheorized despite Stiegler — iterability and feedforward address the same structural problem from different traditions

Plus the gap I'd add: deliberately engineered absence as adversarial strategy, distinct from incidental structural absence.
