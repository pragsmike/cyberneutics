# Diary: Fan-Funnel as Residuality Move, Purpose as Relation, and the Naming Calculus

**Date:** 2026-05-01

The walk pattern is now visible in retrospect. Each next book selected by what the previous made visible. Not optimizing, not following a syllabus. The idealized syllabus was always a fiction; the Deleuzian walk is the fact. This applies to the reading and it applies to engineering.

## Fan-funnel is enacting a residuality move

This is the load-bearing recognition.

Residuality says: don't specify the system up front and harden it against anticipated failures. Generate the stressors — the shocks the system might encounter — and let identity be what survives them. The system is the residue of the stressor field, not a thing-prior-to-stressors that then encounters them.

Fan-funnel says: don't reason from the situation to the decision in one move. Generate scenarios — the divergent futures the situation might unfold into — and let the decision be what survives deliberation across them. The decision is the residue of the scenario field, not a thing-prior-to-scenarios that then gets validated against them.

Same shape. The fan is the stressor generator. The funnel is the survival test. The deliberated choice is the residue.

Both refuse identity-prior-to-encounter. Both refuse to start with a specified entity (the system, the decision) that then encounters its environment (stressors, scenarios). Both say: the entity is constituted by the encounter. You don't have a system that is then stressed; you have a stressor field whose residue we call the system. You don't have a decision that is then tested; you have a scenario field whose residue we call the decision.

### What this adds to material already in the repo

The repo already says (in [`cyberneutics-and-residuality.md`](../residuality-theory/cyberneutics-and-residuality.md) §2.4) that fan-funnel *generalizes* residuality's two-step algorithm to sense-making, and ([`fan-as-stressor-generator.md`](../residuality-theory/fan-as-stressor-generator.md)) that the fan can serve residuality's stressor-generation step upstream. Both framings are correct and stand. Today's recognition is at a different layer: the *anti-identity grammar* that ties fan-funnel to residuality is the same grammar driving the day's other recognitions. Purpose-is-not-property. Boundary-is-not-feature-of-territory. Decision-is-not-a-thing-the-deliberation-validates. One grammar across multiple layers.

Naming the grammar matters because it explains why fan-funnel and residuality compose rather than compete. They are the same move at different scales.

### Sharpening the black-swan evaluation result

The black-swan evaluation showed that the committee did not demonstrably outperform solo on outcome quality. This has been held honestly — the inspectable-reasoning-records framing was already the position before the result came in, and the result was treated as confirming a positioning rather than damaging it. Today's recognition supplies the structural account.

Outcome quality is a property claim. *Was the decision right?* requires a context-free notion of rightness that property-thinking supplies and relation-thinking refuses. Residuality methodologies — and fan-funnel as one — produce relation-quality outputs. *This is what survived this stressor/scenario field given these participants and these stakes.* Run the fan again with different scenarios, you get a different residue. The output is constituted by the field it survived, not pre-existing to it.

The right metric isn't *did the committee get the right answer*. It's *did the deliberation engage the stressors that turned out to matter*. That's a question the inspectable reasoning record actually supports. *Was the verdict correct* often isn't a well-posed question for the regime fan-funnel was built for.

### Positioning consequence

Cyberneutics is a residuality-style methodology for decisions under genuine uncertainty. Not a competitor to decision-quality methodologies — a different methodology for a different regime, where decision-quality methodologies make a category error by assuming a stable criterion. The criterion isn't stable in the wicked-problem regime. That's why it's a wicked problem.

This is a cleaner positioning argument than has been used in the practitioner-facing material. Worth surfacing in essay 10 or wherever the decision-theory framing sits.

## Purpose is a relation, not a property

This wants its own working note in `wild/relations-not-properties/` or extension of an existing thread; capturing the recognition here while it's fresh.

Pirsig's quality. Gibson's affordances. Paperclip-as-router-reset. The paperclip didn't gain a property when routers acquired pinholes; the world gained a relation. Affordances are *relations between* an animal and its environment, not features of either alone. A chair affords sitting to a creature with that body and those joints. To a beetle, it is a cliff.

Meadows's *the purpose of a system is what it does* is useful as a corrective against stated-purpose hypocrisy ("our purpose is excellence" while the system reliably produces mediocrity). But it smuggles teleology back in through the side door. The system *does* what it does. Calling that doing a *purpose* is already an observer move, already a relation between the doing and someone for whom the doing matters. Once purpose is treated as a property, you can optimize it, engineer for it, write Harvard Business Review articles about aligning with it. You can also be catastrophically wrong about it without any feedback that you're wrong, because property-claims don't get corrected by the world the way relation-claims do.

O'Reilly's critique of Forrester and Meadows applying industrial dynamics to social systems is the same critique. Industrial dynamics worked in factories because the relations were institutionally locked down. The plant exists to make widgets. Workers exist to operate machines. The *purpose* could be modeled as a property because the relations had been frozen by institutional fact. Export the frame to a city, an economy, a watershed — relations no longer locked, and the property-style modeling fails predictably.

D&G's industry/nature distinction names the same observation in their vocabulary. Industry is the territorialization that fixes flows into channels with stated purposes. Nature, in their sense, is the deterritorializing flow that keeps escaping the channels, finding new uses, new connections, new lines of flight. The paperclip becoming a router-reset is a tiny line of flight. Capitalism, on their reading, is unusual in being an industry that *runs on* deterritorialization — it keeps breaking its own channels to make new ones — which is why it is so corrosive to traditional forms and so good at finding what previously locked-down materials can newly afford.

The structural pattern: from *we drew this boundary for our purposes* to *the boundary is a feature of the territory*. From *for these observers under these conditions* to *intrinsically*. The same slip Meadows makes about purpose. The same slip the explainability-demanders made about LLMs (asking for inspectable production rules from a pile of linear algebra). The same slip the component-architects make about systems (treating the joints as clean when they aren't).

The cyberneutics framing has been careful about this in places (inspectable reasoning records as deliverable, not better decisions as deliverable) and could be more careful in others. The moment someone writes "the purpose of cyberneutics is to produce better decisions," the relation-not-property discipline has been lost.

## The system/environment boundary as artifice of control

This may be subordinate to the purpose-as-relation point or its own thread; recording it here, undecided.

The system/environment distinction is drawn by an observer, not found in nature. Von Foerster says this; Maturana and Varela say it from biology. The cell membrane looks like a clean boundary until one notices the membrane is actively constructed by the cell from materials it pulls across the boundary it is supposedly defining. The boundary is an ongoing achievement, not a given.

The sharper version of this — that the boundary is *an artifice of control* — locates the observer concretely. The control engineer draws the boundary at the place where their actuators stop having authority. Inside that boundary, the model is law because we wrote the law. Outside, hopes and disturbance rejection. The boundary moves when authority moves. Bigger budget, better sensor, new contract with a vendor — the boundary shifts. It was never a feature of the world.

Control theory's *internal model principle* makes this operational: to regulate against a disturbance, the controller must contain a model of the disturbance's dynamics. The "outside" leaks in as a model on the inside. The boundary is permeable by construction, because controlling across it requires importing it. The cleanly separated environment is exactly what is unavailable if action is intended.

This connects to Rosen's modeling relation as already developed in the 04-26 diary. *Adequacy is never proven, only tested.* And the criterion of adequacy — *good enough for what, for whom, under what failure costs* — is not a technical question. It is a stakeholder question. Engineering as social activity, not just technical, because the boundary-drawing and the criterion-setting are both political acts.

This loops to the staff-teaching observation. Engineering is a series of model transformations: analysis (problem space, what is the environment, what is the problem) to design (solution space, what can we build, science of the artificial) to code, with intermediate models. But those are only parts of the larger situation. We are deliberately crafting assemblages in the midst of other assemblages, and everything we do changes what is, and we start over, even if we don't write it down that way. NASA-style systems engineering with stakeholders, stake drivers, operators, phone answerers, and the movie-credits-scrolling-for-four-minutes scale is the realistic frame.

## Things are their own context

Another point on the narrative-proof pile. Adding to [`2026-04-03-narrative-proof.md`](2026-04-03-narrative-proof.md).

The electron cannot outrun its own self-field. Classical electrodynamics had to add the radiation reaction back in by hand (Abraham–Lorentz, then Dirac's relativistic version) because an accelerating charge that doesn't push back on itself is mathematically unviable. The electron is always already in conversation with the trail it leaves. That's how light works — the electromagnetic field is what the charges do *and* what they respond to, simultaneously, with no clean separation.

Same shape as von Foerster's eigenforms — the operator whose fixed point is itself, the object that is its own context. Same shape as Zurek's pointer states — stable because the environment selects them, not because they are intrinsically stable. Same shape as Bradley's enriched categories of language — meanings stabilize through the company they keep. Same shape as Deleuze's virtual-to-actual — differentiation produces the actual through the relations, not from a prior identity. Same shape as Faulkner refusing the omniscient narrator because the events *are* the multiple tellings, not a thing the tellings approximate.

Five traditions, no obvious mutual influence, same observation. You cannot out-context context. The physical universe doesn't have a context-free layer underneath. It is relations all the way down.

## The naming calculus, and what LLM companionship may shift

A pending decision rather than a finished observation.

O'Reilly uses concepts like assemblage from D&G without using the names. Same with writers using category theory without naming it; one in the recent reading admitted parenthetically. The strategic move pre-LLM was clear: name-recoil cost too high for mixed audiences. *Assemblage* in a software engineering paper makes one cohort nod knowingly and another close the tab. *Functor* in a programming essay does the same in reverse.

LLM companionship lowers the friction of vocabulary acquisition. A reader who hits an unfamiliar term and can ask their companion *what does this mean in the context of what I already know about X* is a different reader from one who had to put the book down and pick up another book. The activation energy for cross-domain recognition has dropped at conversational speed.

But the same mechanism makes faking engagement easier. A reader can have *assemblage* explained twenty times and never have it become operational, because explanation is not internalization. The two reader-types — those who use the LLM to metabolize, those who use it to substitute for metabolizing — diverge more visibly than before. Naming gets cheaper *and* shallower simultaneously.

For cyberneutics specifically: keep the two-register architecture (practitioner / lineage) but stop being shy about the lineage register. The cost of naming has dropped; the benefit (extensibility, network legibility, contributors finding the larger conversation) has not, and may have risen. A practitioner with an LLM companion can handle *this is Deleuze, here is why it matters here* in a way they couldn't fifteen years ago. *Open question (raised 2026-05-01): when and how to surface the lineage more openly in the practitioner-facing material.*

The lament worth recording: the missed-opportunity cost when audience lacks pattern vocabulary. Once you know what an *assemblage* is, the word conveys a raft of entailments — the whole Pask mesh activates from a single token. Design patterns in programming aimed at this and partially succeeded; what they delivered always had an ad-hoc, parochial feel — they wouldn't always compose. Category theory is, on a working description, a set of provably coherent, composable, universal design patterns. Naming the lineage is offering the mesh.

## The syllabus that never gets smaller

Pre-LLM, a self-directed scholar's reading list grew slowly because each new entry came from a citation, a recommendation, a footnote chased down. Post-LLM, the list grows at conversational speed because every exchange surfaces three new authors, two new fields, four new connections.

The pre-LLM limit was *not knowing what existed*. The post-LLM limit is *knowing what exists and not having time*. The second is more honest, harder to live with. The wild/ structure and slow reading are the engineering response — local, working, but not solving the cultural version of the problem.

The discipline is to use the LLM as a *companion to slow reading*, not a substitute for it. Still working through Rid, Coles, Cilliers, Taleb, Deleuze. The conversations situate the books, recognize when something read three weeks ago in one tradition shows up in another tradition being read now, and notice the convergences that make narrative proof possible. The reader who lets the LLM do the reading for them gets the surface and misses the substance. The reader who reads with the LLM alongside gets further than they could have alone.

This is the operating regime, not a temporary backlog. Worth naming so future readers of the diary know that's the condition.

## Clojure as reference point

Note-stub for later expansion. The Clojure community is the least stultified of the programming communities encountered. Most communities stultify because the language ships with a metaphysics — Java has classes-and-hierarchies, Haskell has types-as-propositions, Python has there-should-be-one-obvious-way — and the metaphysics produces orthodoxy. Clojure shipped with Hickey's anti-metaphysical metaphysics: data is data, functions transform data, complect at your peril. *Simple Made Easy*, *Hammock Driven Development*, *Are We There Yet*. The community treats thinking as the work and code as the residue. That is Naur. That is cyberneutics. That is the same insight found independently in a different domain. Another point on the narrative-proof pile.

The Cult Repo Clojure documentary is in the to-watch queue. Worth a quiet evening when one materializes. Add to a communities-that-got-it-right list when that file exists.
