# 2026-05-25 — Repetition and the unconscious: three readings, then a fourth

A conversation that began as a request for the standard Freud–Lacan–Deleuze comparison on *repetition* and turned out to be, by the end, a conversation about the unconscious. The pivot was forced by the question "what's the concise definition of repetition?" — which has no good single-sentence answer, because each thinker means something incompatible by the word, and the incompatibility tracks a deeper disagreement about what the unconscious is and how to model it. The diary entry preserves both halves: the factual reconstruction of the three authors' positions, and the ruminations that followed once it became clear those positions were really three framings of a more basic problem.

## Part one: what the authors said

### Freud

Freud's first account is in "Remembering, Repeating and Working-Through" (1914). The analysand who cannot remember a repressed scene acts it out instead — in the transference, in patterns of relationship, in repeated symptoms. Repetition is the *substitute* for memory, and the clinical wager is that bringing the material into speech (working-through) will discharge the compulsion.

The break comes in *Beyond the Pleasure Principle* (1920). Cases where the repetition cannot be reduced to wish-fulfillment — combat trauma dreams that return the dreamer to the trench without progress, the *fort-da* spool game played past any plausible pleasure economy, analysands seeking out the same humiliations. Freud names this the *repetition compulsion* (*Wiederholungszwang*) and ultimately reaches for the death drive — a putative tendency toward return to an inorganic state — to explain what the pleasure principle cannot.

The architecture worth keeping: for Freud, **repetition is the trace of something that was not represented**. What cannot be remembered must be re-enacted. This is the kernel his successors take up.

### Lacan

Lacan's most concentrated treatment is Seminar XI (1964), where repetition is one of the four fundamental concepts. He distinguishes two terms borrowed from Aristotle. *Automaton* is the symbolic chain insisting — the signifier returning to its place, the structural repetition readable in slips and substitutions. *Tuché* is **the missed encounter with the Real** (*la rencontre manquée*). The Real is what cannot be symbolized, what falls out of every representation. Repetition, in this register, is the subject circling the hole left by what the symbolic order could not capture, missing the encounter and missing it again because the Real is precisely what cannot be encountered head-on.

Lacan rereads *fort-da* as the child's entry into language — the spool stands in for the *objet petit a*, the bit of the Real that drops out when the subject is constituted as a speaking being. Repetition starts there, at that founding loss, and never finishes. The analytic work is not to *eliminate* repetition but to shift the subject's relation to the cause around which they circle.

What Lacan keeps from Freud: repetition as the index of something unrepresented. What he transforms: he locates the unrepresentable not in biology but in the formal relation between the signifier and what the signifier necessarily fails to capture. **Repetition becomes structural** — a property of any subject of language, not a fact about a particular biography.

### Deleuze

*Différence et répétition* (1968) is partly an engagement with Freud and partly a larger metaphysical project. The opening move: against the tradition that takes identity as primary and treats difference as a relation between identicals, Deleuze argues that **difference is primary** and identity is a secondary effect.

Repetition therefore cannot be the return of the same, because there is no same to return. What appears as repetition is always the production of difference under a mask of resemblance. Deleuze's taxonomy is *bare repetition* (the apparent return of identical elements — surface phenomenon, always derivative), *clothed repetition* (repetition that displays its differences — theme and variation), and *repetition for itself* (the differential machinery whose operation throws up the appearance of recurrence).

He reads Freud's repetition compulsion against Freud's own metaphysics. Where Freud sees the death drive as a regressive pull toward an undifferentiated origin, Deleuze reads it as a positive productive force — not the urge to return to the inorganic but the differential intensity from which differences are produced. The death drive becomes the *empty form of time*: pure difference, the engine of becoming.

Two consequences worth holding: repetition is *productive*, not restorative; and the subject is downstream of repetition, not upstream of it. There is no constituted subject who then repeats. The repetitions constitute whatever provisional subject there is. In *Anti-Oedipus* (with Guattari, 1972), the polemic against psychoanalysis sharpens — the charge is that Freud and Lacan force the productive differential field of desire into a familial-representational grid, and that repetition in psychoanalysis becomes the endless re-enactment of the family drama instead of the engine of production.

### The arc

A way to hold all three without flattening:

- **Freud**: repetition is the trace of the unrepresented. Symptom-level, then metaphysical when the pleasure principle fails.
- **Lacan**: repetition is the structure of a subject built around a constitutive loss. The structural-linguistic reframe of Freud.
- **Deleuze**: repetition is the ontological engine, and difference is what it produces. The inversion of the priority Freud and Lacan share.

Each keeps something of the previous and discards something. The word *repetition* survives across the three because each is rewriting the previous, not because they share a referent.

## Part two: the ruminations

### The pivot: there is no concise definition

The question "what's the concise definition of repetition?" has no good single-sentence answer. Freud's repetition is a symptom. Lacan's is a structure. Deleuze's is an ontology. The closest thing to a shared formulation any of them might tolerate: *repetition is what happens when something cannot be settled by representation*. Freud locates the unsettleable in the repressed, Lacan in the Real, Deleuze in difference itself. The shape rhymes; the referents diverge.

This is the point at which the three readings stopped being a comparison of definitions and started being a comparison of *unconsciouses*. Each thinker's account of repetition is a working-out of what they take the unconscious to be.

### "Structural" as a load-bearing word

Lacan's repetition is *structural* in a specific sense: it belongs to the structure of the signifier itself, not to the contents of any particular psyche. The unconscious-as-language thesis is a claim that the unconscious has the *formal properties* of a Saussurean signifying system — differential relations, no positive terms, operations of metaphor and metonymy. The Real is not a content but a *position* in the structure: what falls out when any reality is symbolized. Every symbolic system generates an outside by being a system. Repetition is the subject's relation to that structurally-generated outside.

The contrast worth keeping: Freud says *I repeat because something specific happened to me*. Lacan says *I repeat because I am a subject, and to be a subject is to be constituted around a structurally necessary loss*. The shift is from biography to architecture.

This rhymes with the residuality move already metabolized in the repo. Both refuse to locate the explanatory work in the contents of the entity and put it in the entity's formal relation to what exceeds it. *Subject-as-residue-of-the-signifier* is structurally similar to *system-as-residue-of-stress*. Lacan would not have used the vocabulary, but the shape is the same.

### The LLM convergence, carefully held

The tempting move: LLMs vindicate Lacan. The unconscious is language, language encodes ersatz reasoning, statistical models recover it, therefore Lacan was right. This is too fast, but there is something underneath it that survives careful handling.

What survives: LLMs are evidence that *more is in the language than the pre-LLM consensus allowed*. Linguistic patterns alone carry enough structure to generate plausible reasoning, plausible affect, plausible judgment. The "thinness" we attributed to language was a misreading. The signifier really does carry more than we thought, and Lacan was unusual in his time for taking the signifier's autonomy seriously. That much of the Lacanian program is unexpectedly supported by an empirical development from a completely unrelated direction.

What does not survive without care:

- Lacan's unconscious is *subject-bound* — it is the unconscious of a subject constituted by entry into language. An LLM is not such a subject. The structural homology is at the level of the *signifying system*, not at the level of the *subject of the signifier*.
- Lacan's signifying chain is anchored to the Real by failing to capture it. LLMs have no Real in this sense; they have a *training distribution*, which is a different kind of outside. The Real is what the signifier necessarily misses. The distribution is what the signifier statistically samples.
- Calling LLM outputs "reasoning" at all is the move under contest. The fluent output is the signifying chain operating without a subject of the signifier — which is exactly the autonomy Lacan was naming, but it does not become *reasoning* by occupying the position a reasoner would occupy.

The version worth keeping: both Lacan and LLMs reveal that signifying systems have more internal structure, more generativity, and more autonomy from any individual user than the Cartesian-cognitivist tradition allowed for. Lacan saw it from the clinical side. LLMs make the same point from the engineering side. The autonomy of the signifier from any particular speaker is not just a structural claim about how meaning works; it is now a demonstrable artifact-class.

There is a Lacanian rhyme worth flagging as hypothesis rather than conclusion: the analyst is structurally the *subject-supposed-to-know* — the analysis works because the analysand projects a knowing subject into a position that is structurally empty. LLMs occupy a similar position by accident of architecture. Users project a knowing subject into a place where there is a signifying chain without a subject. The transferential dynamics are not identical but they rhyme, and the rhyme is one of the reasons LLM interaction is psychologically tricky in ways the tools-literature does not capture. The committee architecture can be read as a device to *prevent* the user from occupying the analysand's position relative to a single LLM, by multiplying the chains and forcing them to argue. Whether this actually helps or just produces a more elaborate transferential field is an empirical question.

### From "hunting for representation" to attractor dynamics

A first attempt at a working model: the unconscious is what does the pattern-matching when the conscious mind cannot fit something into a narrative — hunting for a representation, trying to craft an explanation. This formulation has the shape of search, which is intuitive but wrong for all three thinkers' technical concepts. It puts an agent inside the unconscious, doing the searching. Freud's later position is that the repetition compulsion is precisely the phenomenon that *cannot* be reduced to search-for-representation, because the cases that forced him to the death drive (the war dreams especially) iterate without converging. Lacan would say there is no homunculus searching; there is a signifying chain operating by its own laws, and the appearance of agency is downstream of the chain rather than upstream. Deleuze would reject the instrumental framing entirely — representation is a surface effect of repetition, not its goal.

The reformulation that survives all three: *conscious experience is what the narrative-framing machinery can absorb from the productive field. What it cannot absorb continues to be produced anyway, and its continued production has the shape of orbiting attractors — not because anything is seeking the attractor, but because the differential dynamics have basins.* No agent inside. The cybernetic feedback loop has no genie hunting around the setpoint; the orbit is a property of the loop's structure.

Two refinements worth keeping:

- **Contingent vs structural residue.** Some of what is unabsorbed could in principle be absorbed by a different framing (contingent — what working-through can shift). Some of it could not be absorbed by any framing because framing as such generates a remainder (structural — what no analytic effort can dissolve). For cyberneutics this distinction matters: contingent residue is what better elicitation or a better committee can surface; structural residue is what no committee can surface. Knowing the difference tells you when more deliberation will help and when it will only produce more elaborate orbits.
- **One process, not two.** The conscious/unconscious split itself is doing more work than it should. In the more Deleuzian version: there is one productive field. Some of its differences get taken up into narrative framings; others do not. The "orbit" is not happening in a different *place* from the narrative; it is happening in the same place, and what makes it an orbit rather than a narrative is just that the framing did not catch it.

### Rosen's modeling relation and the unconscious as natural object

The move that did the most work in the conversation: **treat the unconscious as a natural object, modelable under Rosen's modeling relation rather than directly formalizable**. The modeling relation has a natural system *N* and a formal system *F* connected by encoding and decoding maps, with the hope (not guarantee) that causal entailment in *N* commutes with inferential entailment in *F*. For complex *N* the commutation may be unachievable; Rosen's strongest claim is that living systems are characterized by having *more causal entailment than any single formal system can encode*.

Applying this to the unconscious is a reclassification from *formal object* to *natural object*. The consequences:

- It cuts against the strongest version of Lacan's claim. "The unconscious is structured like a language" reads as a claim that the unconscious *is* (or has the form of) a formal object — a signifying system. The Rosen move says no: the Saussurean structure lives in *F*, not in *N*. Lacan's models may be good models — even great models — but the model is not the thing, and the commutation between them is an empirical question Lacan did not really pose as such.
- It aligns with the biological-naturalistic Freud — the Freud of the *Project for a Scientific Psychology* (1895), who held throughout his career that psychoanalysis would eventually be grounded in brain biology and that its vocabulary was provisional. Lacan's "return to Freud" was selectively the *structural-linguistic* Freud against the biological-naturalistic one. The Rosen move re-empowers the Freud that Lacan demoted.
- It is compatible with Deleuze and Guattari, but they would resist the word "natural" because they read "nature" as already a representation. The productive field they describe precedes the nature/culture split. The Rosen move can absorb this: the productive field *is* the natural object, and both "nature" and "the signifying chain" are particular models of it.

The stronger reading worth holding: the unconscious is not just modelable-with-effort but *constitutively under-modeled*, like an organism — it exceeds any formal system one can build for it. Freud's models, Lacan's models, the cognitive-neuroscience models, the predictive-processing models are all partial encodings of a natural system that exceeds them. None is wrong, exactly. None is complete. **The gaps between them are not failures to be eliminated but evidence that the target is the kind of thing that has more causal entailment than inferential entailment can match.** This is sharper than what residuality says directly, and it generalizes the residuality posture (modeling under continuous negotiation, no single model closes) from technical systems to the unconscious.

### Dream logic as ungated generation

Dreams as the brain weaving narratives out of degraded input — attenuated sensations, surfacing memory fragments, traces of pruning and consolidation activity. The narrative faculty is online and does what it does; the gatekeeping faculties (reality-checking, cross-modal consistency, executive regulation) are offline or attenuated. The professionals have left the building; the janitors are running what's left.

This deflates Freud in a productive direction. Freud's dream-work has a teleology — condensation, displacement, representability, secondary revision are *operations of disguise* serving wish-fulfillment via censorship-evasion. The ungated-generation model has no such teleology. The condensations are not strategic disguises but the cheapest stitches when multiple weakly-activated traces have to fit into a single image. The displacements are not affect-management but what happens when affective tags get attached to whatever image is currently active because the binding machinery is loose. The mechanism is right; the explanation is wrong. The dream-work operations are *generic signatures of the narrative faculty running under those conditions* — and therefore not signatures of repression specifically. They are signatures of any local-composition generator without effective global gating. Dreams show them. LLMs show them.

The tarot refinement: the dream-images themselves are like tarot cards — they have content but the content is underdetermined with respect to meaning. The *recall* is where an agenda meets the image-content and produces an interpretation. The dream-as-recalled is not the dream-as-occurred. Hidden influences operate at the interpretive layer, not at the generation layer. The analyst doing hermeneutics on a reported dream is doing hermeneutics on a *reading* of a dream — not archaeology on the dream itself. The dream-content is the deck; the recollection is the reading.

### What is the unconscious, then?

The candidates the conversation surfaced: the lizard brain (primitive subcortical structures, unmodelable in Rosen's sense, natural-world-like); something in the brain that behaves like an LLM (local-composition narrative generator without global gating); and the unconscious as a *modeling construct* we posit to explain behavioral regularities that are not visible to introspection.

The answer the conversation converged on: **these are not the same thing, and the unconscious is the modeling construct, not the substrate.**

- The **lizard brain** does categorial-affective work — autonomic regulation, basic affect, threat detection, reward signaling, sleep-wake cycling. Not a language processor, not a narrative generator. A *categorial substrate* that imposes basic distinctions (safe/dangerous, attractive/aversive, familiar/novel) on the world before language operates. It modulates the *parameters* under which the higher faculties run rather than whispering interpretations from below.
- The **neocortical narrative faculty** does representational-compositional work — pattern completion, narrative construction, prediction. The LLM-like component. It operates on inputs from below plus memory traces plus current sensation. It is closer to a language processor and to a story-generator, and it has the LLM-like signatures.
- The **unconscious** is what we posit to explain behavioral regularities that introspection cannot see. It is a *role in our explanatory schemes*, filled differently by different theories — Freud's wishful repository, Lacan's signifying chain, the cognitive-science cognitive-unconscious of fast automatic processing, the predictive-processing prior structures, the lizard-brain affect-modulators, the neocortical narrative substrate.

Freud reached for the unified placeholder because he did not have the decomposition. He posited a single agency with content and motivation because that was the model available to him. The model was wrong about agency and unity but it was tracking real phenomena. Lacan tried to recover the unity by relocating it from biology to structure; he ran into the symmetric problem from the other direction. Deleuze and Guattari refuse the unification entirely — the productive unconscious is machinic, distributed, multiple. The position the conversation converged toward is closest to theirs: no single unconscious, multiple substrates, "the unconscious" as the term we use when we have not yet decomposed.

### Hidden agency vs hidden substrate

The cyberneutics payoff worth making explicit:

If the unconscious is a modeling construct rather than a thing, then cyberneutics is doing something specific: it is building *external scaffolding for substrates we lack good models of*. The LLM is such a substrate. The committee architecture is not modeling the LLM's unconscious. It is providing the gatekeeping and auditing that the LLM lacks internally — the same way the waking auditors provide gatekeeping the sleeping brain lacks.

The fan asks the LLM to operate as the night-shift narrative faculty does — producing locally coherent scenarios without enforced global consistency. The funnel is the day shift arriving to audit. This is not because the LLM has an unconscious that we are exposing. It is because **the LLM is all generator and no gate**, and cyberneutics adds the gate.

The distinction this draws is: **hidden agency vs hidden substrate**. Hidden agency is the Freudian frame — there is something inside concealing things with motivation. Hidden substrate is the deflationary frame — there are mechanisms not visible to introspection that do work, and they do not have agendas. The first leads to interpretive paranoia. The second leads to external scaffolding. Cyberneutics is committed to the second, and the ungated-generation model of dreams is committed to the same shape at a different level of organization.

The unconscious can stay as a useful term, but it no longer has to do the heavy lifting. The substrates can be named, the gates can be named, the gaps can be named, and the modeling can happen at the level where the actual mechanisms operate.

## Notes for further consideration

*For the future-reader self. The conversation produced several cross-tradition claims I have not yet fully metabolized. Each is paired with a test question; the strongest are flagged.*

- **Strong claim**: *Cyberneutics and clinical psychoanalysis are doing structurally the same thing, with structurally similar targets, under structurally similar epistemic constraints* — both are modeling-practices for natural systems that exceed formality, both proceed by repeated probing and accumulation of partial models, both let disagreement between models reveal the topology of the underlying thing. **Test**: Is there an analytic-practice analogue of the inspectable reasoning record? If so, what does the analyst do with it that cyberneutics doesn't, and vice versa? **Flag**: this is stronger than I would have made unprompted; it follows from the Rosen move but the strength of "structurally the same thing" wants more scrutiny before it gets repeated.
- **Strong claim**: *Freud's dream-work operations are generic signatures of any local-composition generator without global gating, and the repressed-content story is one specific instantiation rather than the essence.* **Test**: Do brainstorming sessions, free association in analysis, and fan-operation outputs all show condensation-displacement-representability-secondary-revision signatures in identifiable ways? If yes, the genericity claim is supported. If only dreams show them, the claim overreaches. **Flag**: this is a sharp claim about Freud and it would land badly with most psychoanalysts; worth holding tentatively until at least one of the generic instances is checked.
- **Hypothesis**: *LLMs occupy the structural position of the Lacanian subject-supposed-to-know by accident of architecture*, and the transferential dynamics this produces are part of why LLM interaction is psychologically tricky in ways the tools-literature does not capture. **Test**: Is there independent evidence in the human-AI interaction literature of transferential phenomena that the standard human-factors frameworks miss? Look in the parts of the AI-companionship and ELIZA-effect literatures that are not just about anthropomorphization. **Flag**: this is more provocative than substantiated.
- **Modest claim**: *The cyberneutics fan is structurally dream-work used productively — narrative-faculty-without-gating run deliberately, with the curated input that makes the divergences informative; the funnel is the morning meeting where you decide what to do about what the night shift produced.* **Test**: Does this framing add anything to the existing fan-funnel exposition that the current Deleuzian framing (production of differences from a virtual field) does not? If yes, where would it go without bloating the essays? Possibly a sidebar in essay 06 or a short paragraph in the fan operation's documentation. If no, it is a private intuition that helped the conversation move but does not need to land in the repo text.
- **Open question**: Whether the *automaton / tuché* distinction has any cyberneutics-internal use — e.g. as a diagnostic for committees that are circling without producing new difference. The Lacanian register names something the cyberneutics register currently has only impoverished vocabulary for: a committee that runs its symbolic chain (automaton) without producing the kind of fresh actualization (tuché) that would shift the deliberation. **Test**: in past deliberation transcripts, are there cases where the committee converged not by working through but by exhausting variation within a fixed framing? If so, what would the diagnostic look like, and what intervention would it suggest?
