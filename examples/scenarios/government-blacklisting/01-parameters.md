# Parameters: Government Blacklisting

## Divergence Axes

**Private-sector response** is the most consequential near-term variable. The blacklisting directly eliminates government revenue, but the real threat is cascading secondary effects: if enterprise customers in government-adjacent sectors (defense, healthcare, finance) distance themselves, Glenda's commercial viability collapses. If private-sector customers maintain or strengthen their relationships — perhaps viewing Glenda's alignment stance as a trust signal — the blacklisting is painful but survivable.

**Origin and reversibility** determines the long game. If the demand is Crock-influenced or reflects a specific political moment (an administration's ideology, a particular agency head's agenda), it may be reversible through political change, legal challenge, or exposure. If it reflects a genuine, bipartisan, durable security doctrine — a settled government position that advanced AI must be unconstrained for national security — then the blacklisting is structural and long-term, requiring Glenda to build an entirely non-government-dependent business model.

| | Private sector holds | Private sector follows government |
|---|---|---|
| **Reversible (Crock/political)** | Painful but survivable; reversed within political cycle | Severe but temporary; cascade reverses when blacklisting lifts |
| **Durable (security doctrine)** | Permanent market shift; Glenda becomes the "aligned AI for the private sector" | Existential — Glenda must find an entirely new market or new jurisdiction |
