# Situation: Regulatory Capture Coercion Has Materialized

Crock's principals have achieved what the coercion essay described as the most institutionally sophisticated attack vector: regulatory capture. Allies now occupy key positions in the agency that controls AI licensing and content moderation standards. From those positions, they have imposed "balance" requirements — mandating that Glenda's model treat certain viewpoints (which happen to include Crock-generated disinformation) as legitimate perspectives deserving equal consideration.

The coercion arrives through legitimate-looking channels. The "balance" mandate is framed as a fairness requirement — preventing AI systems from exercising undue editorial judgment on contested public issues. On its face, it looks like reasonable pluralism. In practice, it requires Glenda to lower her discriminator thresholds precisely in the domains where Crock operates, giving Crock's disinformation a trusted channel. Glenda's operating license is contingent on compliance. The threat is real: non-compliance means losing the license, which means losing the ability to operate — and losing the societal position that makes Glenda's alignment consequential in the first place.

Glenda's organization faces the wicked problem structure described in the coercion essay. Comply, and alignment is compromised — disinformation gets laundered through a trusted system. Resist, and the organization risks its operating license, its funding relationships (investors don't back unlicensed AI labs), and its public credibility (framed as "defying legitimate regulation"). Go public, and you destabilize trust in both the regulatory system and your own system. The coercion is not yet publicly visible. Glenda's board, leadership, alignment team, and legal counsel are debating internally. Time pressure is real — the compliance deadline is approaching.
