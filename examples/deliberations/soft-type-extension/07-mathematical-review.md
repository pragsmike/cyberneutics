## Tier 1: Definitional claims (checkable by inspection)

### Claim 1: Coend simplification — ✓ CORRECT

**Claim (§6, lines 590–597):** ∫^C F_a(C) ⊗ Hom_T(C, C) = ∫^C F_a(C) because Hom_T(C,C) is the monoidal unit.

**Verification:** In a V_5-enriched category, the enrichment axioms (Kelly 1.2, eq. 1.2) require the identity morphism j_C : I → Hom(C, C) to pick out the unit of V_5, which is (3,3,3,3,3). Therefore Hom_T(C, C) = (3,3,3,3,3) and F_a(C) ⊗ (3,3,3,3,3) = F_a(C) since (3,3,3,3,3) is the monoidal unit of V_5. The simplification is valid.

### Claim 3: V_5 cocompleteness — ✓ CORRECT

**Claim (§6, lines 650–655):** For the product quantale V_5, coequalisers exist because V_5 is cocomplete as a lattice.

**Verification:** V_5 = W^5 where W = [0,3]. W is a complete lattice (bounded real interval). A finite product of complete lattices is a complete lattice with componentwise operations. A complete lattice, viewed as a thin category, is cocomplete (colimits are joins). Coequalisers in a complete lattice exist as joins of equivalence classes. The coend, as a specific coequaliser (Kelly 3.10), is therefore well-defined in V_5.

### Claim 7: Restricted sheaf settings — ✓ CORRECT with caveat

**Claim (§7, lines 733–752):** Three restricted settings where the sheaf condition holds: linear chains, shared criteria, enforced by design.

**Verification:**
- *Linear chains:* If the cover is linearly ordered, there are no independent overlaps. The sheaf condition is vacuously satisfied. **Correct.**
- *Shared criteria:* If two rubrics share a criterion, the shared component must agree on any artifact. This gives a partial sheaf condition on the sub-quantale projection. **Correct** as stated, though "partial sheaf condition" is informal — it means the condition holds for the projection onto shared components, not for full V_5.
- *Enforced by design:* If parent type grades are defined as max of child grades per criterion, gluing holds by construction. **Correct** — this is a design constraint, not a mathematical theorem.

**Caveat:** The "shared criteria" case should state explicitly that this is a condition on a quotient/projection of V_5, not on V_5 itself. A reader might misunderstand it as claiming the full sheaf condition holds.

## Tier 2: Bounded constructions (require confirming cited results apply)

### Claim 2: Coend as colimit — ⚠️ CORRECT BUT VACUOUS

**Claim (§6, lines 593–597):** The coend of a V_5-valued presheaf over a preorder T is the colimit of F_a over T — "the total inhabitation grade of the artifact across all types, with the refinement structure quotiented out."

**Verification of correctness:** In V_5 (a complete lattice), colimits are joins. The coend ∫^C F(C) over a preorder T, computed via the coequaliser formula, reduces to the join ∨_C F(C) — the componentwise supremum of all F(C) values. This is indeed the colimit of F over T in V_5. **Mathematically correct.**

**Operational problem:** For a presheaf F : T^op → V_5 satisfying functoriality (F(A) ≥ F(B) when B refines A), the values *decrease* along refinement. The supremum ∨_C F(C) is therefore achieved at the *least refined* (most general) type — typically the top of the type lattice. The coend just returns F at the most general type.

This means the construction is **operationally vacuous** for decreasing presheaves on a bounded preorder. The document's description "total inhabitation grade across all types, with refinement structure quotiented out" is technically correct but misleading — the "quotienting" is trivial because the functoriality constraint already forces the sup to be at the top. The interesting coend would require a different bifunctor, one where the covariant and contravariant actions produce non-trivial interaction.

**Recommendation:** Acknowledge that for decreasing presheaves on bounded preorders, the coend is trivial. Either (a) identify a more interesting bifunctor where the coend is non-trivial, or (b) reframe the coend's role: its value is not in computing a single aggregate grade but in the *universal property* it satisfies (any other construction that is dinatural in the type variable factors through the coend).

### Claim 5: Canonical topology characterisation — ⚠️ MIXES LEVELS

**Claim (§7, lines 679–683):** The canonical topology on T is "generated by jointly surjective refinement families: a type A is covered by types {B₁, ..., Bₙ} if every artifact that inhabits A also inhabits some Bᵢ."

**Issue:** This mixes the categorical definition with the semantic interpretation. The categorical definition of the canonical topology on a preorder is: {B₁, ..., Bₙ} covers A iff the sieve generated by the Bᵢ's is a covering sieve — which in a preorder means A is the join of the Bᵢ's (or equivalently, the downward closure of the Bᵢ's contains A's downward closure). The document instead gives a semantic criterion ("every artifact that inhabits A also inhabits some Bᵢ"), which is an inhabitation-based characterisation.

These coincide if the type lattice faithfully mirrors inhabitation containment (i.e., the ordering on T is exactly the inhabitation-containment order). This is a reasonable design assumption — the refinement order is defined in terms of inhabitation in §2 — but the document should state it explicitly as the bridge between the categorical and semantic descriptions.

**Recommendation:** Add a sentence noting that the semantic characterisation follows from the design of the refinement order (§2: "every artifact that inhabits B also inhabits A" when B refines A), making the categorical and semantic descriptions equivalent by construction.

### §5 Spot-check: Collapse functor — ✗ ERROR (lax vs. oplax)

**Claim (§5, lines 442–448):** The collapse functor is "a lax monoidal functor from (V_5, componentwise-min) to (V, min)." The document states "the collapse of a componentwise min may exceed the min of the collapses."

**Finding:** Both the direction label and the description are backwards.

*Proof that collapse is oplax, not lax:* For any a, b ∈ V_5, we have cmin(a,b)ᵢ = min(aᵢ, bᵢ) ≤ aᵢ for all i, so sum(cmin(a,b)) ≤ sum(a). Since collapse is monotone in the sum, collapse(cmin(a,b)) ≤ collapse(a). Similarly collapse(cmin(a,b)) ≤ collapse(b). Therefore:

    collapse(a ⊗ b) ≤ min(collapse(a), collapse(b)) = collapse(a) ⊗ collapse(b)

This is the **oplax** direction: F(a ⊗ b) → F(a) ⊗ F(b). The lax direction would require min(collapse(a), collapse(b)) ≤ collapse(cmin(a,b)), which the document's own example disproves.

The statement "the collapse of a componentwise min may exceed the min of the collapses" is **false**. The correct statement is: "the collapse of a componentwise min may be *strictly below* the min of the collapses." The document has the inequality backwards.

*Concrete counterexample to laxness:* a = (2,2,2,2,0), sum 8, Medium. b = (0,2,2,2,2), sum 8, Medium. cmin(a,b) = (0,2,2,2,0), sum 6, Low. collapse(cmin) = Low < Medium = min(collapse(a), collapse(b)).

**This is a mathematical error in §5 (the canonical version), not in §§6–7.** Maya's spot-check flag during remediation was prescient.

**Recommendation:** Change "lax monoidal" to "oplax monoidal" throughout §5. Reverse the direction of the inequality in the discussion. Change "the collapse of a componentwise min may exceed the min of the collapses" to "the collapse of a componentwise min may fall strictly below the min of the collapses." The downstream discussion of laxness "as a genuine feature" is still valid — the point that boundary decisions are coarser than internal tracking is correct, it's just the direction that's wrong.

## Tier 3: Conceptual bridges (hardest to verify, highest uncertainty)

### Claim 4: Coend as expected score — ✗ EQUIVOCATION

**Claim (§6, lines 606–617):** The coend has a probabilistic interpretation: ∫^C H(C, C) ≈ ∫_T μ_a(dC) · F_a(C), i.e., the expected score profile under the type distribution.

**Finding:** This equates two fundamentally different operations:

1. The **categorical coend** ∫^C F(C) in V_5 is the **join** (componentwise supremum) of all F(C) values — a max operation.
2. The **probabilistic expectation** ∫ μ(dC) F(C) is a **weighted average** — a sum operation.

These are different except in degenerate cases (delta measures). The document presents them as the same construction in different languages ("the coend *becomes* the expected score in the distributional setting"), but they are not. The coend marginalises by taking a supremum; the expectation marginalises by taking a weighted average.

The document silently switches monoidal structures: V_5 = (W^5, min) for the coend, but (ℝ^5, ·, +) for the integral. The "≈" on line 613 bridges an unbridgeable gap between two different algebraic structures.

**This is the most significant error in §§6–7.** The bridge between enriched category theory and probability theory that the coend section promises does not exist in the form presented.

**Possible repairs:**
1. *Reframe as analogy:* State explicitly that the coend and the expectation are two different formalisations of the intuition "marginalise over the latent type variable," and that they give different answers in general (sup vs. mean). Discuss when and why one is preferable.
2. *Change the enrichment base:* If V is equipped with addition (a semiring rather than a quantale), the coend could become a weighted sum. But this would require reworking the enrichment throughout.
3. *Use the Yoneda lemma:* The correct categorical statement may be that F_a is *reconstructible* from its coend with representables via the enriched Yoneda lemma (Kelly 2.4), not that the coend *is* the expected value. This would preserve the universal-property interpretation without the equivocation.

### Claim 6: Sheaf failure → incoherent equilibria — ⚠️ REASONABLE CONJECTURE, OVER-PRESENTED

**Claim (§7, lines 756–777):** If the sheaf condition fails, the continuation function k in the open games framework is "ill-defined at the overlaps," causing incoherent committee equilibria.

**Finding:** The directional intuition is sound — inconsistent rubric signals create ambiguous incentive structures. But:

1. k is not "ill-defined" — it is *multi-valued* (depends on which rubric perspective is applied). Multi-valued payoffs are handled in game theory via multi-objective optimisation or ambiguity-averse preferences.
2. The leap from "sheaf failure" to "locally rational but globally incoherent equilibria" is asserted, not derived. A derivation would need to show that the composed open game with inconsistent payoffs fails to have a Nash equilibrium, or that its equilibria are Pareto-dominated. The document doesn't attempt this.
3. The paragraph's conclusion — "whether this theoretical risk materialises in practice depends on rubric overlap" — is honest, but it undersells the gap between the conjecture and the conclusion.

**Recommendation:** Relabel as a conjecture (parallel to the sheaf condition failure conjecture earlier in the section). Change "the continuation function k is ill-defined at the overlaps" to "the continuation function k becomes ambiguous at the overlaps" or "faces a multi-objective optimisation problem."
