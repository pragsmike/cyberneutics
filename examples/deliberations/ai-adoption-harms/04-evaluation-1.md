## Independent Review

### Charter

The committee was charged with determining the appropriate framework for AI engagement by informed practitioners, given documented harms from unstructured use and claimed benefits from structured use. The charge specified that the precautionary position (Reva) must survive at full strength, that the structured-use position must earn its conclusions through evidence and mechanism, and that unresolved tensions must be documented honestly.

### Rubric Scores

**1. Reasoning Completeness: 3**

Reasoning chains are explicit and traceable throughout. Reva's opening builds from specific evidence (Pierre case report, Buck & Malte cross-sectional study, *Science* sycophancy study, Olsen Danish data) to a specific demand (controlled evidence of safety). Vic's Round 1 challenge to Reva traces from her implicit prescription (restriction) to its ungrounded policy assumption (feasibility). Frankie's Round 4 framework proposal is the strongest example: it explicitly concedes Reva's premises, identifies what non-engagement costs, and derives conditions from the concessions. The one area where reasoning is slightly compressed is Joe's nuclear/asbestos pattern-matching — he identifies the structural question (which failure pattern are we in?) but does not fully specify what evidence would distinguish them.

**2. Adversarial Rigor: 2**

The debate is genuine — characters challenge each other directly, and several arguments evolve through the exchange. Vic's cross-examination of Reva on evidence asymmetry is the strongest adversarial moment. Reva's recycling analogy against Frankie is sharp and unscripted. However, there are moments of pulled punches. Reva's response to Maya in Round 1 ("I do not need to read the methodology") is the self-sealing pattern in action, and while the Round 1 Analysis notes this, no character confronts Reva directly with "you are doing the thing we said you do." This is the most significant missed adversarial opportunity. Additionally, Maya disappears in Rounds 3 and 4 — her paranoid realism about institutional incentives is exactly what the failure-mode discussion needs, and her absence weakens the rigor of that section. Frankie and Joe do not challenge each other despite being a documented productive tension (caution vs. commitment).

What would raise this to 3: Vic directly naming the self-sealing pattern to Reva in debate and forcing a response. Maya contributing to the failure-mode discussion from her political-realism propensity. At least one Frankie-Joe direct exchange.

**3. Assumption Surfacing: 3**

The transcript explicitly surfaces and names its key assumptions. The "Assumptions Surfaced" section in the synthesis is strong — technology permanence, practitioner-population distinction, regulatory utility of records, calibration generalization, and human engagement are all named with attribution. More importantly, assumptions are surfaced *during* debate, not just in the synthesis: Tammy's Round 2 time-horizon framing makes explicit that Reva and Frankie are optimizing for different timeframes, and Vic's Round 3 counterfactual challenge forces Reva to make her implicit assumption (that human reasoning is the reliable baseline) explicit and then challenges it. The meta-assumption that the committee is debating "whether to use" when the real question is "what to do given that AI exists" is surfaced by Vic in Round 1 and shapes the rest of the deliberation.

**4. Evidence Standards: 2**

Evidence standards are generally strong but unevenly enforced. Reva's evidence citations are specific and verifiable (named studies, specific statistics, clinical categories). Vic presses for evidence on both sides, which is the correct posture. However, several claims go unchallenged. Frankie's Therabot citation ("51% depression reduction with large effect sizes") is stated without methodological scrutiny — sample size, population, follow-up period, and comparison condition are not interrogated. The seatbelt analogy in Round 2 is challenged on structural grounds (Reva's response is excellent), but the defensive driving counter-analogy is accepted without data. Joe's nuclear pattern-matching is accepted as instructive without anyone demanding specifics on whether the nuclear outcome (40-year moratorium causing greater climate harm) is empirically established versus being a retrospective narrative.

What would raise this to 3: Vic challenging Frankie's Therabot citation with the same rigor applied to Reva's claims. Someone pressing Joe for evidence that the nuclear moratorium actually caused quantifiable climate harm, or that the counterfactual (more nuclear) would have been better. Treating all sides' evidence with identical rigor.

**5. Trade-off Explicitness: 3**

Trade-offs are named with specificity and consequences. The Decision Space Map explicitly identifies four options with the optimization criteria that favor each. The denominator problem is conceded rather than papered over — the resolution explicitly states the framework does not address the at-risk population and should not claim to. Reva's dissent in the resolution is documented as a substantive temporal-ordering disagreement, not a defeated minority position. The conditions for provisional adoption each address a specific failure mode, and the unresolved concerns are listed with specificity. The time-horizon trade-off (immediate harm reduction vs. long-term infrastructure) is named by Tammy and carried through to the synthesis.

### Aggregate Score: 13/15, average 2.6

### Structural Assessment

**Charter fitness**: The deliberation addresses the stated problem directly. The charge specified that Reva must be engaged at full strength, that the structured-use position must earn its conclusions, and that unresolved tensions must be documented. All three criteria are met, though the adversarial rigor could be higher as noted above.

**Character calibration**: Reva is the strongest characterization — she is formidable, evidence-based, and her response pattern (selective non-engagement with the methodology) is present without being caricatured. Vic is well-calibrated: demanding evidence on all sides, not just one. Tammy's systems framing adds genuine analytical value (time-horizon decomposition, feedback loop mapping, hermeneutic circle identification). Frankie is slightly underweight — the values argument is present but could be more muscular. Maya is well-calibrated in Rounds 1-2 (corporate incentive analysis, "who benefits" question) but disappears in Rounds 3-4. Joe is serviceable but operates more as historical-analogy provider than as the continuity guardian pressing "what makes this different from last time."

**Engagement depth**: The debate evolves meaningfully. Opening positions are not repeated — they are refined, challenged, and modified. Frankie's Round 4 proposal is responsive to Rounds 1-3, not a restatement of his opening. Reva's conditional-no vote is a genuine update from her opening position (which was closer to "no use until controlled study") — she now specifies that implementing the conditions and generating 12 months of data would cause her to reassess. This is character development through adversarial process.

**Synthesis quality**: The synthesis honestly represents the tensions rather than smoothing them over. The resolution's "What This Resolution Does Not Claim" section is the strongest evidence of synthesis quality — it draws explicit boundaries around the decision rather than overclaiming. Reva's dissent is documented as substantive, not defeated.

### Biggest Gaps

1. **Self-sealing pattern confrontation**: The most distinctive feature of Reva's character — selective suspension of scientific reasoning on this topic — is identified in the Round 1 Analysis but never directly confronted in debate. Vic or Maya should name it to her face and force a response. This is the deliberation's largest missed opportunity.

2. **Propagation mechanism**: Maya's challenge ("how does practitioner builds good habits translate into regulation protects vulnerable users?") is the strongest unanswered question. Frankie's "prerequisite for regulation" response is a claim, not a mechanism. No character provides a concrete example of a structured-use artifact that a future regulator could actually use.

3. **Maya's Round 3-4 absence**: Maya disappears during the failure-mode discussion and the conditions proposal. Her paranoid realism about institutional incentives is exactly what those rounds need — who will monitor the off-ramps? Who decides when the calibration data is "good enough"? What prevents the evidence-generation framing from becoming laundering?

### What Would Most Improve This Deliberation

1. **Direct confrontation with Reva's pattern**: In Round 1, when Reva says "I do not need to read the methodology," Vic or Maya should respond: "You are doing the thing we identified in the diary entry — activating a prepared position rather than an analytical response. Your scientific training would not permit you to dismiss a methodology in any other field without reading it. What is different about this field?" Force Reva to respond to the pattern, not just the substance.

2. **Concrete regulatory artifact**: Frankie claims the framework produces "raw material for regulation." Specify one. What does a regulator do with an inspectable reasoning record? Is there a historical example of a voluntary practitioner methodology being adopted as a regulatory standard? Without this, the "prerequisite for regulation" argument is aspirational.

3. **Maya in Round 3**: Have Maya ask: "Who monitors the monitors? If the off-ramp metrics are defined by the same practitioners who want to continue using AI, who ensures the metrics are honest? Is there a structural mechanism for independent oversight, or is this self-regulation?" This is her natural contribution and its absence is conspicuous.

4. **Pharmaceutical analogy resolution**: Reva's pharmaceutical analogy in Round 1 is her strongest move. Joe's food-safety counter is good but not decisive. The committee should more explicitly address *why* the pharmaceutical model (regulated intervention requiring pre-market evidence) does not apply to a voluntary behavioral practice — and the honest answer may be "it partially does, and that is uncomfortable."

### Verdict

**Trustworthiness as decision input**: High

The deliberation produces a genuine map of the decision space with named trade-offs, characterized failure modes, and explicit conditions. The precautionary position is engaged at strength and its strongest arguments are either addressed or honestly documented as unresolved. The resolution's conditional structure and explicit limitations prevent overclaiming. The main weaknesses (pulled punches on the self-sealing pattern, Maya's absence in later rounds, uneven evidence scrutiny) are significant but do not undermine the core analysis. A practitioner reading this deliberation would understand what they are choosing, what they are risking, and what evidence would change the calculus.
